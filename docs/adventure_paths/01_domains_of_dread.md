# Adventure Path 1: Domains of Dread

> **System-facing campaign procedure:** the players are never told that this Adventure Path is active. They encounter only the people, places, monsters, rumours, bargains, and consequences produced by it.

In **Domains of Dread**, the party enters a demiplane containing a powerful evil that is also trapped there. The evil may rule openly, govern through institutions, haunt the land, hide behind a mortal identity, or remain only dimly aware that the domain is its prison. Defeating that evil under the correct conditions opens a way out.

The campaign covers **levels 1–10**, three acts, and three distinct environments. Its common movement is:

> **Survive the domain → reconstruct the ruler's Fall → acquire leverage → confront the ruler → escape, die, submit, or become claimed.**

The procedure supports combat, exorcism, restitution, persuasion, seduction, deception, bargaining, breaking a curse, turning the ruler's servants, or replacing the ruler. The available endings depend upon the generated boss dossier and what the party discovers.

---

## The Hidden-Path Principle

“Adventure Path 1,” “Domains of Dread,” act numbers, boss categories, clue counts, and hidden progress values are system terms. Do not present them as player-facing announcements.

The campaign should begin like an ordinary journey:

> You reach the small town of Zarovich near dusk. Its inn is poor, its only shop is poorer, and a caravan outside the walls belongs to an old woman who reads painted cards for a copper piece.

Nothing tells the players that they have entered a campaign path. They may buy supplies, question the innkeeper, ignore the card reader, investigate a disappearance, or leave town. The domain reveals itself through play: the road returns to Zarovich, a local custom proves compulsory, a victim disappears, or a minion acts upon an agenda.

The hidden path changes the content supplied by ordinary ASH procedures. It does not replace those procedures with narrated plot.

### What the Players See

- Settlements, roads, weather, ruins, and encounter results
- NPC behaviour and monster activity
- Rumours, journals, visions, card readings, and physical evidence
- Allies, enemies, curses, bargains, and usable objects
- Changes to places and factions caused by their decisions
- Their own voluntary patron bonds and Corruption

### What the System Keeps Hidden

- `adventure_path_id = domains_of_dread`
- The selected boss dossier and true identity
- Current act and scheduled content tier
- Unrevealed history, protections, and possible resolutions
- Clue-source counters and insertion quotas
- Domain Pressure and the next threshold consequence
- Unmet conditions required to open the way out

The Table Companion can store this state directly. For analog play, use a shuffled boss deck and sealed packets labelled **First Signs**, **The Fall**, and **The Heart**. No player reads ahead.

## Creating the Domain

When the hidden campaign selector chooses this path, complete the following steps without displaying their labels to the players.

### 1. Select a Boss Dossier

Choose or randomly select one dossier from the [Boss Library](#boss-library). A dossier contains the complete truth of the domain and all content needed to reveal it gradually.

### 2. Generate Three Environments

Every dossier supplies three environment palettes:

1. **The Threshold:** Where ordinary people suffer the domain and the party first encounters its minions.
2. **The Dominion:** Where the ruler's history, institutions, relationships, and principal lieutenants can be discovered.
3. **The Heart:** The ruler's lair, prison mechanism, curse source, or final nightmare.

These are functions rather than mandatory terrain types. A ghost captain might use a storm coast, an island port, and a spectral flagship. A mummy might use a desert caravan route, a necropolis, and a pyramid underworld.

Give each environment:

- One settlement or Haven
- Six explorable sites
- One travel or access problem
- Two boss-linked minions appropriate to its tier
- One history or protection site
- One independent faction not controlled by the boss

At least half of all generated content should remain ordinary regional content. The path is a pressure within the world, not every object in it.

### 3. Establish the Ruler's Fall

Every boss dossier answers six questions:

1. **The Mortal:** Who or what was the ruler before the domain?
2. **The Desire:** What did it want above all else?
3. **The Crime:** What did it choose to do in pursuit of that desire?
4. **The Victim:** Who suffered most directly?
5. **The Curse:** How does the prison repeat, frustrate, or preserve the crime?
6. **The Need:** What does the ruler still desire, fear, regret, or deny?

These truths determine the domain's imagery, lieutenants, clues, weaknesses, and non-combat possibilities. They are not optional flavour text.

### 4. Establish the Opposition

Create or select:

- **Six minion types:** two for each act
- **The Hand:** A physical enforcer introduced during Act I
- **The Voice:** A social, religious, or institutional lieutenant introduced during Act II
- **The Jailer:** An elite guardian of a protection, tool, prisoner, or final route introduced late in Act II

Lieutenants are recurring characters rather than disposable encounters. Each receives:

- A personal want unrelated to serving the ruler
- A reason for loyalty or bondage
- A method of retreat or survival
- One fact about the Fall
- One condition under which they might defect, bargain, or surrender

Killing a lieutenant is valid, but it is never the only supported solution.

### 5. Establish Protections and Leverage

Create three **Protections** that prevent an immediate level-1 solution. Examples include a phylactery, legal authority, a repeated haunting, shared bodies, a coven bond, control of dreams, a resurrection engine, an immortal army, or immunity outside a particular circumstance.

Create three forms of **Leverage**:

- **Tool:** A weapon, rite, antidote, name, key, counter-spell, or material capable of bypassing a Protection.
- **Ally:** A victim, heir, creation, former lover, rebellious servant, rival, priest, or alternate identity whose agency changes the confrontation.
- **Secret or Circumstance:** A truth, location, anniversary, oath, emotion, or domain law that makes another resolution possible.

The party need not obtain all three, but different finale routes require different combinations.

### 6. Establish Domain Pressure

Each dossier defines a six-step process by which the ruler's influence worsens without waiting for the party. Write two to four objective fictional triggers. Examples include a minion completing an abduction, a lieutenant capturing a Haven, a plague victim entering a settlement, or an institution accepting the ruler's protection.

Do not advance Domain Pressure merely because a session ended, the party rested, or a roll failed. Display consequences in the fiction; never show “Domain Pressure 4” to the players.

## Three Acts Across Ten Levels

Character level controls what content the hidden engine may introduce. It does not announce an act transition. The route into a new environment appears naturally through a map, prisoner, invitation, dream, ship, road, ritual, or discovered entrance.

| Act | Levels | Hidden Purpose | What the Players Experience |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **I — Endure** | 1–3 | Establish the prison, minions, Hand, and boss nature | Local problems become connected; departure proves impossible |
| **II — Unearth and Prepare** | 4–7 | Reveal the Fall, Voice, Jailer, Protections, and possible leverage | History changes the meaning of earlier events; tools and allies become reachable |
| **III — Break the Domain** | 8–10 | Acquire leverage, neutralize Protections, resolve the ruler | The party enters the Heart and chooses how to confront the boss |

An act opens only when both conditions are true:

- The party has reached the minimum level.
- The required discoveries or achievements have occurred.

If the party reaches level 4 without confirming the boss's nature, remain in the Threshold and prioritize missing nature clues. If it reaches level 8 without reconstructing the Fall or locating leverage, remain in the Dominion until those leads have been pursued.

### Level-by-Level Distribution

| Level | Required Path Inserts | Opposition | Discoveries and Unlocks |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | One omen; one minion incident | Weak or ambiguous minions | Establish a Haven and the domain's first unnatural rule |
| **2** | Two minion schemes; one clue source | The Hand acts indirectly or appears briefly | Establish that local grief is coordinated; first evidence of the boss's nature |
| **3** | Two nature clues; one boundary event | The Hand confronts, bargains, or retreats | Confirm the boss family and that the party cannot simply leave |
| **4** | Mortal and Desire clues | The Voice enters through a local institution | Open the Dominion environment; identify the ruler's public identity or former self |
| **5** | Crime and Victim clues | Minions suppress witnesses and records | Identify the defining wrongdoing and its principal victim |
| **6** | Curse and Need clues | Hand and Voice pursue conflicting agendas | Reconstruct the Fall; reveal why ordinary victory or escape fails |
| **7** | Three leverage leads; one Protection clue | The Jailer appears; a lieutenant may be turned | Locate a Tool, Ally, and Secret or Circumstance; a patron offer may appear |
| **8** | Two leverage opportunities; elite minion site | Elite servants guard the ruler's apparatus | Acquire sufficient leverage and expose all three Protections |
| **9** | Protection sites and final-route clues | Resolve remaining lieutenants | Neutralize the Protections required by the chosen finale route; open the Heart |
| **10** | The Heart and final confrontation | Boss plus surviving assets | Slay, release, persuade, seduce, deceive, bargain with, replace, or lose to the ruler |

These are content guarantees rather than predetermined scenes. A Crime clue might be discovered in a trial record, on a corpse, in a lieutenant's confession, or carved into the foundation of a ruin.

## Quietly Inserting the Path

Whenever the party enters a new site, meets a new significant NPC, hears a rumour, or triggers an unexpected event, roll **1d6 Path Presence** behind the player-facing result.

| d6 | Hidden Insertion |
| :---: | :--- |
| **1–2** | No path insertion; use ordinary regional content |
| **3** | Add an omen, repeated symbol, dream, taboo, or domain law |
| **4** | Connect the result to an eligible minion scheme |
| **5** | Embed the next eligible clue in the result |
| **6** | Move an eligible lieutenant, Protection, leverage lead, or Domain Pressure |

Apply three safeguards:

1. **Maximum:** Add no more than one substantial path insertion to a single generated scene.
2. **Minimum:** Before the party advances a level, ensure the level's Required Path Inserts have appeared. If not, the next eligible result supplies the missing insert without rolling.
3. **Relevance:** Use only content unlocked for the current act. Do not place a final phylactery clue in the first inn unless the dossier explicitly uses early foreshadowing.

The resulting scene is presented normally. Do not say “you found a boss clue.” Say that the innkeeper's grandfather appears in a fifty-year-old portrait without having aged, that every grave has been opened from within, or that the card reader turns over a crowned corpse beneath three moons.

### The Recurring Seer

A card reader, blind hermit, condemned prophet, talking portrait, dream-child, or similar figure may recur at environment transitions. The Seer is not a quest menu. A reading provides symbolic versions of five dossier elements:

- **The Beast:** The boss's nature
- **The Broken Crown:** The Fall
- **The Chain:** A Protection
- **The Hand:** A Tool or Ally
- **The Door:** The final circumstance or location

A reading counts as one clue source, never as complete proof. Ignoring or distrusting the Seer does not block the campaign because every truth has other sources.

## Clues Without a Dungeon Master

### The Three-Source Rule

For every conclusion the party must reach, the boss dossier supplies three independent clue expressions:

1. **Witness:** A survivor, spirit, minion, lieutenant, victim, or inherited memory
2. **Record:** A journal, legal document, portrait, song, grave, experiment, map, or card reading
3. **Place or Object:** A ruin, body, recurring event, altered environment, tool, or Protection

Two independent sources **Confirm** a truth. The third prevents a failed expedition, destroyed record, or distrusted witness from stalling the campaign.

Required conclusions are:

- The boss's creature family or power
- Each of the six parts of the Fall
- Each of the three Protections
- Each viable finale route

The system records which source types have appeared. The players record only what they actually learned in the campaign chronicle.

### Testing a Theory

Players may state a theory at any time. The Table Companion compares it with the hidden dossier, or an analog group opens the relevant sealed confirmation only after possessing two marked sources.

- **Correct:** Confirm it through a concrete event, recovered fact, or changed NPC behaviour.
- **Partly correct:** Confirm the accurate portion and reveal a contradiction pointing toward what remains missing.
- **Incorrect:** Introduce a contradiction; do not punish the party or advance Domain Pressure.

Guessing is allowed. Incorrect theories cost only the actions the party chooses to spend pursuing them.

### Clues Cannot Be Destroyed

An individual clue source can be lost, but its truth cannot become inaccessible.

- A burned journal leaves a witness or a copy in an archive.
- A killed lieutenant carries evidence or leaves a spirit, servant, or victim behind.
- A captured witness moves to an identified enemy site.
- A missed site transfers its clue to a related future result.
- A deceptive clue must contradict an established fact and is labelled as testimony, never as system truth.

## Minions and Lieutenants

### Minion Tiers

Each boss dossier supplies two minion types for every act.

| Tier | Levels | Function |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Signs** | 1–3 | Cause local grief, express one aspect of the boss, and remain plausibly unrelated |
| **Keepers** | 4–7 | Suppress history, control institutions, guard witnesses, and operate the domain's machinery |
| **Wardens** | 8–10 | Guard Protections, leverage, the Heart, and the boss's final vulnerabilities |

Minions need an activity beyond “attack the party.” Give each active group a written scheme such as collecting bodies, replacing officials, destroying family records, poisoning wells, capturing dreamers, enforcing a tithe, or finding a missing relic.

### Lieutenant Recurrence

When a lieutenant would die or be removed, follow the fiction:

- If killed under valid conditions, it remains dead.
- If defeated without addressing its supernatural survival method, it returns changed.
- If captured, use reaction and morale procedures to determine whether it bargains.
- If persuaded, seduced, redeemed, or freed from control, record its new allegiance and what it can provide.
- If it escapes, move it to a known eligible site and advance its scheme only when a written trigger occurred.

Do not invent a replacement lieutenant solely to preserve encounter difficulty. The boss must adapt using remaining people and resources.

### Resolving a Lieutenant

Every lieutenant must support at least three approaches:

- Defeat or kill it
- Break the coercion, curse, debt, or belief that binds it
- Offer something it wants more than continued service

Its clue remains obtainable through every supported resolution.

## Curses Are Not Corruption

A curse may be imposed by a monster, trap, relic, bloodline, failed defence, or rule of the domain. It can alter the body, dreams, luck, relationships, or available actions.

A curse is an affliction. It does not mean that the character chose evil and never adds Corruption. Record it separately, give it discoverable behaviour, and provide at least one means of removal, redirection, fulfilment, or acceptance.

Forced possession, mind control, lycanthropy, undeath, and magical transformation are likewise conditions unless the player knowingly chooses to embrace them for power.

---

## Voluntary Dark Patrons

A character may accept a Dark Patron during this path. Possible patrons include the domain itself, a rival prisoner, a dead god, a victim seeking vengeance, one of the ruler's enemies, a powerful lieutenant, or the ruler.

Patronage is always an explicit choice. Before a choice can add Corruption, state its consequence plainly:

> **If you accept this Gift, mark one Corruption and bind yourself to the stated Price. Do you accept?**

If that warning was not given, the action cannot add Corruption.

### The Patron Offer

Every offer states three things:

- **Gift:** The exact ability gained
- **Price:** The immediate obligation, sacrifice, restriction, or visible change
- **Claim:** What the patron will ask for if the character seeks greater power

Declining carries no Corruption and no hidden mechanical penalty. The patron may continue pursuing its established agenda, but it cannot punish a character merely for refusing a choice that was presented as optional.

### Corruption Track

Corruption belongs to an individual character and remains visible to that player.

| Corruption | Voluntary Threshold |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0 — Free** | No patron claim |
| **1 — Marked** | Accept a Lesser Gift and its stated Price |
| **2 — Bound** | Accept a Greater Gift or knowingly fulfil a corrupt demand for power |
| **3 — Favoured** | Accept a Transforming Gift; the patron's influence becomes permanent or visibly supernatural |
| **4 — Claimed** | Voluntarily surrender freedom, take the dark throne, betray the escape, or accept the patron's final ownership; the character is lost |

Corruption never increases because of:

- A failed roll
- Taking damage or falling to 0 HP
- Being cursed, possessed, charmed, or transformed against one's will
- Using an ability already paid for by a previous patron bargain
- Refusing a patron's later request
- The group making a choice on the character's behalf

To gain another mark, the player must accept a new Gift, corrupt demand, or Claim after being told the consequence.

### Renouncing a Patron

A character may renounce the bond at any time and immediately lose access to all patron Gifts. Removing Corruption requires concrete restitution or a severance expedition appropriate to the patron. Each completed act removes one mark. The patron can oppose the attempt through ordinary fictional actions, but a failed severance attempt does not add Corruption.

### Patron Placement Across the Arc

- **Levels 1–3:** A patron may offer survival, escape from a curse, or power against a minion.
- **Levels 4–7:** It may offer forbidden history, access to a Protection, or a power modelled upon the boss.
- **Levels 8–9:** It may offer decisive leverage with a severe but explicit Claim.
- **Level 10:** It may offer victory in exchange for becoming Claimed, replacing the ruler, or remaining in the domain.

The offer is a temptation, not a mandatory balancing mechanism. A party that refuses every patron remains fully capable of completing the path through mortal allies, recovered tools, and discovered circumstances.

## The Finale

At level 10, the party enters the Heart with whichever Protections it has neutralized and whichever lieutenants, allies, curses, patron Gifts, and unresolved consequences remain.

Every boss dossier defines which of the following routes are possible and their exact requirements.

### Slay

Physically destroy the ruler using the required Tool or Circumstance after disabling the relevant Protections. Combat cannot produce permanent victory until these conditions are met.

### Release or Redeem

End the Curse by confronting the ruler with the Crime, Victim, and possibility of restitution, acceptance, grief, or death. This route requires actions established by the dossier, not one lucky Charisma roll.

### Persuade or Seduce

Use the ruler's Desire and Need to make it voluntarily surrender power, open the prison, choose the party over its obsession, or enter a situation in which it becomes vulnerable. The party must possess a credible offer, relationship, or Secret before a reaction or Charisma test can decide uncertainty.

### Deceive or Compel

Exploit a domain law, true name, divided identity, oath, prophecy, institutional rule, or false assumption. The ruler may be induced to break its own Protection or fulfil the condition of its Curse.

### Bargain

Negotiate release at a known price. A bargain can be honourable, tragic, selfish, or corrupt. Any Corruption is stated before acceptance and occurs because a character chooses the bargain, never because the negotiation roll failed.

### Replace

Take the ruler's throne, role, Curse, or patron. The other characters may escape, but the voluntary replacement becomes **Claimed** and loses the campaign unless the dossier explicitly provides a sacrificial release without corruption.

### Failure

The path is lost when:

- Every character dies or becomes permanently unable to act.
- Every surviving character voluntarily becomes Claimed or submits.
- The party chooses to abandon escape and serve the ruler.

A failed confrontation does not automatically end the campaign if free characters survive. Apply capture, injury, loss of leverage, a completed Pressure consequence, or another established result and allow the survivors to continue.

When the ruler is validly resolved, the Heart produces a visible exit: lifting mist, an open road, waking from the shared dream, a returning ship, a broken wall of darkness, or the restoration of the world from which the domain was cut.

## Boss Dossier Specification

Each implemented boss requires the following data. A dossier is incomplete until every field is authored.

| Field | Required Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Identity** | True nature, public identity, aliases, and what can reveal it |
| **Fall** | Mortal, Desire, Crime, Victim, Curse, and Need |
| **Environments** | Threshold, Dominion, and Heart palettes with six sites each |
| **Minions** | Two Signs, two Keepers, and two Wardens with active schemes |
| **Lieutenants** | Hand, Voice, and Jailer with loyalty, want, survival method, clue, and defection condition |
| **Pressure** | Six consequences and two to four exact fictional triggers |
| **Clues** | Witness, Record, and Place/Object expressions for every required conclusion |
| **Protections** | Three reasons immediate victory cannot be permanent |
| **Leverage** | Tool, Ally, and Secret or Circumstance |
| **Patrons** | At least one optional patron source with Lesser, Greater, Transforming, and Claim offers |
| **Finale** | Requirements and consequences for every supported resolution route |
| **Loss** | How characters die, submit, transform, serve, or replace the ruler |
| **Exit** | The fictional sign that confirms the domain has opened |

### Dossier Families and Reuse

The 38 dossiers should share procedures without collapsing into reskins. Implement reusable modules for:

- **Undying Body:** Vampires, liches, mummies, death knights, and dracoliches
- **Haunting:** Ghost lords, ghost knights, warlords, and captains
- **Institutional Rule:** Warlords, poison courts, false priests, and fallen champions
- **Hidden Control:** Hypnotists, Elder Brains, doppelgangers, hive queens, and dream entities
- **Pack or Family:** Wererats, wolfweres, werewolves, loup-garou, hags, and divided bloodlines
- **Creation and Transformation:** Flesh golems, vivisectionists, living walls, puppets, and stitched subjects
- **Arcane Architecture:** Liches, evokers, sorceresses, rakshasas, and curse-makers
- **Cosmic Function:** Death incarnations, denied succession, dream law, and negative-plane breaches

Shared modules provide data shapes, clue templates, and procedures. Each dossier still requires a distinct Fall, Pressure, environment trio, lieutenants, and Resolution matrix.

The player snapshot contains only revealed lore, encountered NPCs, visible conditions, known items, current patron terms, and the character's chosen Corruption.

The settlement, wilderness, dungeon, encounter, rumour, and NPC generators request an optional hidden **path insertion**. The server returns only the completed fictional result. It never emits labels such as `boss_clue`, `act_2`, `domains_of_dread`, or `domain_pressure` to player clients.

When another one of the future Adventure Paths is selected, this same interface supplies its own insertions. The players learn which campaign they are in only by understanding the world—if they ever give it a name at all.
