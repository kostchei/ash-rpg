# Adventure Path 2: The Mind Below

> **System-facing campaign procedure:** the players are never told that this Adventure Path is active. They meet missing people, altered friends, subterranean factions, protective relics, and impossible water. The aboleths become apparent only if the players assemble the evidence.

In **The Mind Below**, an aboleth savant is extending its rule upward by stealing useful minds and changing the people around the party. Its first weapon is not an invading army. It is the trusted official, returned captive, frightened witness, or beloved relative who has been made to serve.

The campaign covers **levels 1–10**, three acts, and three independently selected environments. It has 27 possible geographic combinations:

> **Surface origin → subterranean descent → drowned dominion**

Its common movement is:

> **Notice changed people → follow the traffic in minds → learn how control works → acquire protection and aquatic access → dismantle the Savant's support → confront the intelligence below.**

The final region is genuinely underwater. A character without a means of breathing underwater cannot enter it, and some end zones also require protection from pressure, heat, or blindness. This is a logistical problem with several discoverable solutions, not a saving throw that can be forced through.

---

## The Hidden-Path Principle

`the_mind_below`, act numbers, progress tracks, encounter budgets, and aquatic-access tags are system information. Player-facing results contain only the fiction.

The campaign might begin:

> The rain has turned the street to dung-coloured mud. The innkeeper says the missing clerk returned yesterday, paid his debts, and left again before dawn. Nobody remembers seeing him eat, sleep, or speak to his wife.

It might instead begin at a witch trial or beneath the red towers of a desert court. Nothing announces that an aboleth is the final enemy. At first, every event has a plausible local explanation.

### What the players see

- NPCs whose priorities, loyalties, or memories have changed
- Disappearances and organized movement toward old waterways
- Shared dreams, repeated phrases, mucus, water, and psychic anomalies
- Factions who fear one another more than they understand the threat
- Protective herbs, charms, relics, bargains, and transformations
- Physical consequences when the Savant's operations succeed

### What the system keeps hidden

- The selected surface, cave, and end zones
- **Reach**, **Awakening**, **Knowledge**, and **Access** values
- Which NPCs are controlled, compromised, pretending, or free
- The locations and functions of the three Savant installations
- Unintroduced aquatic-access methods
- The current encounter budget and unused reinforcement budget

At least half of generated people and places remain ordinary regional content. Not every cruel official is controlled and not every disappearance belongs to the Savant.

### No-GM guarantees

Because no referee is present to repair a bad random result, the generator enforces the following safeguards:

- Do not repeat an encounter seed within the same zone until all six have appeared.
- Never place more active creatures than the party-size limit, even if the remaining budget could purchase them.
- An encounter that threatens the party's only aquatic-access method must telegraph the threat and provide a repair, escape, or replacement lead. A single unseen roll cannot permanently remove the only route forward.
- Before Act III, reveal at least three aquatic-access methods from different sources. At least one cannot depend upon a particular character class.
- Generate the selected end zone and its requirements early enough that available access methods can satisfy them.
- If an encounter result contradicts established facts, preserve the established facts and reinterpret the result as a rumour, failed enemy plan, related location, or evidence of deliberate deception.

## Creating the Path

### 1. Select one environment for each act

Choose or randomly select:

1. One **Starting Zone** for Act I
2. One **Cave Route** for Act II
3. One **End Zone** for Act III

Any starting zone can lead into any cave route, and any cave route can reach any end zone. Establish a physical connection when the path is generated: a sewer entering lava tubes, a desert aqueduct reaching a sandstone river, or a funerary barge descending toward a volcanic sea.

### 2. Establish the Savant's need

Choose what the aboleth is taking from the surface:

| d6 | Need | Typical targets |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Administrative knowledge | Clerks, magistrates, tax collectors, guild officers |
| 2 | Arcane technique | Wizards, astrologers, artificers, occult scholars |
| 3 | Ancestral memory | Elders, priests, genealogists, spirit mediums |
| 4 | Engineering knowledge | Miners, aqueduct keepers, architects, navigators |
| 5 | Social authority | Nobles, beloved healers, watch captains, family heads |
| 6 | Particular minds | Dreamers, prodigies, reincarnated enemies, psychic sensitives |

The need determines who is abducted, who is returned under control, and what the Grand Savant is trying to complete.

### 3. Create the control network

Give the Savant:

- One surface recruiter who appears entirely ordinary
- One transport lieutenant who moves captives underground
- One subterranean warden who controls a route or settlement
- One dissident former servant who understands part of the process
- Three recurring controlled NPCs whose behaviour can change over time
- Three installations supporting the final confrontation

Mind control changes NPC decisions before it directly attacks player agency. Controlled people conceal entrances, transfer prisoners, destroy evidence, frame rivals, and protect operations they would once have opposed.

Direct control of a player character should be short, clearly signalled, and leave meaningful decisions whenever possible. Against a lone character, it must never become an automatic campaign-ending skipped turn.

## Progress and Consequences

Track four values from 0–6. They are changed by specific fictional events, never merely because a session ended or a character failed a roll.

| Track | Meaning | Advances when |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | People and institutions the aboleths can direct | An operation succeeds, a controlled official retains power, or a relay is established |
| **Awakening** | Completion of the Grand Savant's project | Captives, memories, relics, or infrastructure reach the depths |
| **Knowledge** | What the party has proved about the enemy | Independent clues are corroborated, a servant is questioned, or a memory archive is recovered |
| **Access** | Practical ability to enter and operate in the final realm | Protection, allies, routes, vehicles, spells, or adaptations are secured |

Do not expose the numbers. Reveal their effects.

| Value | Reach or Awakening consequence | Knowledge or Access consequence |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–1 | Isolated anomalies | Rumours and incomplete leads |
| 2–3 | Coordinated local operations | The party can identify patterns and seek specific tools |
| 4–5 | Institutions or routes openly serve the plan | The Savant's structure and viable entry routes are understood |
| 6 | Surface capture or completion becomes imminent | The party can attempt a properly prepared final expedition |

A failed adventure may create an opportunity for Reach or Awakening to advance, but failure itself is not the trigger. The trigger is what an enemy then accomplishes in the world.

## Encounter Scaling for 1–6 Players

Encounter balance is a risk estimate, not a promise. Reaction rolls, morale, retreat, negotiation, and environmental solutions remain part of the encounter procedure.

### Party Power

**Party Power** is the sum of the participating player-character levels. Each combat-capable retainer contributes half its level, rounded down.

| Party at level 5 | Party Power |
|---|---:|
| One character | 5 |
| Two characters | 10 |
| Four characters | 20 |
| Six characters | 30 |

### Encounter ceiling

Add the levels of all hostile creatures that can participate in the encounter.

| Intensity | Maximum enemy-level total |
|---|---:|
| Pressure or incidental danger | ½ Party Power |
| Standard encounter | Party Power |
| Dangerous encounter | 1½ Party Power |
| Major confrontation | 2 × Party Power |

This is a ceiling. An encounter does not have to spend all of it.

### Hazards and objectives

Hazards consume part of the same ceiling.

| Hazard | Effective monster level |
|---|---:|
| Minor: darkness, slippery ledge, ordinary current | ½ average character level |
| Major: flood, lava flow, collapsing passage | Average character level |
| Extreme: pressure failure, eruption, abyssal current | 2 × average character level |

If the party must rescue prisoners, protect a vessel, interrupt a ritual, or carry a helpless NPC, reduce the remaining monster allowance by one quarter. If enemies begin with surprise, prepared crossfire, or a decisive environmental advantage, treat the encounter as one intensity harder rather than adding monsters.

### Active enemies and waves

No ordinary encounter begins with more than **party size + 1 independently acting hostiles**. Additional creatures appear as a reinforcement wave, use a mob action, or remain occupied until the party changes the situation.

- One character normally faces one or two active enemies.
- Two characters normally face no more than three.
- Large parties receive more roles and waves rather than inflated enemy HP.
- A non-boss creature more than two levels above the party average requires clear warning and an avoidable or exploitable position.

### Example: one encounter at three scales

A sandstone passage contains kuo-toa scouts negotiating with an abolethic warden while rising water threatens both groups. The party must recover a captive carrying directions to the drowned realm.

At level 5:

- **One character:** one level-3 scout and a minor current hazard.
- **Two characters:** one level-5 warden and one level-3 scout.
- **Four characters:** one level-5 warden, three level-3 scouts, and a major flood hazard.
- **Six characters:** one level-5 warden, five level-3 scouts, a level-4 slime, and a major flood hazard.

The same premise is preserved. A small party meets an isolated exchange; a large party interrupts the full delegation.

### Boss tempo

Do not turn the Savant into an HP sponge.

| Players | Final confrontation structure |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Savant with reduced tempo, an allied asset, and at least two exploitable weaknesses |
| 2 | Savant plus one bodyguard or environmental threat |
| 3–4 | Savant, one lieutenant per two characters, and intermittent lair effects |
| 5–6 | Savant, lieutenants, one reinforcement wave, and an active lair effect |

For a solo character, the Savant alternates its full attack routine and psychic or lair action rather than using both in the same round. An ally, vehicle, summoned power, or disabled installation must prevent a single domination effect from ending the confrontation.

# Act I: The Stolen Minds — Levels 1–3

The party encounters disappearances, improbable betrayals, and people whose personalities have subtly changed. The incidents initially resemble local crime, superstition, ambition, or family conflict.

Act I ends when the party establishes that captives, memories, or information are being transported underground.

## Starting Zone A: The Cesspit City

A temperate, overcrowded city of canals, night-soil carts, collapsing tenements, crooked guilds, poor inns, expensive clean water, and wealthy citizens who seldom touch the streets.

**Factions:** the Night-Soil Guild, canal smugglers, a compromised watch precinct, an occult college, and beggars who recognize people who “came back wrong.”

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A returned clerk tries to destroy records of subterranean property. He is controlled but fights only if prevented from reaching the archive. |
| 2 | Guild carts carry sedated captives beneath heaps of dung. Ordinary workers follow one controlled foreman and an amphibious handler. |
| 3 | A corpse contains water in its lungs and translucent mucus beneath its fingernails, though it never entered the river. |
| 4 | Guests at a poor inn share the same dream. One sleepwalker opens a door for something waiting outside. |
| 5 | Scum attempt to seize a scholar rather than kill the party. Their objective is the important clue. |
| 6 | A sealed cistern contains an abandoned psychic relay and a route into the old waterworks. |

Possible descents include sewers, drowned catacombs, a buried river, and ancient waterworks.

## Starting Zone B: The Ashen Shire

A rural district of hedgerows, muddy farms, sacred wells, abandoned mines, and gathering witch trials. Psychic symptoms are blamed upon witches; some of the accused possess genuine protective knowledge.

**Factions:** a manipulated witchfinder, hedge witches, a frightened village council, shepherds watching the sinkholes, and families hiding changed relatives.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A child predicts disappearances because something speaks through the village well. The mob assumes the child causes them. |
| 2 | Every inhabitant of a farm calmly walked into a flooded quarry, leaving dinner on the table. |
| 3 | Scum attack a witchfinder's prisoners to seize one herbalist who knows a mental ward. |
| 4 | A changed farmer quietly prepares his family for transport underground. |
| 5 | Anyone listening to the singing well hears instructions in the voice of someone they trust. |
| 6 | Controlled villagers excavate a sealed passage while armed neighbours protect them. |

Possible descents include a quarry, sacred well, limestone sinkhole, abbey crypt, and flooded mine.

## Starting Zone C: The Red Spires

A desert of red sand, decadent courts, mystical fortresses, observatories, buried aqueducts, and towers constructed around ancient water sources.

**Factions:** rival sorcerer-princes, water merchants, tomb robbers, aqueduct guardians, and a pleasure cult unknowingly repeating abolethic rites.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A mirage shows party members performing actions they have not yet taken. |
| 2 | An observatory staff vanishes except for an assistant who insists they never existed. |
| 3 | A controlled noble purchases every cistern connected to an ancient channel. |
| 4 | Masked guests at a decadent gathering are evaluated for minds worth stealing. |
| 5 | Bound elementals redirect water toward a buried gate while defenders believe they are repairing the city. |
| 6 | Something in a glass well answers questions in exchange for childhood memories. |

Possible descents include buried aqueducts, an obsidian well, a collapsed ziggurat, and the submerged foundations of a sorcerous fortress.

# Act II: The Descent — Levels 4–7

The party finds a complete subterranean world rather than a single monster lair. It discovers multiple aboleths, controlled settlements, rival factions, the mechanism of domination, and the first reliable accounts of the Grand Savant.

Act II must introduce at least three viable aquatic-access routes: one magical, one factional, and one technological or transformational. No specific class is required.

## Cave Route A: The Emberglass Tubes

Ancient lava tubes lined with gems, metallic fungi, volcanic glass, hot springs, toxic gases, and unstable magma chambers.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A gem seam repeats the final thoughts of dead miners. |
| 2 | Duergar have fitted controlled workers with cooling masks that also transmit commands. |
| 3 | A fungal forest offers safe passage if fed a valuable memory. |
| 4 | Scum rupture their heated brine tank and flood a narrow tube. |
| 5 | A lava bridge can be crossed, destroyed to stop pursuit, or redirected toward a psychic relay. |
| 6 | A derro engineer has built a crude Nautilus but believes the vessel is his mother. |

## Cave Route B: The Living Sandstone

Water-carved passages filled with rivers, bats, slimes, fish, insects, and complete subterranean food chains. This route most readily introduces kuo-toa, merfolk exiles, and breathing magic.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A bat colony forms words when seen from below. |
| 2 | A gelatinous creature contains a living messenger and an intact gill charm. |
| 3 | Kuo-toa pilgrims carry a captive whose dreams are creating a minor god. |
| 4 | A river reverses direction whenever the Savant issues a command. |
| 5 | Sahuagin hunters attack Scum and assume the party are collaborators. |
| 6 | A submerged passage is short enough to cross while holding one's breath—unless something closes the far gate. |

## Cave Route C: The Dust Sepulchres

Dry, airless tunnels containing demon seals, mummified dead, lost funerary cities, and spirits trapped far from living water. Aboleth influence appears through dreams, relics, and controlled tomb robbers.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A mummy recognizes one character as a priest who died centuries ago. |
| 2 | Controlled excavators break a seal because the demon behind it knows a route to the sea. |
| 3 | A spirit offers protection from domination but demands temporary possession of a character's voice. |
| 4 | Murals show the Savant using different names in successive civilizations. |
| 5 | A dry fountain produces water only when someone willingly surrenders a memory. |
| 6 | A funerary barge resting on sand begins sailing when its old rites are completed. |

## Protection with a Price

Protection should enable plans while creating decisions. A failed roll may deplete a relic, inflict a debility, attract attention, or cause an arcane scar. It does not inflict moral Corruption.

| Tradition | Example protection | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Charms and talismans | Bitter crown herb | Double the effective Intelligence or Wisdom save bonus against psychic control | Must be renewed; causes nausea and prevents magical sleep |
| Maker relic | Thought-cage helm | Blocks direct domination | Also blocks telepathy and helpful mind magic; emits a detectable pulse when tested |
| Barrow iron and amber | Memory nail | Anchors one chosen identity or memory | The protected memory cannot naturally change until the nail is removed |
| Aberrant reagent | Illithid membrane hood | Makes the wearer appear controlled to psychic senses | The wearer experiences stray commands and alien dreams |
| Blessed rite | River-spirit ward | Breaks a psychic link or reveals its direction | Requires Stock, sacrifice, or a debt to the local spirit |
| Lightbearer invocation | Unclouded sun | Burns away concealment and mental influence | Causes debility, collateral glare, or diminished duration |

Maker feedback, curses, diseases, scars, and transformations are consequences. **Corruption remains the result of accepting an explicit dark patron bargain.**

## Aquatic Access

Every character entering Act III must receive the required capabilities personally or from a functioning party-scale method.

| Method | Scope | Capabilities | Significant drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wizard's water-breathing spell | Party | Breathing | Limited duration; can be dispelled or disrupted |
| Merfolk blessing | Personal | Breathing, movement, communication | Requires an oath, service, or protection of merfolk territory |
| Sahuagin blood rite | Personal | Breathing, pressure, movement | Marks the recipient by scent and produces predatory impulses |
| Kuo-toa gill charm | Personal | Breathing | Must remain wet and may depend on a peculiar minor god |
| Nautilus vessel | Party | Breathing, pressure, movement, navigation, heat | Large, conspicuous, and vulnerable to damage |
| Maker diving armour | Personal | Breathing, pressure, heat | Slow, noisy, and powered by a finite or unstable source |
| Scum adaptation | Personal | Breathing, pressure, movement | Permanent bodily and social consequences; psychic vulnerability |
| Aboleth mucus exposure | Personal emergency | Breathing for 1d4 hours | The victim can breathe only underwater during that time |

### Mucous Cloud and Water-Lung

While underwater, an aboleth is surrounded by transformative mucus. A creature that touches it or hits it with a melee attack from within 5 feet makes a **DC 14 Constitution save**. On a failure, it contracts **Water-Lung** for `1d4` hours. It can breathe only underwater for that duration and begins suffocating if removed into air.

Water-Lung does not automatically create a permanent Scum.

### Permanent Scum

Permanent transformation results from prolonged exposure, deliberate stabilization, surgical alteration, or completion of an aboleth conversion process. Captured characters can be afflicted and later rescued or cured. A character may instead choose to stabilize the change as an access method.

A stabilized Scum can breathe underwater, swim efficiently, resist ordinary deep-water pressure, and sense nearby abolethic transmissions. In exchange:

- It requires regular immersion
- Aboleths recognize and locate it more easily
- Some surface communities consider it a monster
- Certain psychic commands affect it more strongly

Choosing this biological adaptation is not automatically Corruption. Accepting it from the Savant as a patron gift, with an understood claim upon the character, is a voluntary Corruption bargain.

# Act III: The Drowned Mind — Levels 8–10

The final region contains the Savant's dominion, its psychic infrastructure, lieutenants, victims, and several possible ways to end its control.

## End Zone A: The Faerzress Sea

A vast subterranean sea lies beneath a vaulted cavern. Kuo-toa, derro, duergar, Scum, and surface refugees occupy shores and islands. Faerzress disrupts navigation, teleportation, and divination.

**Required:** breathing. Reliable navigation or a local guide is strongly recommended.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A settlement is divided between free citizens and their controlled relatives. |
| 2 | Every divination reveals a different location for the Savant. |
| 3 | Duergar sell pressure armour containing hidden obedience runes. |
| 4 | Kuo-toa accidentally create a god capable of shielding minds, but it demands worship. |
| 5 | A Scum community wants freedom without being “cured” into bodies its members no longer want. |
| 6 | An aboleth lieutenant governs through a genuinely beloved mortal mayor. |

This zone emphasizes alliances, espionage, settlements, and political control.

## End Zone B: The Black Abyss

An ultra-deep trench contains bioluminescent forests, transparent predators, drifting carcasses, and ruins constructed on vertical cliffs.

**Required:** breathing, pressure protection, and navigation. Light, echolocation, or another way of perceiving the environment is part of navigation. Ordinary water-breathing magic is insufficient by itself.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The descent crosses layers of increasingly hostile pressure. |
| 2 | A vast blind predator follows psychic activity rather than movement. |
| 3 | A ruined station contains records of the Savant's arrival. |
| 4 | Controlled whales carry fortifications and soldiers inside their bodies. |
| 5 | Luminous plants display stolen memories as moving images. |
| 6 | A narrow trench blocks the Nautilus. Another route, a way to alter the vessel, or personal access equipment can be found nearby; this result cannot simply destroy the party's only access method. |

This zone emphasizes isolation, alien ecology, pressure, and cosmic horror.

## End Zone C: The Mireforge

A turbulent, muddy basin is split by volcanic vents. Scalding currents, mineral clouds, ash, poison, and earthquakes continually reshape the terrain.

**Required:** breathing, heat protection, and navigation in zero visibility.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A volcanic plume separates the party and carries voices from elsewhere. |
| 2 | Scum workers maintain psychic towers anchored around a caldera. |
| 3 | A mudslide exposes a city the Savant attempted to erase from history. |
| 4 | Sahuagin launch a suicidal offensive and expect the party to join it. |
| 5 | A damaged Nautilus becomes trapped above an opening lava fissure. |
| 6 | The Savant senses through muddy water; clearing it blinds the Savant but awakens something beneath the sediment. |

This zone emphasizes violent terrain, poor information, unstable alliances, and time pressure.

## Preparing the Final Confrontation

The Grand Savant is supported by three installations:

1. **The Chorus** coordinates controlled minds. Neutralizing it removes or sharply limits mass domination.
2. **The Memory Well** repairs the Savant with stolen memories. Neutralizing it prevents recovery and exposes contradictions in its identity.
3. **The Pressure Heart** controls currents and the physical environment. Neutralizing it removes the strongest lair effects.

Each installation can be destroyed, captured, deceived, reprogrammed, or turned over to another faction. Each one neutralized removes one major boss capability. This is the primary way a small party makes the final encounter survivable.

The Savant can be defeated by:

- Killing it after disabling its protections
- Freeing enough controlled minds to overwhelm the Chorus
- Replacing, poisoning, or falsifying its memories
- Bargaining with rival underwater powers
- Trapping it in a collapsing or sealed realm
- Persuading it that its great project has already succeeded
- Accepting its patronage and becoming its successors

The last outcome may stop the immediate campaign while losing the characters to voluntary Corruption. No failed save makes that decision for them.

## End States

| Result | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Savant killed, network intact | Lieutenants compete for its abandoned minds and infrastructure |
| Network dismantled, Savant escapes | The surface is freed, but the enemy remembers the party |
| Controlled population liberated | Survivors must reconcile actions, memories, and transformed bodies |
| Rival faction inherits the network | One tyranny may have been exchanged for another |
| Savant deceived or persuaded | The bargain lasts only while its assumptions remain true |
| Party accepts succession | The old Savant falls and a new intelligence begins looking upward |

The path ends when the control process has been stopped, redirected, inherited, or allowed to complete—not merely when one aboleth body reaches zero HP.
