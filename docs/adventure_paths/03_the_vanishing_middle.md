# Adventure Path 3: The Vanishing Middle

> **System-facing campaign procedure:** the players are never told that this Adventure Path is active. They take honest work in a mortal quarrel, watch both sides reach for help, and discover — late, and by paying for it — what the help costs.

In **The Vanishing Middle**, nobody is trying to destroy the world. Two absolute principles are each trying to save it, and both are correct about what is wrong. Law can end a plague, settle an inheritance, stop a raid, and hold a border. Chaos can free a slave, break a bad law, cure an incurable body, and overturn a bought verdict. Each intervention is defensible. Each intervention licenses the next one from the other side. The catastrophe is assembled entirely out of reasonable local decisions.

The campaign covers **levels 1–10**, three acts, and three independently selected environments, giving 27 combinations. Its common movement is:

> **Take work in a mortal quarrel → watch both sides invite a patron → learn what aligned power costs → gather what neither pole has touched → hold it through the Weighing.**

This path is not resolved by killing a boss. There is no boss. There is a **shrinking list of things that still belong to nobody**, and the campaign is about what remains on that list when the two hosts arrive.

Its single design thesis:

> **The middle shrinks the same way in a province and in a person, and it is measured with the same list.**

---

## The Hidden-Path Principle

`the_vanishing_middle`, act numbers, Hold values, the Middle count, Allegiance, Stain, and Escalation tiers are system terms. Player-facing results contain only the fiction.

The campaign might begin:

> The ferry has been stopped four days. Both claimants to the crossing are decent people with documents, and the town has begun taking sides by street. The elder heir has sent to the assize at Marrowgate for a judge. The younger has sent somewhere else, and will not say where.

Nothing announces a cosmic war. For the whole of Act I there is no cosmic war — only two households, a drought, or a fever, and a set of people who are each right about one thing.

### What the players see

- A local dispute in which both sides have a genuine claim and no obviously correct answer
- Ordinary people who begin talking about order, or about freedom, with more heat than the dispute deserves
- Help arriving from outside: a judge with an unusual warrant, a physician with an unusual draught
- Weather, measurement, memory, and biology becoming slightly unreliable in one direction or the other
- Offers made to the party personally, stated plainly, always refusable
- Places and people they liked, quietly ceasing to be neutral ground

### What the system keeps hidden

- The selected Quarrel, Theatre, and Weighing Ground
- **Law's Hold**, **Chaos's Hold**, and the current Middle count
- Which of the twelve Middle names have been struck, and by whose act
- Each character's Allegiance bars and the current Escalation tier
- Which Anchors are reachable, and which are already stained
- Which character the Recognition is waiting for

At least half of all generated people and places remain ordinary regional content. Not every judge carries a Warrant, and not every changed creature was changed by a Lord.

### No-GM guarantees

Because no referee is present to repair a bad random result, the generator enforces the following:

- **No pole ever claims a character on a failed roll.** Allegiance is earned by deeds the player chose. Corruption requires a stated Gift, Price, and Claim, and an explicit acceptance.
- **Refusal is always free to the character.** A pole may spend a refusal against the world (see *Called*, below). It may never punish the refuser with a hidden penalty.
- **The Symmetry Rule is public, and is telegraphed once before it is ever billed.** The first matched escalation is shown arriving and can be avoided; after that the party is deemed to know the price.
- **The last name in the Middle cannot be struck by a random result.** When a roll would take it, it becomes contested instead, and the party get one clearly signposted chance to hold it.
- **At least two Anchors must be reachable at any time from Act II onward**, from different sources, and no Anchor may require a specific class, spell, or ancestry.
- **The Champion's Doom never removes the Beloved by fiat.** It is always the stated price of something the party asked for, and they may always decline and go without the help.
- If a result contradicts an established fact, preserve the fact and reinterpret the result as rumour, a rival's failed attempt, or a deliberate forgery — of which this path produces a great many.

## Creating the Path

### 1. Select one environment for each act

1. One **Quarrel** for Act I — the mortal dispute
2. One **Theatre** for Act II — where escalation is bought and displayed
3. One **Weighing Ground** for Act III — where the claim is settled

Any Quarrel can escalate into any Theatre, and any Theatre can reach any Ground. Establish the physical and political connection when the path is generated: the ferry town is a week upriver from the Opal Court; the fever city already pays tribute to the Ruled Province.

### 2. Write what each side is right about

Neither side of the Quarrel may be simply wrong. Roll or choose a pair.

| d6 | The side that will reach for Law is right that | The side that will reach for Chaos is right that |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | The agreement was signed, and has been honoured for sixty years | The agreement was signed by people who had no right to sign it |
| 2 | Without a register the raiding never stops | The register is a list of who to take first |
| 3 | The cure works, repeatably, on everyone | The cure ends the patient and leaves the body running |
| 4 | Somebody must hold the crossing or nobody eats | The last holder starved them on purpose |
| 5 | The verdict was procedurally correct | The verdict was bought |
| 6 | Children are dying now, and the method is written on the wall | The method killed the eastern quarter, and is still killing it |

### 3. Name the two Petitioners

Somebody invites each pole in. They are mortals, they are local, and they are not villains.

| d6 | Petitioner | What they ask for | What they get |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | A widow with a document | A judge who cannot be bribed | One — and everything that follows a judge |
| 2 | A guild master | An end to the shortage | Fixed prices, fixed wages, fixed people |
| 3 | A physician | A cure | A cure |
| 4 | A young heir | Her inheritance restored | The record of the theft unmade, and the record of everything else with it |
| 5 | A captain of twenty | Force enough to hold the line | Force enough |
| 6 | A parent | Their child back | Their child back, in some sense |

Both Petitioners are recurring NPCs. Neither may later be revealed as secretly evil. Their tragedy is that they succeeded.

### 4. Name the Middle

Write **twelve** things in the region that currently belong to nobody: places, institutions, professions, roads, families, festivals, markets, a bridge, a song. Give each a name and a location. This list is the campaign's record and its Toll.

| d12 | Category | Example |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | A crossing | The Low Ferry |
| 2 | A market | Thursday market at Ashfold |
| 3 | A court or assize | The Grey Assize, which hears both sides |
| 4 | A trade | The nail-smiths, neither guilded nor free |
| 5 | A road | The Drover's Way |
| 6 | A house of healing | The Almshouse at Sedge |
| 7 | A festival | The Ash Fair, kept by both peoples |
| 8 | A family | The Ferrymen of Marrowgate |
| 9 | A holy place used by everyone | The Ford Shrine |
| 10 | A record | The Parish Roll |
| 11 | A song, story, or custom | The Naming of the Flood |
| 12 | A person of standing who has taken no side | Reeve Alder |

### 5. Place the Recognition seed

Decide nothing about which character is the Champion. Choose instead **where** the Recognition is waiting — a mural, a servant who has seen this face before, an object cut to a hand. It lands on whichever character reaches it, and it lands on exactly one.

### 6. Locate the Anchors

For each of the five Anchors, decide one place it could be found in this region and one reason it is currently out of reach. Do not decide which ones the party will get.

## The Record: Two Bars and a List

Three measures. Two rise; one falls.

- **Law's Hold** — how many named things answer to Law
- **Chaos's Hold** — how many answer to Chaos
- **The Middle** — the remaining names, written out, and struck through one at a time

Hold rises and the Middle falls only on a **fictional event**: a faction swears under a Law rite; an institution accepts a Warrant; a place is used for a Chaos rite; a person publicly accepts a Gift; a dispute is settled by aligned power in front of witnesses. Neither bar moves because a session ended, because a roll failed, or because time passed.

### The Toll

The Toll of this path is the struck list itself, kept in full, with who struck each name and how. It never shortens. A won campaign that ends with nine names struck is a different ending from one that ends with three, and the finale reads the list directly.

### The Tell

A character can read the region's tilt by walking through it.

| Hold | What Law feels like | What Chaos feels like |
|---:|---|---|
| **1–2** | Weights and measures become accurate everywhere at once, including in the market where they were profitably wrong. Debts are called on the correct day. | Bread stales in an hour, or keeps a month. Two honest witnesses to one theft describe different weather. |
| **3–4** | Every door in the district has been rehung to the same height, and nobody remembers ordering it. Newborns receive one of eleven approved names. | The road to the mill is a different length going than coming. A woman's shadow has begun arriving before she does. |
| **5–6** | The dead stay exactly where they were put, in rows, and the grass above them grows to one height. Nobody writes a new song, because the old ones are sufficient. | Livestock are born wearing their parents' faces. The river has opinions. A man killed on Tuesday is being killed again. |

### The Reading

When a name is struck, say it in three clauses: **the count, the name, and what is now possible or foreclosed.**

> "Nine left. The Grey Assize is Law's since the assize of the flood. It will still hear your case, and it will not hear it unless you swear."

> "Seven left. Thursday market is Chaos's — it opens when it likes now, and the prices are honest for the first time in a century."

A measure the players cannot read is a Doom Clock in costume. This one is legible from the street.

## Allegiance

Allegiance is how the world reads you. It is **not** Corruption, and the two must never be conflated.

- **Corruption** (see [Domains of Dread](01_domains_of_dread.md)) is what you have sold. It requires a stated Gift, Price, and Claim, and an explicit yes.
- **Allegiance** is what you have been seen to do. It is earned by deeds the player chose, and it can rise without any bargain ever being accepted.

Keep two bars per character, **Law** and **Chaos**, from 0 upward. Both can be high at once, and a character carrying both high is the most Moorcockian object this system can produce.

### Deeds

| Deed | Bar | Points |
|---|:---:|---:|
| Swear a binding oath under a Law rite | Law | +1 |
| Have a thing measured, named, registered, or fixed by a Law instrument | Law | +1 |
| Deliver a person to Law's judgement | Law | +1 |
| Use a Warrant's power where witnesses can see it | Law | +1 |
| Accept an office, commission, or remit | Law | +2 |
| Ask a Lord of Law for direct aid | Law | +2 |
| Accept a Gift | Chaos | +1 |
| Use a Gift where witnesses can see it | Chaos | +1 |
| Break a sworn oath for advantage, and let it be known | Chaos | +1 |
| Free something that was bound, or unmake a standing rule | Chaos | +1 |
| Feed a bound hunger | Chaos | +1 |
| Wear a form that is not yours | Chaos | +2 |
| Ask a Lord of Chaos for direct aid | Chaos | +2 |
| Solve the problem in front of you by mortal means, publicly, when a pole had offered | — | −1 to your higher bar, once per expedition |
| Complete a renunciation expedition against one pole | — | −2 to that bar |

Refusing an offer is worth nothing in either direction, and carries no hidden penalty.

### Thresholds

| One bar | State | Effect |
|---:|---|---|
| **0–2** | Unremarked | Nothing. |
| **3–5** | Noticed | That pole's servants make you one offer per act. The opposing pole's servants select you first when choosing targets. |
| **6–8** | Marked | You carry a visible sign. Unaligned commoners react at **−2 on the 2d6 reaction roll**. The opposing pole treats you as a legitimate target rather than a bystander. Once per act you may call for aid; it always comes, and it always costs. |
| **9+** | Called | The pole asks for the final acceptance, stated as Gift, Price, and Claim. Accepting is Corruption 4 — **Claimed**. Refusing costs the character nothing. It costs the region: the *opposing* bar rises by 2 and one Middle name is struck, because your refusal is the pretext the other side was waiting for. |

That last line is the whole engine in miniature. Standing in the middle is free for you and expensive for everyone around you, and this path never lets a table forget it.

### The Sum: your own middle

Add both bars. This is how much neutral ground is left **in the character**.

| Law + Chaos | Your own middle | Consequence |
|---:|---|---|
| **0–3** | Whole | You may find, carry, and hold any Anchor. |
| **4–7** | Thinning | You may carry and defend an Anchor, but you can no longer be the one who *acquires* a clean one. |
| **8–11** | Narrow | Any Anchor you carry takes a **Stain** if you fight with aligned power while carrying it. |
| **12+** | Gone | You cannot touch an Anchor without staining it. You are not evil and you are not lost. You are simply no longer neutral ground. |

**The design consequence, stated plainly:** the party's most powerful members become escorts for its most ordinary one. Whoever declined the sword and the office is the only person who can carry the thing the campaign is about. Let tables discover this rather than announcing it; when they do, the retainer, the youngest character, or the one who kept saying no becomes the centre of the campaign, and every fight becomes a fight about keeping somebody else's hands empty.

## The Symmetry Rule

Each pole answers the other. It answers **what was spent**, not what level the party has reached.

| What the party spent to resolve a situation | The other pole's next response |
|---|---|
| Nothing aligned | Ordinary. Scale to Party Power as usual |
| One Gift or Warrant use, unwitnessed | Ordinary, but it arrives one scene sooner |
| One Gift or Warrant use, witnessed | **One intensity step harder** |
| A binding, an artifact, or a Lord's granted aid | One step harder, **and the opposing Hold rises by 1** |
| A Lord acting in person | The opposing pole sends a peer. This is a set-piece event, not an encounter: it happens to the region whether or not the party attend |

The tier falls again only when the party settle a comparable problem **without aligned power, in front of witnesses from both sides**. Winning quietly does not de-escalate. Winning visibly and mortally does.

> **Worked example.** At level 5 the party break a raid using the Warrant of the Clean Cut, in front of the whole village. The raid is broken perfectly, the village is grateful, and none of that is taken back. Four days later the answering encounter is not the standard warband the level would suggest but a dangerous one, and the thing at its head is wearing somebody's face. The party did not lose that fight. They set the price of the next one.

This is also the path's version of the engine framework's *failing obvious move*. Choosing the better side works, tactically, every single time. Let it work. Then let the count go up anyway.

## Encounter Scaling for 1–6 Players

Use the Party Power, encounter ceiling, hazard, and active-enemy rules from [The Mind Below](02_the_mind_below.md) unchanged. This path adds three rules of its own.

**Matched response, not scaled response.** The Symmetry Rule sets intensity before Party Power sets the ceiling. A solo character who has spent nothing faces standard encounters at level. A solo character carrying a Black Blade faces dangerous ones — a consequence they purchased, having been shown the price.

**Hosts are terrain.** From late Act II both poles field armies. An army is never an encounter and never has a level total. It is an **extreme hazard** plus an objective: cross it, cut through it, get somebody out of it, be somewhere before it arrives. Characters do not defeat hosts. They survive them, and steal from between them.

**Duels are the boss form.** This path's set-piece confrontations are single champions with witnesses, not warbands. Build a champion as a major confrontation for the party size. A champion always fights on a stated condition — first blood, until the standard falls, until one yields — and the condition is negotiable beforehand and enforceable during. For a solo character, a duel must offer a non-lethal victory condition and one exploitable point in the champion's own allegiance: a Law champion cannot break terms it named aloud; a Chaos champion cannot resist being offered something better halfway through.

## Gifts, Warrants, and Bindings

Both poles arm mortals, in different currencies, and the difference is the texture of the whole path: **Chaos gives you something and takes something. Law lends you something and tells you what you may no longer do.**

Accepting either is a Corruption event and must be offered in the standard form — Gift, Price, Claim — before it can mark anything.

### Chaos Gifts (d12)

Each is a Lesser Gift (Corruption 1) unless the offer states otherwise. Using one publicly is Chaos Allegiance +1 per expedition.

| d12 | Gift | What it does | The price, which is permanent |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Ninth Hand** | A limb of some other creature's kind grows from your back: one extra attack or object interaction each round | Armour must be recut, and nothing hides it |
| 2 | **Unbound Flesh** | Reshape face and frame over an hour; perfect disguise | Each use, roll d6. On a 1 you cannot return to your own shape until somebody who loves you says your name |
| 3 | **Vermilion Blood** | Regain 1d4 HP at the start of each of your turns while below half | Divine and primal healing no longer works on you. Ever |
| 4 | **The Laughing Lung** | Speak, and one compulsion, oath, geas, or standing command within Near ends | One of your own promises ends with it. The Oracle chooses which |
| 5 | **Storm-Coloured Eyes** | Advantage on any check to find another way through, out, or around | Written words swim. You can no longer read |
| 6 | **The Gate in the Chest** | Four gear slots inside you that nothing can find or search | Something else lives in there, and once per expedition it is hungry |
| 7 | **Borrowed Speed** | Once per encounter, take two full turns in one round | Afterwards you have lost an hour and cannot account for it. Others watched you spend it |
| 8 | **The Crooked Wound** | Your critical hits transform the target instead of killing it | You do not choose into what, and it survives |
| 9 | **Poison Grace** | Immunity to poison and disease; your bare touch sickens | Mortal food no longer nourishes you. Something else does |
| 10 | **The Second Shadow** | Once per encounter a duplicate of you acts on your initiative | It remembers what you decided to forget, and it is willing to discuss it |
| 11 | **Chorus** | Speak with any living thing | They all speak to you. Always. Everywhere. Sleep needs a quiet room, and there are none |
| 12 | **The Sword** | You are offered a Black Blade | See below. A Greater Gift: Corruption 2 |

### Law Warrants (d12)

A Warrant is conditional authority. It functions while you keep its Rule. Break the Rule and it is revoked permanently, the breach is recorded, and every servant of Law in the region knows your name inside a week. Accepting a Warrant is Corruption 1 and Law Allegiance +1.

| d12 | Warrant | Grant | Rule |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Kept Word** | No lock, gate, or sworn guard refuses you passage | You have never broken a promise, and still have not |
| 2 | **The True Weight** | You know the exact weight, worth, and provenance of anything you handle | You may not misstate a price |
| 3 | **The Straight Road** | Terrain, weather, and Chaos cannot lengthen your journey | You may not turn aside from the road for any reason, including screaming |
| 4 | **The Fixed Name** | You cannot be transformed, possessed, aged, or shapechanged | You may not change your appearance, name, trade, or allegiance |
| 5 | **The Counted Wound** | Damage from any single blow is capped at 6 | Declare a combat routine, and follow it exactly, in every fight |
| 6 | **The Standing Verdict** | Once per expedition, state a fact aloud; if it is true, all present must act as though it is settled | You may not speak of anything you have not personally verified |
| 7 | **The Muster** | Call up ten disciplined soldiers, once | You lead them, and you account for every one of them afterwards, by name |
| 8 | **The Unchanging Hearth** | A place you name cannot burn, flood, rot, or be entered by the unbound | Nothing new may be built there, and nobody new may settle |
| 9 | **The Clean Cut** | Your weapon severs Chaos: on a hit, one mutation, binding, or transformation ends | You must strike every marked creature you meet. Every one |
| 10 | **The Recording Eye** | Perfect recall of anything you have witnessed, admissible before any servant of Law | You must testify whenever asked |
| 11 | **The Quarantine** | Seal a district; nothing of Chaos crosses the line | Nothing crosses the line. Not food, not physicians, not children |
| 12 | **The Office** | Legitimate authority within a stated remit; Law's institutions obey you inside it | You enforce every decree in force — including the ones you did not write, and the ones that arrive next month |

Warrants 3, 11, and 12 exist to make the point that Law need not be cruel to be unbearable. Run them straight. The Quarantine works.

### Bindings

Both poles will put a thing inside a thing. This is the sorcery of this path, and it is a transaction rather than a spell — no slot, no preparation, no tier. It is available to any character, not only to casters.

**Procedure.** Find a binder, or perform the rite yourself. Roll **1d20 + INT** (Chaos) or **1d20 + WIS** (Law) against **DC 15**. On a success the thing is bound. On a failure it is loose, present, and free. Either way the binder permanently loses **1 point** of an ability score: Chaos takes CON or CHA, Law takes WIS or DEX. The point does not come back.

| Function | Chaos binds | Law binds | Benefit while bound | What it costs, continually |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Combat** | A hunger, into a weapon | A discipline, into a weapon | +1d6 damage; never misses a creature it has already tasted | Chaos: it must taste blood every expedition. Law: it may only be drawn against an enemy named in advance |
| **Protection** | A skin, over yours | A wall, around you | AC 18 against one declared damage type | Chaos: the skin is not yours, and it shows. Law: you may not retreat from ground you have defended |
| **Travel** | Wings, fins, or too many legs | The road itself | Double movement; ignore one terrain type | Chaos: the limbs stay. Law: you may not leave the route |
| **Transport** | A vessel that is alive | A vessel that cannot deviate | Carries the party and its cargo without stopping | Chaos: it eats, and it has preferences. Law: it goes exactly where it was registered to go |
| **Knowledge** | A mouth that answers | An archive that answers | One true answer per day | Chaos: it tells others what you asked. Law: the question is filed beside the answer |
| **Desire** | A glamour | An authority | Advantage on all reaction and negotiation rolls with one named group | Chaos: they want you, and stop wanting anything else. Law: they obey you, and stop deciding |

**When a binding fails (d6):**

| d6 | The loose thing |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Takes the vessel anyway, and now nobody owns it |
| 2 | Takes the binder's face, and leaves for the nearest settlement |
| 3 | Is grateful, is enormous, and follows the party home |
| 4 | Binds itself into the nearest character who has already accepted a Gift or a Warrant |
| 5 | Goes to the opposing pole, and reports |
| 6 | Was already loose. The rite bound something that was watching |

### The Black Blade

The signature object of this path, and the reason Recognition matters. It is not a magic weapon. It is a Lord in iron, currently between owners, and it does the choosing.

**The offer** is made once, aloud, to the Champion. *Gift: what follows. Price: it feeds. Claim: eventually, you.*

**While wielded**

- Damage **1d10**. On a kill, the wielder regains HP equal to the slain creature's level
- A cumulative **+1 to attack rolls** for each life taken during an expedition, to a maximum of +4, resetting at the next sanctuary rest
- The wielder ignores fear, exhaustion, and the effects of one debility
- Once per expedition, ask it a question that one of the people it has drunk could answer. The answer is true. In exchange, the blade chooses the party's next fight

**The hunger.** It must take one life per expedition. If it does not, at the expedition's end it takes **1 point of Constitution** from the wielder, permanently.

**The betrayal.** On a natural 1 on any attack roll, the blade strikes the nearest creature the wielder loves, protects, or has sworn to, using the wielder's full attack and bonuses. This replaces the ordinary fumble result. It is not random malice. It is the blade voting.

**It does not stay put.** Abandoned, sold, buried, or dropped down a shaft, it returns within one expedition, carried by somebody who died bringing it back.

**Ending it.** Four things work, and nothing else does: give it to somebody who wants it more, having told them everything; bind it into an Anchor, which destroys the Anchor and the blade together; return it to the Lord who forged it, in that Lord's presence, at that Lord's price; or die holding it, which merely returns it to circulation.

Accepting the blade is **Corruption 2** and **Chaos Allegiance +2**, and each expedition it is used publicly is +1 more. A party that fields the blade will never again see an encounter scaled to its level.

## The Eternal Champion

### Recognition

At some point in Act I or early Act II, one character is recognised.

| d6 | How it happens |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A mural under whitewash, in a shrine nobody uses, and it is their face |
| 2 | A servant of a Lord stops mid-sentence, bows, and leaves without finishing its errand |
| 3 | An enemy fights them, and says aloud that they always open like that |
| 4 | A child names them correctly, in a language nobody in the region speaks |
| 5 | The whole party share one dream. Only this character remembers being in it |
| 6 | An object made long before their birth fits their hand precisely, and nothing else's |

Recognition is a fact, not a choice, and by itself it confers no power and no obligation. What it does is make that character **worth more than an army** to both poles, because a Champion's allegiance moves the world and a legion's does not. From here on, every offer is aimed at them, and every offer aimed at the others is a route to them.

### Recall

Once per expedition the Champion may ask the Oracle one question about the current situation **as it stood in another cycle**. Resolve on the [Binary Oracle](../oracles/01_core_oracle.md). The answer is always true — of that other cycle.

Then roll **d6 for resemblance, and show the result**:

| d6 | How much that cycle resembles this one |
|---:|---|
| 1–2 | Barely. Same players, different game |
| 3–4 | In its shape, not its details |
| 5–6 | Closely enough to act on |

The players are told the number. Deciding what a true answer about another world is worth in this one is the mechanic; hiding the resemblance would make it a trap instead of a decision.

### The Beloved and the Doom

At Recognition, the player names a **Beloved**: a person, a place, an oath, or a thing that is not a weapon. Write it down.

The Doom is that decisive help, from either pole, is paid for out of the Beloved. Whenever the party ask a Lord for direct aid, call for aid at the Marked threshold, or accept an intervention that solves something they could not solve themselves, the price is named first, from this ladder — and they may always decline and go without.

| Step | The price |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The Beloved is changed in a way that is not obviously worse, and is not reversible |
| 2 | The Beloved becomes known to both poles, and is now a target |
| 3 | The Beloved is harmed in a way the party could have prevented, had they been there |
| 4 | The Beloved is offered a Gift or a Warrant, and takes it |
| 5 | The Beloved is lost |

If the Beloved is lost entirely, the Champion gains a permanent and genuine benefit — advantage on all saves against fear, domination, and despair — and permanently loses the ability to hold an Anchor. That is the tragedy written as arithmetic: it makes them stronger, and it removes them from the only work that matters.

### The Companion

Any other player character may declare themselves the Companion. It is a standing declaration made in the fiction, not a class feature.

- Once per expedition the Companion may spend their action to cancel one Doom price, taking it themselves at one step lower
- The Companion may carry and hold an Anchor on the Champion's behalf, if their own Sum allows it
- If the Champion dies, the Companion is the next Recognition. The cycle does not stop for a funeral
- Only the Companion can carry out the **Break the Recurrence** ending, because the Champion cannot end themselves

## The Anchors

The party's win condition is not the mirror of either pole's. Neither pole needs the whole region; each needs only enough of it. The party need **something that belongs to neither**, and it cannot be bought with anything either pole supplies.

Five Anchors. Each has an acquisition, and each has a way to be **stained**.

| Anchor | What it is | How it is obtained | What stains it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Unclaimed Ground** | A place where neither pole has performed a rite and no oath has been sworn — a ford, a threshing floor, a crossroads | Find it, then make both poles' local servants walk away from it, usually by making it worth nothing to them | Any aligned power used on that ground, ever, by anyone |
| **The One Who Refused** | A person offered by both poles who took neither, and is still alive | Find them, protect them without using aligned power, and persuade them to come willingly | Coercion; payment from an aligned faction; being saved by a Gift or a Warrant |
| **The Held Oath** | An agreement between two parties in real conflict, kept with no enforcement behind it | Broker the Act I Quarrel to a settlement using no Warrant, no glamour, and no threat backed by a pole | Either party having been compelled, glamoured, registered, or frightened into it |
| **The Made Thing** | An object made by hand by a named maker, containing at least one honest error, still doing its work | Commission it, or buy it fairly. The maker must know what it is for, and agree | Magical repair; improvement by either pole; payment in aligned coin |
| **The True Account** | A record of what actually happened in the Quarrel, attested by witnesses from both sides | Collect testimony, cross-check it, and get both sides to sign the same page | Testimony compelled by a Warrant; a memory altered by a Gift; a copy deposited in a Law archive "for safety" |

A stained Anchor still works. It simply comes with a receipt: **each Stain gives the corresponding pole one Claim at the Weighing.**

| Total Stains | What remains possible |
|---:|---|
| **0** | Every finale route is open, including a Third Principle |
| **1–2** | Every route except Third Principle, which needs an unstained set |
| **3–4** | Consecration is possible but contested; expect to answer every Claim |
| **5–6** | Consecration requires a concession: one Anchor, one character, or one permanent limit on the sanctuary |
| **7+** | The poles are no longer arguing about whether they own it. They are arguing about which of them does |

## Acts, Counted in Names

This engine's acts are not level bands and not calendar time. They are **attrition of the middle**. Levels follow the count; the count does not follow the levels.

| Act | Middle remaining | Levels | What is true |
|---|:---:|:---:|---|
| **I — The Quarrel** | 12 → 9 | 1–3 | No pole is present. The dispute is entirely mortal, and entirely soluble |
| **II — The Matching** | 8 → 4 | 4–7 | Both poles are present and answering each other. This is where the list collapses fastest |
| **III — The Weighing** | 3 → 0 | 8–10 | Open war between the hosts. The party hold a site and answer Claims |

Budget content to the length using the act budget table in [Adventure Path Engines](../oracles/07_adventure_path_engines.md). For this path the **standing problems** carry the weight: brokering the Quarrel, keeping an Anchor clean while fighting, deciding which character is allowed to be the one who holds it, and choosing which of the twelve names to spend when spending one is the only way through.

# Act I: The Quarrel — Levels 1–3

A mortal dispute with two defensible sides. No Lord appears, no Gift is offered by anything inhuman, and the party can end the whole thing with talk, work, money, or violence. The act closes the first time aligned power is used in the region **by anyone** — including an enemy, including the party, including the Petitioners who invited it.

Whatever else happens in Act I, the **Held Oath** and the **True Account** are only obtainable here, while the quarrel is still made of people.

## Quarrel A: Ferry-and-Ford

A river town on a crossing, and two claimants to it. Both have documents. Both are decent. Both are wrong about one thing, and the town has begun taking sides by street.

**Factions:** the elder heir and her assize petition, the younger heir and his friends from downriver, the Ferrymen who work the crossing and want neither, the barge trade, and a parish priest who is quietly recording everything.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A boundary stone has been moved. Both sides can prove it. Both are telling the truth: it has been moved twice. |
| 2 | A travelling judge offers the party a Warrant so that they may settle the case themselves. It would settle it perfectly, and permanently. |
| 3 | A ferryman refuses coin from either claimant and is beaten for it in the yard. He is a candidate for the One Who Refused. |
| 4 | Two witnesses to the same drowning describe different weather. Neither is lying. |
| 5 | An armoured stranger asks only who currently holds the ford, writes the answer down, and leaves without eating. |
| 6 | A grain barge sinks in the channel. Whichever claimant feeds the town first is owed the town. |

## Quarrel B: The Marches of Two Peoples

A settled valley and a herding people, old claims on both sides, and a drought pushing them into the same water.

**Factions:** the valley reeve and his proposed register, the herd-chiefs and their obligations to each other, a shrine used by both, raiders who are nobody's, and the families with a foot in each camp.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Herd beasts are being born wearing their parents' faces. The valley calls it a curse and the herders call it a blessing, and both are correct. |
| 2 | The reeve proposes a register of every animal and every person in the march. It would end the raiding within a year. |
| 3 | A raid goes wrong and children are taken. They can be returned by force, by trade, or by asking, and the three routes make different enemies. |
| 4 | Somebody has been burning hayricks on both sides and leaving a mark of eight arrows. He is fourteen, and from the valley. |
| 5 | A priest passing through offers to consecrate the shared shrine properly. The herders would still be welcome. They would be welcome as guests. |
| 6 | The drought breaks in a single night, over one side of the valley only. |

## Quarrel C: The Fever and the Two Cures

A city with a wasting sickness and two remedies on offer. One fixes the body exactly as it was, and fixes everything else about it: no illness, no growth, no children, no healing of old wounds. The other remakes the body, and the survivors are healthy, thriving, and not quite recognisable.

**Factions:** the physicians' college, the changed and their families, a quarantine faction on the council, the river district that will be sealed, and a merchant who sells both.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A physician has used both and kept records. Both work. She cannot publish either without choosing a side. |
| 2 | A household is split: the changed son is healthy and unrecognisable, the fixed daughter is exactly as she was in March and always will be. |
| 3 | A quarantine is proposed. It will hold the fever, and it will starve the river district. |
| 4 | The draught comes from somewhere. The source is a person, and she is willing. |
| 5 | A crowd demands the party choose publicly. Whatever they choose, a Middle name is struck. |
| 6 | The merchant selling both is not lying about either, and is the only reason anybody survived the winter. |

# Act II: The Matching — Levels 4–7

Both poles are present, answering each other, and buying mortals. This is where the Symmetry Rule is learned by being burned by it, where Recognition lands, where the Black Blade finds a hand, and where the list collapses fastest.

Act II must present at least **three sources of Anchors** from different quarters, at least one of which requires no aligned faction's goodwill. It must also stage one **set-piece event** the party cannot attend and cannot stop: a muster, a consecration, a mass changing, an assize.

## Theatre A: The Opal Court

An ancient, sorcerous, beautiful city that has belonged to Chaos for a thousand years, and is bored. Bindings, blades, and audiences with Lords are all purchasable here at posted prices. Nothing in it is hidden, and that is what makes it dangerous.

**Factions:** the ruling houses, the binders' street, a slave market whose goods have been improved, a Law embassy with a treaty, and the harbour, which has an arrangement with the sea.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The market's goods have been improved and prefer their new forms. Abolition is contested by the abolished. |
| 2 | A binder will sell any binding at the standard price — one point of yourself — and his price list is honest and nailed to the door. |
| 3 | A ruling house invites the party to dinner to look at the Champion. They will pay well for the look. |
| 4 | The harbour's agreement with the sea is being renegotiated, and the sea has sent somebody. |
| 5 | The Law embassy's treaty is read aloud in council. Every clause is beneficial. Signing ends the city. |
| 6 | A Black Blade is in a case in the third gallery, and it noticed the party at the door. |

## Theatre B: The Ruled Province

Law's model: clean, fed, defended, and quietly unbearable. Nothing here is done out of cruelty and everything here works.

**Factions:** the registry, the assize, the sealed eastern quarter, a garrison that has never lost, and an archivist who wants to help.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Every child in the district holds one of eleven approved names, and the registry can find any person alive within the hour. |
| 2 | A plague was stopped here. The method is on the wall. It cost the eastern quarter, which is still standing, still sealed, and still occupied. |
| 3 | The assize will hear the party's case and settle it correctly, if they swear. |
| 4 | A man is condemned for changing trades. His guild agrees with the verdict, and so does he. |
| 5 | Chaos raiders are coming. The province's defence is flawless and requires every person to be exactly where the register says they are. |
| 6 | An archivist offers to keep a copy of the True Account safe. Accepting stains it. |

## Theatre C: The Unmade Marches

Land that Chaos has already crossed. Terrain, weather, and biology are all negotiable. The people who live here are not monsters; they have adapted, and several of the adaptations are enviable.

**Factions:** villages that use local impossibilities as tools, a Law expedition fixing the land square mile by square mile, herds that were people, and something enormous that is dying slowly.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A village where the road's length depends on the traveller's intentions. The villagers use it as a lock, and it has never failed. |
| 2 | Something enormous is dying, and its dreams are the local weather. |
| 3 | A Law expedition is fixing the land in squares. The inhabitants are inside the squares. |
| 4 | A herd that were people last year. Some of them still keep appointments. |
| 5 | A spring cures anything, and changes what it cured into something that cannot be cured again. |
| 6 | The party's own maps stop agreeing with the ground. A carried Made Thing still measures true, and everybody notices who is carrying it. |

# Act III: The Weighing — Levels 8–10

Both hosts are in the field. Ordinary life in the region has stopped, and what remains of the Middle is being spent by both sides as fast as they can reach it. The party hold a site, complete what Anchors they can, and answer Claims.

## Ground A: The Found City

A city that cannot be sought and can only be arrived at: it appears to those who have stopped looking for a side. It is real, it is inhabited, and it exists exactly as long as unclaimed things exist to sustain it. Both hosts are outside it, and neither can enter while it holds.

**Required:** at least three Anchors present inside the walls. If the Anchor count falls below three, the city begins to be findable — by everyone.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A refugee column arrives. Half of them are Marked, and admitting them costs the city a wall. |
| 2 | An envoy from each host arrives on the same afternoon with the same offer, phrased in opposite words. |
| 3 | Somebody inside is quietly performing a rite, believing it will protect the place. |
| 4 | The city's water comes from outside, and the host camped over the spring is being polite about it. |
| 5 | A Lord walks up to the gate in person, unarmed, and asks to be admitted as a guest. |
| 6 | The One Who Refused is recognised by a soldier from their old life, who is loyal, and appalled, and about to write a letter. |

## Ground B: The Field Between Hosts

Two cosmic armies on a plain, and everything mortal caught between them: refugee roads, the last unaligned villages, a hospital, and the party's site in the middle of the corridor both hosts want.

**Required:** movement. Nothing here can be held by standing still.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | A village on the corridor is offered evacuation by both sides, on conditions, and has an afternoon to choose. |
| 2 | A supply train belonging to neither host is being taken by both. |
| 3 | Both hosts declare a truce to bury their dead, and the truce is real, and it lasts one day. |
| 4 | A champion of one host offers the party single combat on stated terms, in front of witnesses from both. |
| 5 | A breach opens in the line and the fastest route to an Anchor runs straight through the fighting. |
| 6 | The hospital is shelled by nobody. Both hosts investigate, and both conclude the other did it, correctly. |

## Ground C: The Scale

A place where the Balance is physically present and visibly tilting: a mountain, a machine, a tree, a courtyard with an actual pair of scales that nobody built. Holding it aligns you. Touching it aligns you. It is the most valuable object in the region and the worst possible prize.

**Required:** a decision about who, if anyone, is permitted to touch it.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---:|---|
| 1 | The tilt can be corrected by hand. The correction lasts as long as somebody stands there holding it. |
| 2 | Both hosts have sent their most reasonable representatives, and the two of them have been talking for three days. |
| 3 | A previous Champion's remains are here, still gripping the mechanism. |
| 4 | The Scale answers questions about the future, and every answer it gives moves it. |
| 5 | A faction of mortals guards it and has done for six generations. They are exhausted, and they want to hand it over. |
| 6 | Somebody has worked out how to break it, and is not wrong that breaking it would end the war. |

## The Weighing

At the end, both poles arrive to claim the site. This does not begin as a battle. It begins as a **claim**, because both poles believe they are entitled and both are partly right.

**Procedure**

1. **Count Claims.** Each pole may make one Claim per Stain on the Anchors, plus one Claim for each Middle name it struck itself, to a maximum of six each.
2. **Answer Claims.** Each Claim must be answered individually. A Claim is answered by one of: an unstained Anchor; testimony from the Held Oath's two parties; an Elemental Ruler's sponsorship; a publicly renounced Gift or Warrant; the Champion's refusal spoken in front of witnesses from both hosts; or a mortal witness of standing who will say what they saw.
3. **Concede what cannot be answered.** Each unanswered Claim takes one thing: an Anchor, a character's freedom, or one permanent limit written into the sanctuary — a gate that must stay open, a name that must be registered, a rite that must be permitted annually.
4. **Then, and only then**, does it become a fight, a duel, a bargain, or a founding.

### Routes

| Route | Requirement | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Consecrate the Middle** | Four Anchors present, clean or answered, and one witness from each host willing to say aloud that the site belongs to neither | The sanctuary exists. Both poles may enter it and neither may act within it. The region has a middle again, and it is the only place either pole can meet the other |
| **Tilt Deliberately** | Leverage: an Anchor either pole wants more than the win, or a Champion willing to be spent | One pole takes the region on the party's written conditions, and the conditions are honoured. The Toll stands. This is a real ending, not a defeat |
| **Introduce a Third Principle** | All five Anchors unstained, an Elemental or Beast Ruler's sponsorship earned by reciprocity, and the Champion refusing both poles at the Weighing itself | The players name the new principle, and it must be something neither pole can claim: growth, death, craft, kinship, memory. The cycle now has three terms, and nobody living knows what that does |
| **Break the Recurrence** | The Companion acts, the Champion consents, and the site holds through the Weighing | The cycle ends. There will be no further Champions. There will also be no further inevitability: the next crisis has nobody chosen to solve it, and nobody fated to lose |
| **Take the Role** | One character accepts a Called offer at the Weighing | Corruption 4, Claimed. That character is lost to play, and the region may well be saved by it. This is available to any character and is never required |
| **Hand it to the Hosts** | Do nothing, or arrive too late | The region becomes an absolute. See below |

### End states

| Result | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Sanctuary consecrated, Toll light | A neutral city exists, and both poles send envoys to it. The party administer neutrality, which is a full-time occupation |
| Sanctuary consecrated, Toll heavy | The sanctuary stands in a region that has nothing left to shelter. It becomes a destination for refugees from both sides, and both sides notice |
| Deliberate tilt to Law | Order, permanence, safety, and a province that will never produce anything new. The written conditions hold for exactly as long as the officials who signed them |
| Deliberate tilt to Chaos | Freedom, invention, and a region in which nothing can be relied upon twice. Those who thrive, thrive |
| Third Principle introduced | A cosmology with three terms, one of them mortal in origin. Both poles will spend a generation working out how to attack it |
| Recurrence broken | No Champion, no prophecy, and no help. Every subsequent crisis is solved by ordinary people or not at all |
| A character takes the Role | The region is saved and the party is one member short, permanently, and that member sends letters |
| The Middle reaches zero | The region becomes an absolute. Play continues inside it: resistance, exile, collaboration, emigration, or service. The next Adventure Path is built on the transformed region, one tier up |

## Ignoring, Failing, Winning Early

**Ignoring.** This path is unusually kind to a party who walk away, and that is deliberate. Both poles pay excellently for neutral contractors, and neither has any interest in harming somebody who is useful to both. Each neglect tick — measured in expeditions spent elsewhere, never in elapsed time — strikes one Middle name and produces a genuine offer: a vacated holding, a cheap charter, a grateful faction with a treasury. The party who ignore this path should get rich. That is the choice, and it must be a real one. Three consecutive ticks converts the path into a **Background Transformation**: the region becomes an absolute offscreen, and that is simply the setting now.

**Failing.** If a pole wins, the region is theirs and play continues inside it. Losing to Law and losing to Chaos produce two entirely different survivable campaigns — one is smuggling, forgery, and false papers; the other is triage, adaptation, and the constant renegotiation of what your friends currently are. Both are worth playing.

**Winning early.** Consecrating a sanctuary in Act II earns **Inheritance**, not a shorter campaign. The party now hold a neutral site that both poles want, cannot enter, and will spend the remaining acts undermining — through refugees, through envoys, through the party's own Allegiance bars, and through the entirely sincere argument that the sanctuary is prolonging a war that one clean victory would end.

## The Shape of Play

A compressed arc, to show how the parts turn each other.

**Levels 1–3.** The party are hired over a ferry dispute. They investigate, find a moved boundary stone, find that it was moved twice, and broker a settlement between the two heirs over three sessions of talking, one warehouse fire, and one fight with river thugs who belong to nobody. The settlement holds. Nobody has met a Lord. The party have the **Held Oath**, clean, and do not yet know it is worth anything. Twelve names in the Middle; twelve still.

**Levels 4–5.** The younger heir's friends downriver turn out to have promised something in exchange for their help, and a judge arrives at Marrowgate carrying a Warrant. The Grey Assize accepts it — an entirely reasonable decision that ends the bribery it had suffered for a century. *Eleven left.* The party, hunting the last of the river thugs, are offered the Clean Cut and take it, and use it in the village square, and are cheered. Four days later something wearing the reeve's face walks into the same square. They win that fight. *Nine left.*

**Levels 6–7.** A mural under whitewash in the Ford Shrine has the ranger's face on it. A binder in the Opal Court offers everything at a posted price. The party's fighter, who by now is Marked by Law and cannot be talked to by farmers, works out that the ranger cannot carry the True Account any more, and that the only clean pair of hands left belongs to their hired torchbearer. That realisation is the campaign turning. The blade is offered to the ranger in the third gallery. The table argues about it for an hour.

**Levels 8–10.** Both hosts are in the field. The party spend two expeditions doing nothing but earning a river's reciprocity so that they can bring a sponsor to the Weighing who costs them nothing. The torchbearer carries four Anchors into a city that cannot be found. At the Weighing, Law makes three Claims and Chaos makes two, and the party answer four of them, and concede the fifth: a gate that must stay open. The sanctuary stands with a gate that must stay open. Seven names were struck getting there, and the list is read aloud at the end, because that is the ending.

## Design Sources

Named for implementers, in the same spirit as the boss library's reference column.

The cosmology, the doomed champion, the sword, and the shrinking neutral ground are Michael Moorcock's — the Elric, Corum, and Hawkmoon sequences, and the *Eternal Champion* frame that links them.

Four mechanics are adapted from Chaosium's *Stormbringer* / *Elric!* line, which remains the best-designed treatment of this material at a table:

| Borrowed | Original shape | ASH form here |
|---|---|---|
| **Allegiance** | Points accrued to Law, Chaos, or the Balance by deeds, with thresholds that make a character a servant of a power | The two Allegiance bars, the Called threshold, and the **Sum**, which is this path's own addition: the bars added together measure how much middle is left in the person |
| **Demon binding** | Sorcery is summoning and binding rather than a spell list; the summoner permanently spends POW to bind an entity into an object | The Bindings table: a transaction with a DC, a permanent ability-score cost, and a continuing price, available to any character rather than only casters |
| **Chaos gifts and features** | Servants of Chaos accrue mutations and boons together, rolled from a table | The Chaos Gifts table, paired with the Law Warrants table so that both poles arm mortals in their own idiom |
| **The runeblade** | A soul-drinking weapon that strengthens its wielder, must be fed, and sometimes strikes the wielder's friends | The Black Blade: the hunger, the natural-1 betrayal, the stolen dead answering questions, and the four ways to be rid of it |

Two deliberate departures from the source system:

- **Allegiance never removes agency.** Stormbringer will let a power take a character. Here a pole can only ever be accepted, in a stated offer, by a player who said yes — consistent with the [Corruption rules](01_domains_of_dread.md). What refusal costs is paid by the world, not by the refuser.
- **Neutrality is a mechanic rather than a third alignment.** The party do not win by scoring Balance points. They win by assembling five things that no power has touched, which is why the Sum matters and why the strongest character usually cannot be the one to carry them.
