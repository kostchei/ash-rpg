# Campaign Pressure & Escalation

> **"The world moves for reasons, not because every campaign owns the same clock."**

Campaign pressure is **optional**. A new campaign starts with no global pressure unless its premise already contains an active pursuit, deadline, rival, spreading danger, or closing opportunity. Add a track only when a force can change the situation without waiting for the party.

Pressure is not punishment for taking time to explore. Advance it only when a stated fictional trigger occurs.

---

## 🧭 Anatomy of a Pressure

Write five things before adding a track:

1. **Moving Force:** Who or what is acting?
2. **Shape:** How does its movement feel in play?
3. **Steps:** How much room exists before a decisive change? Usually 4–8.
4. **Triggers:** What specific events advance or reduce it?
5. **Consequence:** What changes when the final step is reached?

The consequence should change the situation rather than end the campaign. The riders catch the party, a rival claims the relic, suspicion becomes open hostility, or the plague reaches Sanctuary. The players still decide what happens next.

---

## 🧩 Pressure Shapes

| Shape                  | Use When                                                     | Progress Means                             | Example Consequence                              |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Pursuit**            | Someone is chasing or tracking the party                     | The pursuer closes distance                | The Ash Riders reach the party's camp            |
| **Rival Race**         | Another group wants the same objective                       | The rival completes milestones             | The rival expedition claims the star-metal first |
| **Faction Heat**       | Attention, suspicion, or retaliation accumulates             | A faction recognizes and targets the party | The guild posts names and bounties at every gate |
| **Spreading Crisis**   | Fire, plague, corruption, war, or panic moves geographically | More people or places are affected         | The blight reaches Sanctuary's wells             |
| **Revelation**         | Evidence is changing the campaign's understood truth         | Clues make one explanation unavoidable     | The patron is exposed as the hidden conspirator  |
| **Opportunity Window** | Favorable circumstances will not last                        | Access or advantage is being lost          | Winter closes the mountain pass                  |
| **Escalation Ladder**  | Consequences intensify in distinct stages                    | The opposition changes tactics             | Spies become raids; raids become siege           |
| **Countdown**          | A real, known deadline exists                                | Time or required events run out            | The eclipse opens the sealed gate                |

A **Doom Clock** is simply a countdown with catastrophic framing. Use it when that is genuinely the campaign premise, not as a default rule.

---

## ⚙️ Advancement Procedure

At the end of a scene, watch, or session, ask:

1. Did a written trigger occur?
2. Did the party's actions accelerate, delay, redirect, or resolve the moving force?
3. Has the fiction created a new pressure that deserves its own track?

Advance one step for a normal trigger and two only for a major irreversible turn. Reduce a track when the party earns distance, misdirects the opposition, repairs the damage, or seizes initiative. Resolve and archive it when the moving force no longer applies.

Do not advance a track merely because the party rested, explored a side path, or spent table time roleplaying unless that passage of time was already a stated trigger.

## 👁️ Optional Pattern: The Quantum Nemesis

The former three-vector Arch-Nemesis procedure remains available for campaigns built around an unknown mastermind:

1. Write three plausible explanations for the campaign's recurring evidence.
2. When a major discovery occurs, use context or the Oracle to associate it with one candidate.
3. After three strong clues support one candidate, treat that explanation as true and reinterpret the others as factions, mistakes, or secondary threats.

This is a **Revelation** pattern, not a required villain structure. A campaign may instead concern exploration, survival, rivalry, politics, a chase, episodic expeditions, or problems with no single mastermind at all.
