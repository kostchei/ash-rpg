# Adventure Path Engines

> **An antagonist is the face of an Adventure Path. Its engine is the process by which it changes the world.**

Adventure Paths sit above ordinary [Campaign Pressures](05_emergent_campaign_engine.md). A pressure describes one force currently moving: a garrison occupying a road, a plague crossing a river, or a cult collecting a relic. An Adventure Path explains why those pressures recur, what persistent state their consequences change, and how the campaign enters a new phase.

An Adventure Path is therefore not a single Doom Clock. It consists of:

1. **Antagonist:** The power, faction, condition, or conflict that gives the path a recognizable face.
2. **World Process:** The characteristic verb by which it advances.
3. **Persistent Footprint:** The people, places, resources, or relationships changed by its progress.
4. **Recurring Counterplay:** The kinds of expeditions and decisions the process repeatedly creates.
5. **Transformation:** The possible states of the world after the path is resolved, redirected, or allowed to prevail.

The following engines are working foundations for ASH Adventure Paths. Each describes a different way for the world to progress, not merely a different final enemy.

**Expanded procedures:** [Populating Adventure Path Engines](08_adventure_path_population.md) supplies generation order, three acts, at least two antagonist options, concrete requirements, opening situations, and outcomes for every engine below. These are authored design procedures; runtime eligibility must be verified separately.

## Antagonists, Methods, and Three Acts

Every engine supports a **three-act campaign across levels 1–10**. Acts use the engine's own units, with approximate content bands of levels 1–3, 4–7, and 8–10. Restoration states, legal steps, repeated manifestations, and arrival waves are developments within those three acts, not additional acts. An earned early victory remains possible.

Choose the antagonist separately from the engine's method. Each engine has at least two options in the [population procedures](08_adventure_path_population.md#three-acts-multiple-antagonists), with different motives, dependencies, and means of opposition. Domains of Dread supplies many boss dossiers; displacement can concern Githyanki refugees, an orc portal migration in the shape of the Warcraft arrival scenario, or a resident ruler exploiting either. Invocation can serve fire, Nurgle-inspired plague, Orcus or other death cults, or magic and change.

The nine outer-power entries are **methods**, not exclusive assignments of one method to one god. Mandates, participation, conjunction, proliferation, and the other processes can serve different patrons. Choose one primary method to govern progression and the ending. A supporting method explains a dependency or operation; it does not add an unrelated second campaign clock.

The new [Slumbering Catastrophe](#12-the-slumbering-catastrophe-preparation-and-awakening) covers an ancient red dragon, tarrasque, or other dormant destroyer: discover what wakes it, gain experience and effective weapons, and attempt the kill before full awakening and rampage.

## Advancement: Asymmetry First

The model for path advancement is *Cthulhu Wars*, in which each faction earns its powers by completing a list of requirements written in its own idiom. The Black Goat spawns, the Yellow Sign captures, Cthulhu devours. No two lists are interchangeable, and no faction is playing the same game as its opponents.

Adventure Paths work the same way. Four consequences follow, and they matter more than any shared vocabulary.

**1. Each antagonist has its own Requirements.** Usually four to eight conditions, written in that antagonist's verbs, each granting one specific capability. Complete them in any order their physical or procedural prerequisites permit; the chosen order shapes what the party eventually faces. The Titans' Release Deeds are the model. The [population procedures](08_adventure_path_population.md) supply concrete requirements where the summaries below give only an outline. Completion remains in history even if a later deed disables its capability; distinguish an accomplished deed from a currently functioning power.

**2. Each antagonist has its own win condition, and it is rarely "complete the list".** Tharizdun needs three Bonds broken in one region, not all eight. The Compact needs only to be the sole provider. The Witch King needs bodies and infrastructure and never needs to win a battle. Write the specific state of the world that ends the path in the antagonist's favour, and write how far short of the full list that state can be reached.

**3. The party's win condition is not the mirror of it.** This is what makes a path a story rather than a tug of war. The party do not win by preventing every Requirement, and preventing all of them is usually not a victory at all. They win by achieving their own condition, which is a different kind of thing: assembling a circumstance, providing a service, obtaining a verdict, restoring one distinction, making arrival permanently expensive. In several engines the party can allow the antagonist most of its list and still win; in several others they can deny the entire list and still be nowhere.

Write both conditions on the same page, in different columns, and let the players see that the two columns do not describe the same contest.

**4. Each path must offer more than one independently sufficient player success condition.** This is an authoring requirement for new paths and an audit requirement for existing ones, not a claim that every current dossier already satisfies it. Write at least two executable ways to resolve the central problem, each with discoverable evidence, feasible access, an actual completion event, and a distinct consequence. They are alternatives joined by **OR**, not mandatory components joined by AND. Different weapons for the same compulsory boss kill do not meet this requirement. A valid early success remains a success; later acts can concern its inheritance. [The Stolen Dawn](../adventure_paths/04_the_stolen_dawn.md) develops three independent victories: defeat the caster, permanently disable renewal, or establish a counteracting power.

### Two Things Are Universal

Everything else varies by engine. These two do not.

**The Toll.** A list, never a number, of what the path has already cost permanently: places burned, people spent, oaths taken, sacrifices made to repair something. The Toll never decreases, no engine is exempt from it, and it is what stops a won campaign from feeling free. Several engines make the Toll gate which endings remain available.

**The way progress is spoken.** Any measure a path uses needs three expressions:

- **Ledger** — the written line on the [Campaign Pressure page](../campaign_record/threat_vectors.md). Names first, counts second. "The mill, the ford, and the watch answer the Prefect", not "Ascendancy 3".
- **Tell** — what a character perceives without being told a number. The price of bread. Whether the road behaves. Whether the dead stay where they were put. Whether strangers meet your eye. A good Tell lets the party read the ledger by walking through a town.
- **Reading** — the sentence spoken when something moves, in three clauses: **the count, a name, and what is now possible or foreclosed.**

> "Six services in Compact hands, four of them in Briar Watch. The clinic at the Delta is yours. Briar Watch will not talk to you while their physician is theirs."

A measure the players cannot read is a Doom Clock in costume. Conceal one only where concealment *is* the engine, and then track the visible shadow instead: disappearances rather than captives, unverified allies rather than thralls.

### Acts in the Engine's Own Units

A path is built for ten levels and exactly three campaign acts, but transitions follow whatever the engine actually runs on. Use the approximate level bands to budget content and threat, not to postpone a fulfilled condition or force a missing event.

| Act shape           | Counted in                          | Engines that use it                          |
| :------------------ | :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| **Knowledge gate**  | What the party have proven          | Captive Domain, Nyarlathotep                 |
| **Calendar**        | Winters, thaws, harvests            | Ithaqua                                      |
| **Cycle**           | Manifestations survived             | Cthulhu                                      |
| **Legal phase**     | Decrees in force; rungs of a ladder | Bane, Angels                                 |
| **Cascade**         | Nodes lost to a spreading graph     | Tharizdun, Shub-Niggurath, Witch King        |
| **Assembly**        | Components gathered                 | Ragnaros, Titans, Yog-Sothoth                |
| **Wave**            | Arrivals, emergences, doublings     | Githyanki, Tsathoggua, Marut                 |
| **Saturation**      | Share of a population reached       | Hastur, Aboleth Savant, Tcho-Tcho, Azathoth  |
| **Attrition**       | Names left in the middle            | Eternal Cycle                                |
| **Preparation race** | Waking deeds and acquired countermeasures | Slumbering Catastrophe                    |

Whatever the units, budget content for the length. A ten-level path is not three big dungeons.

Each act now requires a different persistent zone, optionally chosen randomly from compatible unused zones. The [XP mathematics and site-generation plan](../plans/path_sites_xp_and_zones.md) governs reward sufficiency and generation across every engine. The site counts below are initial content estimates, not evidence that a path supplies enough XP; audit reachable branches, story bonuses, reset losses, and progression through campaign success. Level 9 at the final boss is acceptable, with finale rewards able to contribute toward level 10.

| Act | Sites | Set-piece events | Standing problems |
| :-- | :---- | :--------------- | :---------------- |
| I   | 6–8   | 3                | 2–3               |
| II  | 10–12 | 5                | 4–5               |
| III | 6–8   | 4                | 3–4               |

A **site** is somewhere the party go and return from with the ledger changed. A **set-piece event** happens to the region whether or not they attend: a levy, a flood, a trial, an offensive, a hanging. A **standing problem** cannot be solved by arriving anywhere—a sequencing puzzle, a triage, an identification, a substitution, a restitution, an infiltration. Standing problems are what keep nine levels from becoming nine levels of clearing rooms.

### Ignoring, Failing, Winning Early

Each engine states its own version of these, because the antagonists do not react to neglect in the same way at all. Some accelerate geometrically when unopposed; some barely move, because they were built entirely out of reactions; one or two are actively made worse by being fought. The general principles:

**Ignoring.** Use each engine's actual triggers. Seasons and scheduled arrival waves follow their committed calendars; reactive operations need the triggering deed; the Sleeper's concealed accumulation does not produce generic warnings or compulsory offers. Otherwise, neglect means passing over an actionable opportunity: an expedition, a refused summons, or an abandoned intervention. An actor may then complete the cheapest eligible Requirement only if its resources, access, and prerequisites permit it. Consequences can create offers such as paid work, abandoned strongpoints, or scarce services. Three consecutive ignored opportunities may produce a **Background Transformation** if the engine's actual winning conditions can now be met. They do not grant missing resources or bypass an unbroken seal. The changed region can return in later play with that history intact.

**Failing.** Losing ground re-frames a path; it does not end one. When the antagonist's win condition is actually met, the world is theirs and play continues inside it: resistance, exile, survival, emigration, collaboration, service. The next engine is built on the transformed region, usually one tier up. A campaign that can survive its own defeat is worth more than one that was never able to lose.

**Winning early.** A party who meet their condition in Act I or II should not receive a shorter campaign as a prize. Two answers, and each engine names which one fits: **Overreach**, in which the antagonist's patron, rival, creditor, or heir takes an interest and the path continues at a higher altitude; or **Inheritance**, in which the party now hold what they broke—the domain, the relay network, the throne, the clinics the cult used to run—and the remaining acts are about holding it without becoming it. The Toll is still owed either way.

## Asymmetric Properties

In an asymmetric game every faction has its own board, its own economy, and its own turn. It also has something less obvious: **a problem of its own.** Cthulhu is unkillable and enormously expensive to bring anywhere. The Dragon is immensely strong and starts asleep. The Goblins are endless and cannot feed themselves.

That pairing is the whole design. A goblin warren with an overpopulation mechanic is not a horde of monsters; it is a population under pressure that must vent every season or starve. Which means a party who destroy a war-band every autumn are not defeating the warren. They are running its culling programme, for free, and the warren is healthier every spring for their help.

**Give every antagonist a power, a suffering, and an obvious move that fails.** The power makes it dangerous. The suffering is where the party get purchase. The failing obvious move is what the first act is for.

| Engine | Plays by rules nobody else has | Suffers from | The obvious move that fails |
| :----- | :----------------------------- | :----------- | :-------------------------- |
| **1. Captive Domain** | Rewrites the land's rules; cannot die without the Killing Condition | It is bound by its own laws, and cannot leave the realm it made | Looking for the way out; there isn't one |
| **2. Aboleth Savant** | Perfect inherited knowledge; domination that leaves no seam | Acts only through held minds, each of which is a thread back to it; cannot model a person choosing badly on purpose | Killing suspected thralls — you are killing victims and blinding yourself |
| **3. Titans** | The world is physically made from their bodies | Each is missing pieces of itself and can act only through servants until restored; they do not coordinate | Siding with the gods, who committed the original crime |
| **4. Tharizdun** | Not an agent; erodes the categories reality runs on | Cannot act at all — every advance needs mortals to do the work | Killing cultists; the dissolution spreads without them once begun |
| **5. Bane** | Legitimacy; the post outlives the officeholder | Needs visible disorder to justify itself, and must keep the province fed to keep compliance | Assassination and sabotage, which supply the disorder |
| **6A. Cthulhu** | Returns without losing completed preparations | Must meet actual re-entry conditions; contact can expose its routes | Treating a useful banishment as permanent exclusion |
| **6B. Nyarlathotep** | Many masks exploit real endorsements and divided institutions | Needs specific authority and voluntary acceptance; evidence can revoke it | Lending authority without checking the beneficiary and scope |
| **6C. Shub-Niggurath** | Spawn faster than any force can kill | Overpopulation: must expand or starve, and isolated broods die on their own | Clearing sites, which vents the pressure and relieves the network |
| **6D. Hastur** | Spreads through desire; people volunteer | Needs an audience and cannot compel anyone | Suppression. A banned work is an advertised work |
| **6E. Yog-Sothoth** | Distance and sequence stop applying | The geometry has a build order and is fragile to being built wrong | Closing the nearest gate, which redistributes power to the rest |
| **6F. Tsathoggua** | Preserves reserves and prepares interruptions | Needs actual stores, conduits, and enforceable tribute; responses can be exhausted | Taking convenient rest without examining its obligations |
| **6G. Ithaqua** | Makes expeditions costly and spends stored winter in an offensive | A full discharge exposes replenishment; prepared winter access reaches its assets | Enduring winter without changing storage or replenishment |
| **6H. Tcho-Tcho Compact** | Decapitation-proof; genuinely useful to its members | Must keep providing, and the patron's price is a debt that compounds | Attacking cells, which recruits for them |
| **6I. Azathoth** | Mindless; defined resonance exposes the region to it | Capture has boundaries and can be grounded, disconnected, or counter-patterned | Using known qualifying power without accounting for its captured emission |
| **7. Angels** | Correct about the facts, and escalating | Bound by jurisdiction, precedent, and proportion; cannot refuse genuine restitution | Fighting, which proves the charge and raises the next respondent |
| **7M. Maruts** | Recursive; each one destroyed is replaced by two | Enforce one defined violation and cannot exceed the warrant; the doubling triggers on resistance only | Winning. Every victory doubles the response |
| **8. Witch King** | Your casualties become his soldiers | Needs bodies and standing infrastructure; ash is not a resource, and undead destroyed are spent forever | Winning a field battle and marching on without burning your own dead |
| **9. Apocalypse Cult** | Assembles a god from parts, and will substitute inferior ones | Needs the components, the sites, and the logistics to move them | Scorched earth, which in the fire variant is a delivery of fuel |
| **10. Eternal Cycle** | Two absolutes, each able to remake the world | Neither can act without mortal proxies, and each escalation summons the other | Choosing the better side |
| **11. Githyanki** | Desperate, disciplined, and materially capable | Need land and food; militancy is a response to conditions rather than a nature | Fortifying the border, which manufactures the war it fears |
| **12. Slumbering Catastrophe** | Overwhelming force once its specific capabilities recover | Starts dormant, bound, or wounded; prepared instruments can exploit those conditions | Waiting to feel strong enough while an enabled waking operation finishes |

### Using the Table

The **Suffering** column is the campaign. It is where a party of ordinary size gets leverage over something enormous, and it is almost never reached by fighting. Finding it is usually the work of Act I, and the moment a table stops attacking the power and starts attacking the suffering is the moment the path turns.

Resolve the **obvious move** honestly. Tactical victories preserve rescued people, interrupted attacks, expended enemy resources, and earned reprieves even when they do not meet a campaign success condition. Evidence should explain the remaining problem. Never force a win, erase its benefit, or increase the threat by fiat to teach the engine.

## 1. The Captive Domain: Comprehension and Overthrow

**World Process:** The characters are trapped inside a supernatural land ruled by a hidden captor. There is no escape route to find; freedom becomes possible only by understanding the domain and defeating the power that owns it.

The domain may be a Domain of Dread, a closed region of the Feywild, or another captive reality. Its ruler can embody any horror tradition: a vampire, lich, hag, flesh-golem creator, werewolf lord, ghost, mummy, devil, murderous noble, or similarly dominant figure.

### Path Progression

1. **Disorientation:** Roads return to their starting points, borders fold inward, and every attempted departure leads back into the domain.
2. **Learning the Land:** The party discovers the realm's supernatural laws, safe places, factions, repeated horrors, and forbidden acts.
3. **Recognizing the Design:** Apparently separate monsters and tragedies reveal a common history, pattern, or controlling intelligence.
4. **Identifying the Captor:** The party determines who rules the domain, what obsession shapes it, how the ruler feeds upon it, and which servants act knowingly.
5. **Discovering the Protections:** The ruler's immortality, invulnerability, false identity, anchors, and control of the land become concrete problems that can be attacked.
6. **Counter-Armament:** The party recovers weapons, rites, allies, true names, or circumstances capable of removing those protections.
7. **The Domain Defends Itself:** Roads move, refuges are punished, servants are recalled, and the captor tries to divide or bargain with the party.
8. **Overthrow:** With sufficient protections removed, the ruler can finally be confronted and the domain can be released, transformed, or claimed.

### Persistent Footprint

Record discoveries in five groups:

- **Laws of the Land:** Supernatural rules that govern travel, death, promises, memory, hospitality, violence, and other parts of life.
- **Captor Evidence:** History and recurring patterns that identify who controls the realm.
- **Anchors:** People, places, objects, or repeated events that maintain the captor's power.
- **Countermeasures:** Weapons, rituals, allies, and fulfilled conditions that can bypass a protection.
- **Safe Havens:** Places and communities still capable of sheltering resistance.

The party's growing understanding is one form of campaign progress. The captor's destruction of safe havens and consolidation of anchors is the opposing movement.

### Recurring Adventures

- Test a suspected law of the land.
- Recover testimony about an earlier rebellion.
- Determine whether a local horror is a servant, victim, or independent prisoner.
- Expose the ruler's public mask.
- Rescue someone who knows where an anchor is hidden.
- Restore an object whose absence sustains the domain's curse.
- Acquire or forge a weapon capable of harming the captor.
- Remove one protection and survive the domain's retaliation.

Each substantial adventure should reveal a law, recover history, identify the captor, expose a weakness, secure an ally, acquire a countermeasure, or remove a protection.

### Captor Framework

Define seven elements for the ruler:

1. **Horror Identity:** What kind of monster or villain rules the domain?
2. **Public Mask:** How does the captor first appear to the party?
3. **Obsession:** What desire, grievance, or repeated tragedy shapes the land?
4. **Feeding Method:** What does the captor continually take from the inhabitants?
5. **Domain Law:** Which supernatural rule makes the captor supreme?
6. **Anchors:** What maintains that rule?
7. **Killing Condition:** What must be learned, assembled, or changed before the captor can die?

A vampire might have abolished dawn by concealing the remains of three murdered saints. A lich might steal everyone's memories at midwinter to conceal its soul vessels. A flesh-golem creator might replace the dead with replicas linked through a single lightning engine. Defeating any of them requires mastering the particular land they created.

### Transformation

The ruler's defeat ends the captivity, but it does not prescribe one result. The realm might reconnect to its original world, remain as a free land, collapse, choose a new ruler, or offer the victorious party control of it.

### Advancing the Path

**This path has no clock.** The domain does not advance on a track, because it is not going anywhere—it already has what it wants. It reacts. The stages are gated by what the party have *proven*, and the captor's Terms are completed in response to their movement, not on a schedule. A party who learn nothing can stay alive here for years.

There is also no escape stage. The roads return, the borders fold, and no act is spent hunting a way out. The three stages are **Survive**, **Identify**, and **Arm and Overthrow**.

#### Terms of the Domain

The captor completes these in any order, usually in answer to something the party did.

| Term | The deed | Grants |
| :--- | :------- | :----- |
| **Take a haven** | A settlement that sheltered resistance stops doing so, by ruin or by fear | Every remaining haven is one watch further from every other |
| **Feed uninterrupted** | A feeding completed with no witness left alive | The captor recovers from any wound not dealt with a countermeasure |
| **Re-enact the tragedy** | The obsession played to its end with new participants in the old roles | The domain gains one new law, written by the captor |
| **Mark a stranger** | An outsider breaks a law of the land to survive | The captor can find that person anywhere in the domain |
| **Move an anchor** | An anchor relocated to ground the party have already searched | What the party know about its old site becomes false |
| **Be invited** | Someone the party rely on accepts a bargain | The captor may cross any threshold that person could |

#### The Two Conditions

They are not opposites, and the party should be shown both.

| The captor wins when | The party win when |
| :------------------- | :------------------ |
| Every haven is Taken, or every surviving character carries three Marks. The domain then holds no prisoners—only residents—and needs nothing further. | The **Killing Condition** is assembled and used to overthrow the captor, or an authored alternative resolves the captivity. Merely possessing a weapon or knowing a true name enables the attempt; it does not complete the confrontation. |

The consequence is worth stating plainly at the table: **preventing the Terms is not winning, and losing them is not losing.** A party can hold every haven for nine levels and be no closer to leaving. A party can lose four havens, take six Marks between them, and end the path in a single night because they finally hold the one thing that works.

#### The Three Stages

| Stage | Ends when | Enemy pressure during it |
| :---- | :-------- | :----------------------- |
| **I — Survive** (levels 1–3) | Three Laws proven and one Haven sworn: the party can cross the domain and be sheltered | Terms completed against the people who help them, to teach the domain what helping costs |
| **II — Identify** (levels 4–7) | The captor named, its obsession and feeding method established, every Anchor located | Anchors moved, evidence falsified, and a bargain offered to somebody trusted |
| **III — Arm and Overthrow** (levels 8–10) | The Killing Condition assembled and used | The domain defends itself: roads move, refuges are punished, servants are recalled |

**Comprehension is the party's real currency**, and it is spendable. A Law proven is a Law that can be used once to do something the domain believes impossible.

#### Marks and the Other Way Out

Each time a character breaks a law of the land to survive, write the law and the character's name. Marks never clear. At three Marks a character is a fixture of the domain rather than a prisoner in it.

That is not only a penalty. **There is an exit that is not victory:** a character who dies, rises undead, becomes fey, or otherwise stops being the kind of thing the domain was built to hold is no longer held by it, and its laws stop applying to them the way they applied before. This is available from the first session and is a bargain rather than a defeat. Marks are its instalment plan. A party who all take it have not lost the path so much as declined to be the people it was about—and the domain has acquired four new fixtures.

#### At the Table

**Tell.** Distance. Roads shorten as Laws are proven and lengthen as havens are Taken; the party will notice the map becoming honest long before anyone says a number. Also the weather over the castle, the behaviour of dogs, and which songs innkeepers will not finish.

**Reading.** "Five laws proven, three anchors found, and the chapel at Vend still keeps its doors. You can reach the mill before dark now, and the captor knows you can."

**If ignored.** The captor does not hunt anyone; it outlasts them. Each neglect tick Takes a haven and offers the party a comfortable place in the tragedy—a huntsman's warrant, a physician's practice, a marriage. Abandonment means they are inhabitants now, remembered in the next generation's folklore as people who arrived and stayed.

**If failed.** The captor takes Comprehension rather than lives. Proven Laws are forgotten, records rot, maps stop agreeing with the ground. Reduce the party's proven Laws to zero, keep every Mark, and let the current inhabitants tell them about an earlier rebellion that sounds oddly familiar.

**If the captor dies early.** The domain was an appetite rather than a place, and it begins dissolving with everyone still inside. **Inheritance:** the land wants a ruler and will take one with or without consent. The rest of the path is governing a realm whose laws require a monster, without supplying one.

## 2. The Aboleth Savant: Enslavement

**World Process:** The Savant uses mind control to eliminate independent will. Every controlled person becomes an instrument for identifying, isolating, and enthralling the next victim.

The Aboleth's limitless inherited knowledge makes its domination precise, but knowledge is not its objective. It uses forgotten truths, tailored promises, blackmail, psychic force, and controlled institutions in pursuit of a world in which every important decision is an extension of its own mind.

### Path Progression

1. **Anomalous Obedience:** People act against their interests, lose time, abandon relationships, or defend inexplicable decisions.
2. **Hidden Thralls:** Controlled healers, guards, priests, ferrymen, innkeepers, scholars, and messengers conceal abductions and select new victims.
3. **Targeted Conversion:** The Savant enthralls people whose position provides access to many others: magistrates, officers, guildmasters, diviners, and members of the party's support network.
4. **Institutional Enthrallment:** Courts deliver prisoners, temples preach surrender, healers administer psychic agents, and authorities punish resistance while outwardly continuing their usual functions.
5. **The Control Web:** Submerged shrines, altered reservoirs, enthralled prophets, parasites, and psychic relays transmit commands across the region.
6. **Mass Submission:** Shared dreams, contaminated water, public rites, or captured institutions expose entire populations at once.
7. **One Will:** Society still appears to function, but no meaningful independent decision remains.

### Persistent Footprint

Track a network of minds and institutions:

- Free
- Suspected
- Confirmed enthralled
- Recently liberated
- Psychic relay or conversion site
- Protected against domination

Each controlled institution gives the Savant a specific capability. A controlled court supplies prisoners; a temple reaches believers; a courier network transmits commands; a healer can compromise people who seek protection.

### Recurring Adventures

- Determine whether a trusted ally is being controlled.
- Capture a thrall without killing the person trapped inside.
- Rescue victims from a conversion site.
- Protect someone selected for enthrallment.
- Recover a psychic ward, discipline, or counter-agent.
- Destroy a relay without alerting the wider network.
- Feed false commands into the control web.
- Enter an institution where anyone may be a thrall.
- Free a dangerous enemy because their independent will is preferable to the Savant possessing it.

The Savant's knowledge makes surrender tempting. It can promise to restore the dead, end war, prevent betrayal, or remove grief. Its vision of peace is genuine in one respect: minds without freedom cannot choose to hurt one another.

### Transformation

The party must acquire protection against direct domination and isolate the Savant from its network before confronting it. Killing the Savant without dismantling its relays may leave enthralled institutions, autonomous psychic commands, or rival Aboleths capable of inheriting the system.

## 3. The Titans: Unbinding the First World

**World Process:** Servants of the beings who ruled before the gods restore their names, break their prisons, return their stolen powers, and awaken them to wage war upon the divine order.

The gods did not merely defeat the Titans. They constructed the present world from that victory. Mountains may be prison chains, divine weapons may contain stolen Titan organs, and stable laws of nature may depend upon a Titan remaining bound. Freeing one therefore changes both the balance of power and the physical world.

### The Five Restoration States

Each Titan progresses independently through five states:

1. **Forgotten:** Its history and true name have been erased, preventing it from acting coherently.
2. **Named:** Restoring the name allows the Titan to enter dreams, appoint prophets, and coordinate servants.
3. **Stirring:** A chain is broken or an organ returned. A hand, eye, voice, shadow, or elemental aspect manifests and gains one regional power.
4. **Armed:** The Titan recovers a stolen weapon, heart, crown, or elemental authority and can directly attack the gods' institutions.
5. **Incarnate:** The final prison opens and the Titan walks the world in pursuit of revenge.

### Release Deeds

Advancing a Titan requires specific deeds rather than generic progress. Examples include:

- Restore its erased true name.
- Break a chain concealed inside a divine temple.
- Return an organ used to empower a god.
- Recover its weapon from a celestial vault.
- Spill the blood of the god who imprisoned it.
- Reunite its divided descendants.
- Open its prison during a celestial conjunction.

Several imprisoned Titans and several simultaneous Release Deeds create the campaign's strategic choices. Neither the enemy nor the party can pursue every site at once.

### Adventure Path Progression

#### Act I — The Servants and the Map of Chains

Giant clans, primordial beasts, and strange cults raid archives, tombs, temples, and ruins. Repeated symbols reveal that these are coordinated operations against an ancient prison system.

Naming a Titan enables coherent action and opens the next movement within this act. It does not force the party to release one before they can investigate the prison map.

**The Map of Chains.**

The party identifies several Titan prisons and learns that temples, mountains, cities, and natural laws form parts of their bindings. Gods and churches recruit the party while concealing uncomfortable parts of the original war.

The act ends when the party has an actionable map of prisons and their dependencies. A Titan reaching **Stirring** can demonstrate those dependencies by transforming a region, but preventing that release does not block progression.

#### Act II — The Gods' Crime and the Returning Powers

The party discovers why the Titans seek revenge. Some are monsters, while others were betrayed because the new gods needed their bodies or powers. The heroes must decide which prisons to defend, which Titans could be negotiated with, and whether the existing divine order deserves preservation.

**The Returning Powers.**

Multiple Titans occupy different restoration states. One may possess an army but no weapon; another may walk the world while remaining bound by one chain; a third may be only a persuasive voice. The gods intervene openly and turn mortal territory into their battlefield.

The act ends when the party has selected and prepared a viable settlement, rebinding, release, or confrontation. Enemy restoration changes what that plan costs; it does not supply a mandatory success the party must allow.

#### Act III — War of Succession

The party gathers the means to rebind, kill, reconcile, or empower the returning Titans. The finale occurs at the original battlefield, celestial throne, or mechanism that transformed Titan bodies into the present world's laws.

### Persistent Footprint

For each Titan record:

- Current restoration state
- Completed and available Release Deeds
- Servant factions
- Regions physically changed by its stirring
- Stolen powers still held by the gods
- Remaining chains and what each chain sustains
- Grievance, demands, and willingness to negotiate

Every completed Release Deed grants the eventual Titan a specific ability. Every deed prevented or reversed removes an ability from the final confrontation. If it recovers its eyes, it cannot be surprised; if it regains its storm weapon, it controls weather and flight; if one chain remains, it may be limited to an avatar. The campaign's history constructs the final enemy.

### Transformation

The party may rebind the Titans, kill them with the gods' stolen weapons, negotiate a new cosmic settlement, free selected Titans against worse gods, replace the gods, or discover that killing a Titan also destroys the natural law made from its body.

## 4. Tharizdun: Dissolution

**World Process:** The distinctions that make reality coherent are severed one by one until Tharizdun's prison and the world containing it become indistinguishable.

This is not an invasion by a being with different physical laws. It is the destruction of the possibility of stable laws. Cults of slime, madness, entropy, and unmaking may appear unrelated because they do not need to coordinate. Each erodes a different bond whose existence helps keep Tharizdun contained.

### The Bonds of the World

Define several fundamental distinctions that also function as bars of the prison:

- Name and thing
- Self and other
- Life and death
- Form and substance
- Place and distance
- Cause and consequence
- Promise and meaning
- Past and future

Each major enemy operation targets one Bond. When a Bond is damaged, the affected region gains a concrete rule change: names refer to several people, doors open into incompatible places, corpses cannot remain dead, oaths cease to bind, or living bodies flow together like slime.

### Path Progression

1. **Hairline Contradictions:** Small impossibilities, unstable identities, oozes, shared memories, and broken causality appear as isolated horrors.
2. **Local Dissolutions:** A cult or entity deliberately erodes one Bond within a site, settlement, or population.
3. **The Common Absence:** The party discovers that otherwise unrelated outbreaks leave the same void in history, divination, and divine knowledge.
4. **Chain Reaction:** Damage to one Bond weakens others. A town without stable names cannot maintain promises; a place without death cannot preserve a distinction between bodies.
5. **The Porous Prison:** Aspects of Tharizdun act through demon lords, oozes, dreams, contradictions, and people who no longer possess singular identities.
6. **Uncreation:** The remaining Bonds fail and reality can no longer distinguish the prison, its prisoner, or itself.

### Persistent Footprint

Track:

- Each Bond's condition
- Regions suffering a changed rule
- Material, social, and sacred anchors capable of restoring that Bond
- Cults or entities currently attacking it
- Secondary Bonds placed at risk by the damage
- Permanent sacrifices already made to repair reality

### Recurring Adventures

- Determine which distinction a seemingly senseless horror is eroding.
- Recover a true name, original boundary, first oath, or other conceptual anchor.
- Separate people, places, or histories that have begun merging.
- Preserve testimony when memory and identity are no longer reliable.
- Turn rival servants of dissolution against one another.
- Decide what must be permanently fixed or sacrificed to restore a Bond.
- Enter a region where a familiar rule no longer applies.
- Reconstruct one bar of the prison before the failure spreads into another.

### Transformation

Tharizdun cannot be defeated through an ordinary physical confrontation. The party must preserve enough distinctions to reconstruct the prison, create new Bonds from mortal relationships and sacrifices, redirect dissolution into another vessel, or enter the breach and sever Tharizdun's connection to the failing world.

## 5. Bane: Consolidation

**World Process:** Tyranny becomes ordinary by capturing institutions and making obedience appear safer than resistance.

Bane does not advance merely by conquering territory or killing opponents. His servants manufacture or exploit disorder, offer security, acquire emergency powers, and turn temporary controls into permanent institutions. A dead governor can be replaced while the courts, granaries, informants, and garrisons remain obedient.

### Path Progression

1. **Fear and Disorder:** Violence, scarcity, sabotage, or factional conflict makes the existing order appear incapable of protecting people.
2. **The Promise of Security:** Banite agents supply soldiers, food, courts, or decisive leadership in exchange for exceptional authority.
3. **Emergency Rule:** Registration, curfews, weapons restrictions, controlled worship, and political detention become legal necessities.
4. **Institutional Capture:** Temples, courts, trade routes, food stores, and local armed forces answer to the new regime.
5. **Normalized Obedience:** Neighbours inform upon one another, collaboration becomes profitable, and resistance appears criminal rather than heroic.
6. **The Total State:** Bane no longer needs to manifest personally; the occupied society reproduces his tyranny for him.

### Persistent Footprint

Record control separately for institutions such as:

- Armed force
- Courts and law
- Food and trade
- Temples and public belief
- Communications and records
- Local leadership

A settlement is not simply free or occupied. It may possess a loyal garrison but an independent temple, or a Banite court that cannot enforce its rulings without control of the grain road.

### Recurring Adventures

- Expose or prevent a manufactured crisis.
- Break a prison transport or destroy a registry.
- Keep a trade road outside the regime's control.
- Persuade an institution to defect.
- Protect communities that refuse compliance.
- Undermine the belief that Bane alone can provide order.
- Decide whether a useful collaborator can be forgiven.

### Transformation

Bane is defeated when the regime loses the capacity to coordinate and obedience becomes more costly than resistance. Killing an avatar may be part of the finale, but it is insufficient unless the party also breaks the institutions that would recreate the tyranny.

### Advancing the Path

**This path advances by legislation, and it is the party's own victories that pass the laws.** Bane does not need territory or a body count. He needs the paperwork to keep functioning after the soldiers go home, and disorder is what makes the paperwork acceptable. Every dramatic, violent, public success against the regime is evidence that extraordinary measures were warranted.

#### Decrees

Each Decree is a legal instrument. It is enacted, not conquered, and it stays enacted after the official who signed it is dead.

| Decree | Enacted when | Grants |
| :----- | :----------- | :----- |
| **Registration** | A crisis makes counting people look prudent | The regime knows every name, trade, and address in the district |
| **Curfew** | Violence occurs at night, whoever committed it | Movement after dark is itself the offence; no other charge is needed |
| **Weapons licence** | An armed band does something frightening in public | Adventuring companies are criminal by default and must be sponsored |
| **Grain monopoly** | Scarcity, hoarding, or a broken supply road | Hunger becomes administrable; loyalty is a ration |
| **Cult licensing** | A temple is implicated in disorder | Worship requires a permit and the permits are reviewed annually |
| **Emergency tribunal** | An atrocity nobody can explain quickly | Conviction without evidence, and a court that delivers prisoners on request |

#### Compliance

Compliance is separate from the Decrees and is counted in things ordinary people do without being forced: taxes remitted early, registrations filed unprompted, curfews kept on empty streets, informants who volunteer. A Decree without Compliance is an occupation, and occupations end. A Decree with Compliance is simply how things are done.

**The rule that makes this engine bite:** any violent success in a populated place raises Compliance by one, unless the party can point at a visible alternative that is already working. A prefect assassinated in the street justifies the next decree. A burned caravan proves the roads need protecting. This engine punishes heroism performed without infrastructure, and the players will feel it happening.

#### The Two Conditions

| Bane wins when | The party win when |
| :------------- | :------------------ |
| Four Decrees are in force *with* Compliance. He is then unnecessary: the society reproduces the tyranny without him, and killing every Banite in the province changes nothing. | The writ cannot be enforced beyond the walls that issue it. **Every Decree may remain on the books.** Repeal is not required and is not usually available. |

Bane's condition is a threshold; the party's is a capability. They are competing over whether the machine can *reach* anybody, not over the number of laws.

#### Three Legal Phases

| Phase | Levels | The question | Ends when |
| :---- | :----- | :----------- | :-------- |
| **I — Disorder** | 1–3 | Who is manufacturing this, and can it be shown? | A crisis is publicly exposed before its Decree passes, or the first two Decrees pass |
| **II — Emergency** | 4–7 | Which institutions can be kept working outside the writ? | An institution defects intact and keeps functioning, or four Decrees are in force |
| **III — Normalcy** | 8–10 | Can the regime still reach a village that refuses? | Coordination breaks, or neighbours begin informing on neighbours unprompted |

#### At the Table

**Tell.** Bread, checkpoints, and greetings: the price of grain, how many times a day you are asked for papers, and whether people you have helped will admit in public that they know you.

**Reading.** "Four Decrees in force and the temple still unlicensed. Tithes came in early this quarter without a single arrest. You can be sheltered in Oakhaven, but nobody will do it twice."

**If ignored.** The regime consolidates and the province becomes genuinely safe—roads patrolled, bandits hanged, grain arriving, courts quick. Each neglect tick enacts a Decree somewhere the party are not, and offers them something real: a licence, a commission, a reliable border to trade across. Abandonment produces a prosperous, orderly, unfree province and the uncomfortable discovery that many people prefer it. The consequence the party will dislike is not ruin. It is how little was missed.

**If failed.** The Total State, which does not need Bane and will not be dislodged by killing anyone. Play continues as crime rather than heroism, and the ledger of who informs on whom becomes the campaign.

**If the regime breaks early.** Granaries, courts, garrisons, and roads all stop at once. **Inheritance:** somebody has to run the winter, and the Toll decides how bad it is. A party who took the grain road by burning it wrote the famine's casualty figure themselves.

## 6. Powers Outside Reality

The entities commonly grouped together as Great Old Ones do not share one Adventure Path. Their common feature is that they originate beyond conventional reality and do not fully obey its physical laws. Each power nevertheless enters, spreads, and wins through a different process.

Choose one of the following as the primary engine. Other entities may appear as rivals, false explanations, opportunists, or secondary pressures without importing their entire engine.

### 6A. Great Cthulhu: Recurrent Awakening

**World Process:** Dreams, cult activity, and celestial conditions permit repeated physical manifestations. Destroying or banishing the body ends the immediate catastrophe but makes no permanent difference to an immortal entity.

#### Path Progression

1. **Dream Plague:** Sensitive people share images of drowned architecture, impossible stars, and a vast presence beneath the sea.
2. **The Coastal Cult:** Disappearances, sacrifices, recovered tablets, and maritime expeditions prepare the necessary anchor points.
3. **Rising R'lyeh:** Tides, coastlines, and geometry change as the sunken city begins intersecting the world.
4. **First Awakening:** Cthulhu manifests as a direct physical catastrophe. The party can survive, divert, or banish him but cannot permanently kill him.
5. **Cheaper Return:** Surviving cults reuse awakened sites, dreams become clearer, and subsequent manifestations require fewer preparations.
6. **Permanent Intersection:** R'lyeh remains connected to the world and physical banishment ceases to provide meaningful relief.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track dreamers, cult anchors, celestial conditions, surfaced districts of R'lyeh, devastated regions, and the requirements for another awakening. Each manifestation should permanently alter at least one location even if Cthulhu is banished.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Protect or interrogate a significant dreamer.
- Prevent a cult expedition from recovering an anchor.
- Explore a temporarily surfaced part of R'lyeh.
- Evacuate a region in the path of a manifestation.
- Discover why each return is becoming easier.
- Realign, destroy, or invert an earthly or celestial anchor.

#### Transformation

Permanent victory requires making the world unreachable: sink or rotate R'lyeh out of conjunction, destroy the terrestrial anchors, alter the stars, or seal the dimensional intersection while Cthulhu is manifested and vulnerable to exclusion.

#### Advancing the Path

**This path advances by ratchet.** Each Preparation permanently lowers the cost of the next Awakening, and no Preparation is ever undone by a banishment. The path is a sequence of catastrophes that get cheaper, and its measure counts *downward*: the number of things still required before he can be called again.

Defeating or banishing the body does not remove completed Preparations. It does earn the committed interval before re-entry, which the party can use for rescue, salvage, or a permanent remedy. Record that interval and its actual conditions before the confrontation.

#### Preparations

Start at six required. Each completed Preparation permanently reduces the requirement for every future Awakening.

| Preparation | The deed | Grants |
| :---------- | :------- | :----- |
| **The tablet** | A cult expedition recovers a legible instruction | Dreams become specific; sensitives can be recruited rather than merely found |
| **The chorus** | Enough dreamers dream the same night | The cult can locate its own recruits without searching |
| **The angle** | The drowned city's geometry is measured and recorded | Manifestations may occur at any anchor, not only above the city |
| **The raised anchor** | A submerged site is brought within reach of the surface | A permanent arrival point that survives banishment |
| **The offering at sea** | A sacrifice completed beyond sight of land | Weather and tide assist the next Awakening |
| **The star condition** | An astronomical alignment observed and recorded correctly | Future alignments can be predicted and prepared for, instead of awaited |

#### The Two Conditions

| Cthulhu wins when | The party win when |
| :----------------- | :------------------ |
| The requirement reaches zero and R'lyeh stays intersected. Permanent intersection must be discharged or bound; resolve a banishment's committed reprieve without inventing an immediate replacement. | **Coastal exclusion OR source binding.** Discharge the intersection and permanently exclude every committed arrival site, or bind the submerged source so projection is impossible even with anchors intact. Each completed remedy is independently sufficient. |

An immortal cannot be on a casualty list. The party's condition never contains the word "kill", and a table that has understood this stops trying to win the fight and starts trying to win the coastline.

#### Three Cycles

Acts are counted in manifestations, not levels, though they land at roughly the same places.

| Cycle | Roughly | Shape |
| :---- | :------ | :---- |
| **I — Dream and Cult** | Levels 1–3 | Moves toward the first possible Awakening. Survive, divert, or banish it if it occurs; an earned early exclusion can prevent it. An actual manifestation permanently changes a named place. |
| **II — Cheaper Return** | Levels 4–7 | The interval between possible Awakenings shortens as Preparations accumulate. Ends when the party can demonstrate a working exclusion, such as an inverted anchor, and plan how to close the remaining routes. |
| **III — Exclusion** | Levels 8–10 | The last Awakening is fought while the party work on the geometry, not on him. Ends when arrival is closed or the intersection becomes permanent. |

Every manifestation must permanently alter at least one location even when banished. That change is the Toll. Completed Preparations stay completed; exclusion changes whether an arrival route works, not whether the cult learned its earlier lesson. The [executable ratchet procedure](08_adventure_path_population.md#6a-cthulhu-closing-the-coast) defines temporary preparations, scheduled conjunctions, and permanent exclusion separately.

#### At the Table

**Tell.** The sea. Tide tables that stop working, a horizon at the wrong height, and how many people in a given town dreamed the same thing last night.

**Reading.** "Two preparations remain, and there were six in spring. Drowned Cay is under water that was not there in autumn. He can be called before the equinox, and after that he can be called whenever they like."

**If ignored.** The awakenings proceed and do not pursue anyone inland; each neglect tick completes a Preparation. Abandonment depopulates the coast, ends maritime trade, and relocates an entire economy—which is an extremely profitable thing to be early to, and the party will have been.

**If failed.** Permanent intersection. The drowned city stays, physical victory stops meaning anything, and the campaign moves inland to become a story about refugees.

**If the coast is closed early.** **Inheritance:** the party hold the anchors, the angle, and the calculations, which makes them the only people alive who know how to call him deliberately. Several powers would like that, and one of them is already writing to them.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Coastal exclusion:** complete permanent exclusion works at every committed arrival site and discharge the active intersection. **Success B — Source binding:** reach the submerged source and complete its binding through one declared conjunction interval, preventing projection through otherwise intact arrival sites. Neither requires the other. Both end this incursion; killing the manifestation alone does not.

The [player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6a-cthulhu--dream-contact-and-safe-recovery) define accepted benefits and prices, gear or relationship effects, monster objectives, evidence, and completion events. Apply these together with population generation; remedies are alternatives and earned early victory remains valid.

### 6B. Nyarlathotep: Mandates and Divided Interests

**World Process:** Fallible masks acquire real endorsements and exploit divided institutions to realise a local incarnation. They require specific authority and voluntary acceptance; player choices are not retroactively part of a predetermined script.

**Three acts:** useful commissions → trace active mandates → revoke the incarnation or bind the mask. Generate three indispensable mandates, issuers and revocation procedures, evidence trails, and a trapping condition. Actual signatures, witnessed statements, courier routes, and custody determine what authority exists.

**Player decisions:** lend the party's name, narrow a request, verify a beneficiary, protect a witness, or pursue independent help. Renown matters only among communities that know the party and can grant the requested authority. Monsters abduct witnesses and intercept papers rather than omnisciently erasing evidence.

**Transformation:** revoke and sever the incarnation's mandates, or trap and banish its embodied mask during acceptance. Each is sufficient even if the other remains incomplete. Betrayed clients and legitimate offices persist after victory.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Revoke the incarnation:** expose the common beneficiary to the actual issuers, revoke the three mandates, and complete a public severance at the site; its old endorsements can no longer sustain this incarnation. **Success B — Bind the mask:** use the discovered true designation and prepared vessel to trap and banish the local incarnation during its acceptance, even if the institutions still believe its public identities. Collecting the designation alone is insufficient.

The [player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6b-nyarlathotep--endorsements-and-divided-interests) define accepted benefits and prices, gear or relationship effects, monster objectives, evidence, and completion events. Apply these together with population generation; remedies are alternatives and earned early victory remains valid.

### 6C. Shub-Niggurath: Proliferation

**World Process:** Cheap cult cells, spawning grounds, and gates reproduce faster when they form a connected territorial network.

#### Path Progression

1. **Hidden Broods:** Isolated rural cults conceal strange births, missing livestock, and altered bloodlines.
2. **Spawning Grounds:** Groves, caverns, farms, and abandoned settlements become nurseries for servants and Dark Young.
3. **Gate Network:** Cult cells connect their nurseries, sharing sacrifices, monsters, and supernatural fertility.
4. **Runaway Fecundity:** Vegetation, animals, monsters, and people reproduce or mutate beyond the land's ability to sustain them.
5. **The Living Territory:** Connected regions behave like organs of one vast reproductive system.
6. **The Thousand Young:** The network becomes capable of continuously replacing any force used to attack it.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track cult cells, nurseries, gates, connections between them, servant populations, and the central sources feeding the network. An isolated site can be cleared normally; connected sites reinforce and repopulate one another.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Locate a nursery before its first spawning.
- Rescue people selected as parents, vessels, or sacrifices.
- Cut the route connecting two cult regions.
- Cleanse a site without spreading its seeds.
- Hold territory while a gate is permanently closed.
- Find the Mother Gate or root organism sustaining the network.

#### Transformation

Win by severing forced propagation, or by replacing divine dependencies with a stable bounded ecology through a full birth/feeding cycle. Each is sufficient; neither requires killing transformed civilians. Stopping a raid saves its targets even when intact sources can replace the raiders.

#### The Census

This path is not tracked as a threat. It is tracked as a population problem, on a table a tax assessor would recognise, and the party can eventually do the arithmetic themselves.

| Brood | Mouths | Land it can feed | Pressure |
| :---- | -----: | ---------------: | -------: |
| Karst warren | 340 | 260 | **+80** |
| Bog nursery | 90 | 180 | −90 |
| Elderwood grove | 210 | 210 | 0 |

**Pressure is mouths minus capacity.** This constrains feeding and expansion; individual gifts, consent, and source operations have their own recorded conditions.

- Each season, every brood adds mouths. A brood connected to another shares its capacity — which makes connections useful. An isolated brood starves only if its own supply cannot meet consumption.
- **A brood at positive Pressure must vent before the season turns.** It vents in one of three ways: it sends a war-band out, it opens a new nursery, or it starves. It does not choose the noble option. It chooses the cheapest.
- **A destroyed war-band is a successful vent.** Write the relief in the Pressure column in the party's favour, because that is who provided it. Eighty mouths left the warren and did not come back, and the warren will be healthier next spring than it has been in years.

That last rule is the discovery at the heart of the path. Let the party win, be thanked, and be paid, while tally-sticks, food movements, and surviving witnesses make the underlying process discoverable. Once they obtain the census, explain its arithmetic plainly; do not postpone understanding until a prescribed number of raids.

**What the party can actually do.** The census can be discovered and explained through evidence. Do not withhold arithmetic the party have earned simply to preserve a surprise.

| Move | Effect | Cost |
| :--- | :----- | :--- |
| Defeat a war-band | Stops its raid; dead mouths reduce pressure | Actual battle risk; saved people and earned pay remain real |
| Burn a feeding ground | Capacity falls; increased pressure can cause a feasible raid or starvation | Civilian loss and reduced food; enemy route choices remain its own |
| Sever a connection | Each brood loses shared capacity; recalculate actual supply | Hold the cut and rescue dependants; starvation is not automatic |
| Disable or regulate the fecundity source | Forced births stop when the operation succeeds | Reach it and execute severance or bounded regulation |
| Open a direction | Pressure vents somewhere that is not you | Somebody else's valley |

**The three seasons.** *I* — stop actual raids and discover useful gifts. *II* — obtain the census and choose how to provision or end dependencies. *III* — execute source severance or establish a bounded ecology. No victory requires starvation or redirecting violence onto a neighbour.

**Reading.** "Three hundred and forty mouths at the Karst against two hundred and sixty of feed. You killed eighty of them in the spring. They will send a hundred and twenty this autumn, and it will not be because they hate you."

**If ignored.** Pressure vents at whoever is nearest and softest, which is not the party. Broods connect, capacity pools, and the count compounds. Abandonment produces one continuous territory that farms the valley it took.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Sever propagation:** disable the Mother Gate and every independent forced-propagation source so no compulsory expansion remains possible. **Success B — Bound the ecology:** replace each divine reproductive dependency with the demonstrated regulator, supply its bounded population, and complete one full birth/feeding cycle without tribute or compelled propagation. The sources can remain alive under B. Neither requires killing transformed civilians or curing every voluntary gift.

The [player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6c-shub-niggurath--gifts-with-appetites) define accepted benefits and prices, gear or relationship effects, monster objectives, evidence, and completion events. Apply these together with population generation; remedies are alternatives and earned early victory remains valid.

### 6D. Hastur: Complicity and Desecration

**World Process:** Art, status, dreams, and public ritual persuade communities to participate willingly in their own transformation.

#### Path Progression

1. **The Fashion:** A symbol, play, song, colour, or philosophy appears exciting, liberating, and socially valuable.
2. **Private Roles:** Participants adopt secret names and behaviours that grant inspiration, influence, or shared dreams.
3. **Desecration:** Performances at significant sites replace local meanings with the logic of the Yellow Sign.
4. **Public Participation:** Festivals, courts, salons, or political movements normalize the work and marginalize those who refuse it.
5. **Loss of Recognition:** Witnesses increasingly recognise accepted roles in place of personal identities. Players still choose their characters' actions; invoking a privilege carries its disclosed obligations.
6. **Carcosa:** The city or region becomes the final stage, and its inhabitants complete the summoning simply by playing their parts.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track corrupted works, their audiences, desecrated sites, people assigned roles, public acceptance, and acts required for the final performance. Corruption is not merely infection: many participants initially desire what it offers.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Trace a work through patrons, performers, and audiences.
- Recover someone who no longer recognizes their original identity.
- Prevent a significant site from being used as a stage.
- Create a counter-performance or restore the site's former meaning.
- Expose the cost of participation without spreading the forbidden work further.
- Interrupt the final act without performing the missing role oneself.

#### Transformation

Win by enabling withdrawal and permanently dissolving one indispensable role, or by completing the counter-performance that restores all linked venues and cancels the work's claims. Either is sufficient; passive exposure, book destruction, or killing a replaceable performer is not.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Dissolve the cast:** enable an indispensable participant to withdraw and complete that role's dissolution rite at its originating venue, so a replacement audition cannot restore it. **Success B — Counter-performance:** recover the original names and rite and complete a willing counter-performance that restores all linked venues and cancels the work's claims, even with its cast still assembled. Neither is merely burning a book or murdering a performer.

The [player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6d-hastur--roles-recognition-and-audiences) define accepted benefits and prices, gear or relationship effects, monster objectives, evidence, and completion events. Apply these together with population generation; remedies are alternatives and earned early victory remains valid.

### 6E. Yog-Sothoth: Conjunction

**World Process:** Gates are linked into a ritual geometry until distance, sequence, and causality no longer separate the Outside from the world.

#### Path Progression

1. **Impossible Doors:** Isolated portals open into distant places, other times, or spaces that cannot physically exist.
2. **Keyed Gates:** Cultists acquire people, objects, bloodlines, and formulae that function as keys to specific connections.
3. **Gate Economy:** A stable network transports servants, power, and resources instantly between established sites.
4. **Ritual Geometry:** The placement and activation order of the gates forms a larger invocation.
5. **Topological Collapse:** Distant locations overlap, events occur out of order, and closing one gate redirects power into the others.
6. **The All-in-One:** Yog-Sothoth becomes present as the completed network rather than as a creature entering through one portal.

#### Persistent Footprint

Map every gate, key, destination, connection, activation condition, and known interaction. The important state is the topology of the network and which multi-step combinations remain possible.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Discover where an impossible door truly leads.
- Steal, replace, or impersonate a living key.
- Use the network to reach a site otherwise inaccessible.
- Close gates in an order that does not strengthen the remainder.
- Interrupt a ritual sequence occurring in several places or times.
- Invert the master conjunction from inside the network.

#### Transformation

Win by cutting, discharging, and permanently sealing an indispensable junction, or by redirecting the completed conjunction into verified uninhabited containment and closing its return. Defeating a creature protects its targets; the graph determines whether others can arrive.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Cut and seal:** isolate an indispensable junction, safely discharge its stored power, and permanently seal its circuit function. **Success B — Complete into containment:** key the verified uninhabited destination, redirect the required edges, complete the conjunction there, and close the return connection. B can succeed with all original junctions intact. Both leave unrelated routes available according to the resulting graph.

### 6F. Tsathoggua, the Sleeper: Rest and Preserved Strength

**World Process:** Tribute agreements and real subterranean stores support a coordinated emergence. The enemy conserves resources and prepares interruptions; offers of convenient rest draw people into specific obligations.

**Three acts:** investigate absences and offers → discover reserves and establish independent shelter → exhaust emergence infrastructure or sever tribute. Generate actual caches, inhabitants, supplies, tunnels, target strongpoints, and three sustaining agreements. No sealed multiplier converts missing people into arbitrary strength.

**The Absence Ledger** begins with missing names, cargo, dates, and last known locations. Discoveries add actual cache contents, tribute terms, and prepared responses. Investigation earns ordinary rewards and reduces uncertainty; neither compulsory silence nor an automatic warning schedule overrides real events.

**Player decisions:** accept a priced hospice, borrow a servant, press on, or provision independent shelter. Rest advances actual time. A wizard-servant's prepared interruption is finite, discoverable, and expended when baited; the party does not face unlimited reactive actions.

**Transformation:** remove the assets and conduits necessary for the committed occupation, or free pledged servants and sever all sustaining tribute agreements. Each is sufficient. Enemy victory requires taking and supplying actual strongpoints, not simply revealing a surprise army. Rescued people, stores, and independent shelters remain after an early success.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Exhaust the emergence:** destroy or recover the indispensable stored assets and collapse the dedicated conduits, leaving no legal operation capable of supplying the committed occupation. **Success B — End the claim:** release the pledged servants, cancel all three sustaining tribute agreements, and complete the severance rite, expelling the incursion even with physical caches intact. The agreements cannot be renewed by simply appointing another collector after severance.

### 6G. Ithaqua, the Windwalker: Banked Winter

**World Process:** Supernatural cold accumulates during apparent inactivity and is released through brief, overwhelming seasonal offensives.

#### Path Progression

1. **Unnatural Cold:** Summers shorten, auroras spread, and isolated people disappear during sudden storms.
2. **Glacial Anchors:** Cults and monsters establish sites that preserve cold and extend the reach of winter.
3. **Isolation:** Frozen roads, failed harvests, and advancing glaciers divide settlements and prevent mutual aid.
4. **Hibernation:** The enemy withdraws and appears passive while storing cold, monsters, and movement for the future.
5. **Winter Offensive:** The reserve is released in a compressed eruption of storms, instant glaciers, and attacks from previously dormant forces.
6. **Permanent Winter:** The thaw fails to return and Ithaqua can move freely through a world remade for it.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track glacial anchors, isolated settlements, remaining thaw routes, stored Cold Reserve, hibernating forces, and the expected release conditions. The party should have narrow active seasons in which distant objectives become reachable.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Move food or refugees before a route freezes.
- Destroy an anchor during the brief thaw.
- Identify where a hibernating force is storing its power.
- Provoke an early storm to waste part of the reserve.
- Cross a glacier that exists only during the enemy's offensive.
- Reach the source inside the eye of a supernatural winter.

#### Transformation

Win by permanently ending anchor transmission, or by draining the entire reserve into a prepared sink and severing replenishment during its exposed interval. Either is sufficient. A discharge without severance only buys respite. The actual stored reserve determines offensive strength.

#### The Almanac

Keep this path as a calendar. The antagonist is not the tracker; the year is.

| Year | Weeks of thaw | Reachable in that window | Reserve banked over the winter |
| :--- | :------------ | :----------------------- | :----------------------------- |
| **First** | 9 | Anywhere in the inner ring; the near outer ring at a run | anchors × 1 |
| **Second** | 6 | Inner ring; one outer-ring objective, one way | anchors × 2 |
| **Third** | 3 | The inner ring only, unless a route is held open through the winter | anchors × 3 |

**The thaw is the affordable season.** Outside it, distant objectives require explicit exceptional access: cold protection, a known route or guide, maintained shelters, and twice the normal food/fuel provision. Meeting those conditions makes the expedition possible, not safe. Round-trip weeks and hauling limits remain the planning problem; preparation can buy a winter opportunity.

**Hibernation preserves strength, not invulnerability.** Buried anchors and bedded forces remain reachable through their committed winter approaches when the party meets the access requirements. Otherwise use settlement work, refuge construction, provisioning, and discharge lures. Successful winter operations change actual assets and future reserve growth.

**The offensive spends the reserve, all of it, whether or not it spends it well.** That is the lever. A storm provoked out of season is a storm not available in spring, and a party who can bait a discharge into an empty valley have won something arithmetic and permanent.

**The three winters are the three acts**, and levels track them rather than the reverse: roughly 1–3 in the first year, 4–7 in the second, 8–10 in the third, adjusted to however the party actually spent their weeks.

**Reading.** "Six weeks of thaw this year, and it was nine last year. Two anchors down. Spire Peak is four weeks out and you have not provisioned the Delta."

**If ignored.** The reserve grows on schedule and the arithmetic is public — the party can calculate exactly how much worse they made the coming winter by spending the thaw elsewhere. Then, eventually, the thaw does not arrive at all, and the calendar stops being a planning tool and becomes a record of a year that did not happen.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — End transmission:** destroy all functioning banking/transmission anchors and sever their connection so the system cannot suppress the returning thaw. **Success B — Drain and sever:** lure the whole reserve into the prepared sink and permanently sever the replenishment source during its declared exposed interval. B may leave the anchors intact but inert. Neither simple endurance nor a discharge without permanent severance wins.

Apply these together with population generation; remedies are alternatives and earned early victory remains valid.

### 6H. The Tcho-Tcho Compact: Distributed Control

**World Process:** A decentralized human cult captures territory and institutions through resilient local cells rather than one indispensable colossal entity.

Treat the Compact as a political and religious affiliation, not as a people who are inherently monstrous. It spreads because membership provides material advantages: mutual aid, protection, trade, medicine, secret travel, and access to supernatural patrons.

#### Path Progression

1. **Mutual Aid:** Small cells provide services neglected by existing authorities.
2. **Protected Enclaves:** Members control local streets, villages, markets, or roads and defend one another from outsiders.
3. **Parallel Institutions:** Compact courts, traders, shrines, and militias replace public institutions that have lost trust.
4. **Distributed Authority:** Chapters share expertise, but named priests hold specific collection rights; capturing or persuading one changes that chapter's rights without automatically ending every chapter.
5. **Regional Control:** Interconnected cells dominate movement, resources, and political decisions across broad territory.
6. **Cult Polity:** The Compact becomes the functioning state and openly pays the supernatural price of its prosperity.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track local cells, services each provides, popular support, controlled infrastructure, connections between chapters, known patron bargains, and viable alternatives available to ordinary people.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Discover what a community receives from membership.
- Provide a safer alternative to a Compact-controlled service.
- Persuade a local chapter to defect.
- Disrupt communication without punishing dependent civilians.
- Expose the price demanded by the supernatural patron.
- Prevent several autonomous cells from coordinating a regional action.

#### Transformation

Win by establishing independent provision and terminating collection rights, or by securing collective chapter defection and severance while retaining mundane services and expertise. Each is independently sufficient. A priest's death grants succession only under its committed martyrdom procedure; neither route requires killing the patron.

#### Player Procedures and Independent Victories

**Success A — Independent provision:** replace all five services with operational independent providers, survive the next scheduled collection without losing essential care, and execute the contract's termination procedure so collective tribute is unenforceable. **Success B — Collective defection:** obtain all chapters' valid withdrawal, transfer necessary tools and mundane expertise to their communities, and perform the shared severance rite. B keeps the former chapter providers rather than requiring competing institutions; patron-only benefits have known substitutes before withdrawal. Killing the patron's local avatar is not required by either route.

### 6I. Azathoth: Resonant Awakening

**World Process:** Cultists bring the world into resonance with a mindless cosmic power, causing physical law to fail as the world becomes perceptible to it.

#### Path Progression

1. **Impossible Music:** Shared dreams, repeating equations, and sounds without sources appear among sensitive people.
2. **Resonant Instruments:** Cults construct machines, rituals, dances, towers, and living arrangements that amplify the pattern.
3. **Local Instability:** Gravity, time, matter, and probability become unreliable near active resonators.
4. **Celestial Drift:** Stars and planets move into impossible positions as the resonance affects larger scales.
5. **The Failing Lullaby:** The forces that kept Azathoth dormant can no longer maintain separation.
6. **Attention Without Thought:** The world is exposed to a power that can annihilate it without intention or awareness.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track resonators, harmonics they produce, regions of failed physics, components of any stabilizing counter-pattern, and the astronomical configuration. Azathoth has no strategy; all deliberate opposition comes from its cult and the consequences of physical failure.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Trace impossible music to a resonator.
- Recover part of the stabilizing cosmic pattern.
- Survive a site where one physical law has failed.
- Destroy an instrument without completing its final note.
- Retune a cult structure into part of the counter-pattern.
- Move the world out of alignment before the resonance becomes complete.

#### Transformation

There is no final fight with Azathoth. Win by disconnecting and safely discharging the collectors, or by sustaining a completed counter-pattern through the conjunction to establish permanent separation. Either is sufficient. Useful devices as well as specified spell effects can feed capture; grounding and ordinary magic outside boundaries remain valid.

## 7. Angels: Escalating Judgment

**World Process:** Angels judge actual sins, and the habitual violence of adventurers continually adds evidence, aggravating offences, and slain heavenly servants to the indictment.

The first angel may come only to observe, warn, arrest, or demand restitution. Killing it solves the immediate encounter but proves that the accused answer judgment with further murder. The next response is therefore stronger, broader, and less willing to offer mercy.

### Path Progression

1. **Witnesses:** Devas, visions, sacred animals, or supernatural signs observe the party and other powerful mortals.
2. **Warnings:** Heralds name particular offences and demand cessation, surrender, restitution, or penance.
3. **Individual Warrants:** Angels arrive to apprehend or punish named offenders.
4. **Aggravated Judgment:** Resistance adds contempt, obstruction, desecration, and the slaying of heavenly servants to the charges.
5. **Collective Indictment:** The judges conclude that the party, adventuring companies, a kingdom, or mortal civilization habitually answers wrongdoing with further wrongdoing.
6. **Sentence:** Enforcement expands from individuals to organizations, cities, bloodlines, magical traditions, or humanity itself.

### The Indictment

Record named offences rather than an abstract morality score:

- The act committed
- The victim or injured community
- Surviving witnesses
- Harm that can still be repaired
- The judge assigned to the charge
- Aggravating acts committed during enforcement
- Genuine restitution, restraint, mercy, or repentance

A murder cannot be erased by spending atonement points. The party must address what it did, the reasons it did it, and the people who suffered. Some charges may be false or exceed celestial jurisdiction, but inconvenient truth should not automatically prove that Heaven is corrupt.

### Escalating Enforcement

Each failed intervention changes the next response:

- A witness is replaced by an investigator.
- An ignored herald is replaced by an arresting angel.
- A slain arresting angel is replaced by a judge and armed escort.
- A destroyed tribunal produces multiple executioners.
- Repeated celestial casualties support a collective sentence.

This creates increasingly dangerous combat while making combat alone incapable of resolving the path.

### Recurring Adventures

- Investigate whether a named charge is true.
- Find survivors of an expedition the party believed it had killed.
- Return treasure to the community harmed in acquiring it.
- Protect an accused companion while seeking evidence or restitution.
- Complete a difficult penance requested by a victim rather than by the judges.
- Prevent another adventuring company from worsening humanity's case.
- Recover divine precedent concerning mercy, redemption, necessity, or proportional punishment.
- Confront a judge whose sentence exceeds its authority.
- Reach the divine court or source before a collective sentence is issued.

### Judicial Variants

- **Devas:** Investigators capable of recognizing context, repentance, and moral change.
- **Devils:** Adversarial prosecutors who exploit true offences, contracts, technical guilt, and concealed clauses for corrupt ends.
- **Lords of Law and Wind Dukes:** Judges concerned with categorical violations, cosmic allegiance, and destabilizing uses of Chaos.
- **Inevitables:** Enforcement beings assigned to a defined violation and incapable of abandoning it through ordinary persuasion.
- **Aleax:** A personal judgment created for one accused individual, mirroring that person's abilities and sins.

### Transformation

The party may make restitution, demonstrate genuine change, disprove particular charges, establish that the sentence exceeds divine authority, invoke a higher principle of mercy, accept a proportionate punishment, change the heavenly verdict, or stop the power issuing an irredeemably unjust sentence.

### Marut Variant: Correction of the Preserved World

The Marut variant is a separate enforcement process rather than ordinary judgment for personal sin.

Previous heroes saved the world when the natural order required that it end and be replaced. The present world is therefore a cosmic holdover, and everyone alive exists within the consequences of an ancient violation of succession.

#### Path Progression

1. **Isolated Corrections:** A Marut destroys an immortal, restored bloodline, preserved city, or artifact connected to the ancient salvation.
2. **The Impossible Offence:** It declares that the continued world itself violates natural order, but does not explain the forgotten act that caused the violation.
3. **Recursive Enforcement:** Destroying one Marut demonstrates that the assigned force was insufficient; two or more replacements descend.
4. **Widening Correction:** Enforcement expands from individuals to institutions, civilizations, natural laws, and protections created by the old heroes.
5. **The Root Cause:** The party reconstructs how the previous heroes prevented the intended ending and what successor world was denied.
6. **World Succession:** The enforcement source begins dismantling the preserved world so its replacement can finally occur.

#### Persistent Footprint

Track Maruts destroyed, replacements assigned, targets corrected, surviving works of the old heroes, evidence explaining the original intervention, and steps completed toward world succession. Direct resistance should visibly multiply later enforcement rather than merely fill a hidden clock.

#### Recurring Adventures

- Defend something whose connection to the ancient violation is not yet understood.
- Recover testimony or records left by the heroes who saved the world.
- Learn why a person, city, species, or natural law has been marked for correction.
- Survive multiple Maruts generated by an earlier victory.
- Reach a target before the enforcers and determine whether preserving it is worth further escalation.
- Explore the unrealized successor world.
- Locate the source that authorizes and manufactures the corrections.

#### Transformation

The party must eventually address the cause rather than its multiplying agents. It can permit the intended succession, prove that the preserved world has acquired legitimate standing, negotiate a merger with the denied successor, rewrite the cosmic law, or reach and disable the power issuing the corrections.

Other Inevitable paths can use the same recursive structure: slow cumulative enforcement, agents replaced when destroyed, effects widening over time, and victory requiring discovery of the original violation or intervention at the source.

## 8. The Witch King: Conversion

**World Process:** Every uncontained loss becomes new strength for the enemy.

The Witch King's army is an infection rather than a conventional host. Casualties create undead; undead cause displacement; displaced populations carry infection and overwhelm safe settlements; collapsed settlements provide corpses, graveyards, and abandoned fortifications for the next advance.

### Path Progression

1. **Unexplained Dead:** Isolated corpses rise, burial sites are disturbed, and authorities conceal early incidents.
2. **Local Outbreaks:** Villages or armies suffer attacks that create more attackers than they destroy.
3. **Flight and Contagion:** Refugees, contaminated relics, hidden bites, and unburned bodies carry the crisis beyond its origin.
4. **Cascading Collapse:** Roads close, harvests fail, quarantines turn violent, and abandoned settlements become corpse reservoirs.
5. **The Dead March:** Converted territory supports organized undead armies and living collaborators.
6. **Kingdom of the Dead:** The Witch King possesses enough bodies, strongholds, and necromantic infrastructure to replace living society.

### Persistent Footprint

Track named populations and places rather than one abstract infection score:

- Infected settlements
- Refugee movements
- Graveyards and battlefields
- Necromantic nexuses
- Remaining healers, sanctuaries, and defensible roads
- Known transmission methods

### Recurring Adventures

- Hold a bridge or mountain pass long enough for an evacuation.
- Identify the true transmission method.
- Recover a healer, relic, or rare reagent needed for a cure.
- Destroy a graveyard before an approaching army can raise it.
- Quarantine an ally who may still be saved.
- Choose which of several threatened communities can be defended.
- Break the infrastructure that allows the Witch King to command the converted dead.

### Fungal Variant

A fungal or Zuggtmoy-like version replaces the local ecology instead of merely animating casualties. Its victims may remain communicative or apparently content before fruiting. The campaign must determine whether transformed communities can be cured, can coexist with the uninfected, or have become parts of a single hostile organism.

### Transformation

Destroying the Witch King does not end an independent infection. The path is resolved only when transmission has been broken, reservoirs have been contained, and the surviving communities can remain alive without constant intervention.

## 9. Ragnaros and the Apocalypse Cult: Invocation

**World Process:** Worship, sacrifice, and logistics construct the god's presence in the world.

The apocalypse is not passively approaching. The cult must acquire fuel, relics, sites, sacrifices, elemental conjunctions, secrets, or other components. Each completed component makes the eventual manifestation easier to invoke and more powerful when it arrives.

### Path Progression

1. **Recruitment:** Prophets offer power, belonging, revenge, survival, or revelation to communities in crisis.
2. **Acquisition:** Cult cells seek specific relics, victims, locations, and sources of supernatural fuel.
3. **Consecration:** Captured places are changed into furnaces, plague gardens, mutation shrines, or ritual nodes.
4. **Convergence:** Components and cult factions move toward the final site while lesser manifestations appear.
5. **Partial Embodiment:** The apocalyptic power acts through champions, weather, disease, fire, or unstable avatars.
6. **Incarnation:** The cult completes a durable body or world-scale medium through which its power can remain.

### Persistent Footprint

Record the ritual as a set of concrete components rather than a single countdown. Each component should state:

- Who possesses it
- Where it must be used
- What capability it grants the manifestation
- Whether it can be stolen, destroyed, corrupted, or replaced

The cult may continue after losing a component by seeking an inferior substitute. Player victories therefore change the nature of the apocalypse rather than merely delaying a fixed encounter.

### Recurring Adventures

- Raid a supply chain or reliquary.
- Rescue living sacrifices.
- Force the cult to expend a component prematurely.
- Defend or desecrate a required ritual site.
- Turn rival cult factions against one another.
- Corrupt the ritual's destination or intended vessel.
- Permit a controllable partial manifestation to prevent a complete incarnation.

### Cult Variants

- **Fire:** Territory, industry, sacrifice, and destruction become fuel. Each blaze makes the next one easier to begin.
- **Plague:** Disease, despair, fatalism, and the willing acceptance of decay sustain the invocation.
- **Death:** An Orcus-style cult, a necromantic church, or another death patron assembles a vessel from stolen dead, funerary authority, and a binding rite. Specify which material or consent is actually necessary; corpses, memories, and souls are not interchangeable fuel.
- **Magic and Change:** Secrets, betrayals, mutations, and interlocking schemes assemble a reality-altering design.

Nurgle-inspired plague, fire incarnations, Orcus and other death cults, and change cults have separate antagonist dossiers in the [population procedures](08_adventure_path_population.md#9-apocalypse-cult-build-the-actual-manifestation). The bill of materials determines the resulting apocalypse. A plague whose main process is continuing conversion instead uses the Witch King engine; the patron's name alone does not choose the method.

### Transformation

The finale depends on what the cult successfully assembled. The party might prevent incarnation, redirect it into an unsuitable vessel, destroy it while incomplete, or survive a diminished manifestation whose missing components create exploitable weaknesses.

## 10. The Eternal Cycle: Polarization

> Worked in full as [Adventure Path 3: The Eternal Cycle](../adventure_paths/03_the_vanishing_middle.md).

**World Process:** The war between Law and Chaos makes every victory dangerous and drives the world toward an absolute state.

Law can preserve a city while advancing universal stagnation. Chaos can overthrow a tyrant while loosening identity, causality, and form. Neither faction needs to be secretly evil: the catastrophe emerges because limited alliances and defensible local decisions accumulate into cosmic extremity.

### Path Progression

1. **Proxy Struggles:** Mortal factions invoke Law or Chaos to win local conflicts.
2. **Alignment:** Governments, cults, heroes, monsters, and territories are forced to declare allegiance.
3. **Arms Race:** Each side deploys more absolute servants, artifacts, and transformations in response to the other.
4. **Eternal Cycle:** Neutral places, mixed institutions, compromises, and ordinary mortal life become impossible to sustain.
5. **Cosmic War:** Lords of Law and Chaos act openly through armies and champions.
6. **Ragnarok:** One principle becomes absolute, or the combined violence of both sides destroys the world they sought to control.

### Persistent Footprint

Use the worked path's **Law's Hold**, **Chaos's Hold**, and **twelve named things in the Middle**. Each accepted intervention can move a named thing into a pole's Hold; keep the deed and its cost on the struck list. War intensity is visible in matched interventions and their consequences, not a competing third progress track. The danger lies at either extreme and in the escalation produced by their opposition.

### Recurring Adventures

- Accept limited aid from one side without surrendering independence.
- Sabotage an artifact that would give either power an absolute advantage.
- Preserve a neutral city, sanctuary, or institution.
- Rescue a champion from the cosmic role imposed upon them.
- Reverse a local victory whose wider consequence is intolerable.
- Find evidence of an earlier cycle and learn why its settlement failed.
- Search for Balance or an entirely new principle.

### Transformation

The party may restore a temporary Balance, introduce a third cosmic principle, break the mechanism that forces history to repeat, choose the next world deliberately, or accept one form of apocalypse to prevent another.

## 11. Githyanki Refugees: Displacement

**World Process:** Survival pressure becomes a security dilemma between displaced newcomers and established societies.

The Githyanki are not inherently the villains of this path. Their home is collapsing, and each new arrival increases the need for land, food, security, and political authority. Local hostility makes militarization appear necessary; visible militarization makes local hostility rational. Predatory leaders can exploit both reactions until refuge becomes conquest.

This engine also supports **orc migration through a portal in the shape of the Warcraft arrival scenario**. A warlord or warlock may turn the need for a homeland into conquest; resident rulers may exploit fear to seize land or labor. Generate rival factions among newcomers and residents rather than making an entire people the antagonist. Choose why return is unavailable, who controls the portal, and whether its controller needs conquest. The [three-act procedure and antagonist options](08_adventure_path_population.md#11-githyanki-refugees-a-settlement-that-can-feed-itself) distinguish reception, continuing waves, and a durable settlement or conquest.

### Path Progression

1. **First Arrivals:** Scouts, wounded warriors, and isolated families appear through unstable crossings.
2. **Emergency Enclaves:** Refugees fortify temporary camps and compete for scarce supplies.
3. **Continuing Waves:** The scale of the catastrophe becomes apparent as more crossings open.
4. **Security Crisis:** Raids, reprisals, infiltration fears, and cultural conflict divide both populations.
5. **Competing Futures:** Githyanki factions advocate integration, isolation, conquest, return, or renewed war for their lost world.
6. **Sovereignty:** A permanent political settlement is imposed or negotiated, or the conflict becomes a war of survival.

### Persistent Footprint

Track pressures that can move independently:

- Number and location of arrivals
- Food, land, and magical resources available
- Trust between communities
- Militarization and political radicalization
- Stability of planar crossings
- Capacity of any refuge, resettlement plan, or return route

### Recurring Adventures

- Secure food or habitable territory before another arrival wave.
- Negotiate between frightened settlements and militant refugee leaders.
- Expose an atrocity intended to provoke retaliation.
- Close a dangerous crossing without condemning those still behind it.
- Explore the collapsing home dimension for survivors or a remedy.
- Defeat raiders without treating every refugee as an enemy.
- Decide whether limited resources should support integration, resettlement, or return.

### Transformation

Possible conclusions include a shared homeland, peaceful settlement, partition, a repaired home plane, a new Githyanki conquest state, forced expulsion, or mutual destruction. Which ending is possible depends upon the material and political conditions created throughout the campaign, not solely upon the defeat of one leader.

## 12. The Slumbering Catastrophe: Preparation and Awakening

**World Process:** A dormant destroyer regains its capabilities as specific operations disturb its sleep. The party gains experience, weapons, protection, and allies through expeditions, then chooses when to attempt the kill before full awakening permits a regional rampage.

**Antagonists:** An **ancient red dragon** can recover its hoard, breath, and freedom of movement before pursuing revenge or dominion. A **tarrasque** wakes through physical or magical disturbance and follows its established appetites and movement behavior. An ancient war colossus is a further option with power cores and command beacons. Choose the dossier's true sleep condition, awakening triggers, defenses, and a possible killing method before generating the preparation sites.

### Three Acts

| Act | Approximate levels | Principal play |
| :-- | :----------------- | :------------- |
| **I — The Mountain Breathes** | 1–3 | Discover the sleeper and what wakes it; establish why ordinary attacks fail and identify the previous hunt's evidence |
| **II — The Weapons of the Last Hunt** | 4–7 | Gain experience, obtain or forge effective weapons, test countermeasures, recruit support, and interrupt feasible waking operations |
| **III — The Killing Expedition** | 8–10 | Strike the vulnerable sleeper using earned advantages, or intercept its actual rampage if it awakens first |

Preparation can justify an earlier attempt. Full awakening changes the final expedition's circumstances; it neither instantly destroys the world nor invalidates acquired weapons. The creature's established threat does not scale upward just because the party gain levels.

### Persistent Footprint

Keep two related records:

- **Awakening operations:** actors or committed natural events, sites, prerequisites, completion, recovered capabilities, and observable tells. Possible operations breach a seal, restore a furnace, return stolen strength, break a restraint, sound a calling, or open the exit.
- **Preparation:** the party's actual experience and resources; known vulnerabilities; acquired and tested weapons; defensive equipment; allies and weapon crews; provisions; staging routes; and a validated finishing method.

Dormant, Stirring, and Awake describe behavior, not extra acts. Full awakening and escape require the dossier's actual alertness, mobility, and exit conditions. A stopped mine or disrupted rite remains stopped until a plausible operation replaces it. Time elsewhere does not awaken a sealed creature by itself.

### Recurring Adventures

- Rescue miners before their excavation breaks the first seal.
- Recover a lance, siege instrument, or account from a failed earlier hunt.
- Locate and test a wound or weakness before risking the final expedition.
- Obtain a scarce forging material without sacrificing an inhabited place.
- Protect the smith, ritual specialist, or weapon crew from the waking faction.
- Secure protection from breath, trampling, collapse, or the chosen creature's defenses.
- Rebind a limb or close an exit to buy time for another expedition.
- Provision an evacuation and move a heavy weapon into an interception position.
- Strike before a known capability is restored, accepting the risk of an unfinished preparation.

### The Two Conditions and Transformation

The party's intended victory is **slaying the creature with an effective finishing method**. The dossier must make that achievable through discoverable preparations within this campaign. Permanent rebinding, banishment, or a sapient dragon's negotiated settlement can be chosen alternatives with explicit conditions; they do not replace the possibility of the hunt. A mindless tarrasque does not receive a negotiation route by default.

The catastrophe prevails when its actual rampage destroys or captures the committed regional objectives and the surviving communities cannot sustain resistance. Waking merely makes that outcome possible. Movement, evacuation, and destruction occur at mapped places with real travel intervals. Early slaying leaves hoard claims, dangerous remains, surviving servants, and debts to those who supplied the expedition. Defeat leaves a ruined region and the possibility of another hunt. The Toll retains named settlements, crews, habitats, and evacuees lost during preparation or the rampage.

The [full population procedure](08_adventure_path_population.md#12-slumbering-catastrophe-slay-it-before-the-rampage) specifies waking operations, dragon and tarrasque preparations, opening leads, encounter construction, and the resulting endings.

## 13. The Stolen Dawn: Sustained Catastrophe

**World Process:** A sovereign repeatedly renews an already-established supernatural catastrophe. Survival preserves communities while the party discovers and executes one of several independently sufficient remedies.

**Three acts:** town and nearby sites (levels 1–3), wilderness (4–7), underground (8–10). This uses exactly two Cursed Scroll profiles: the Isles of Andrik for both surface acts, then Morzomotha & Karst Deeps for the underground act. Separate town, wilderness, and deep territory instances do not imply three Cursed Scroll profiles. Surface geography and consequences remain continuous.

**Antagonists:** the Pale Regent renews winter through a unique aurora roc; the Mourning Queen uses an irreplaceable horizon bell and star lens. Each has a different discoverable personal vulnerability and permanent means of disabling renewal.

**Player success:** defeat the sovereign **OR** permanently disable its renewal dependency **OR** establish the buried Dawn Engine through a full commissioning night. Each alone ends winter. The engine route can succeed with the sovereign alive and its renewal equipment intact. A temporary missed casting only buys daylight until the next renewal.

**Records and counterplay:** keep nightly renewal facts, settlement food/fuel and route access, and three solution records. Expeditions recover evidence, preserve provisions, obtain effective weapons, reach the renewal site, or restore underground machinery. Enemy operations require actual actors, resources, and travel. There is no seasonal Cold Reserve or generic Doom advancement.

**Enemy success:** all authored surface communities are destroyed, abandoned, or bound into submission, no supplied independent refuge remains, renewal is still possible, and no player victory has occurred. Early victory preserves the returning dawn; the remaining geography supports reconstruction, salvage, and stewardship. The Toll retains losses under every ending.

The [full path dossier](../adventure_paths/04_the_stolen_dawn.md) supplies three acts, two antagonist dossiers, independent evidence, concrete success predicates, site roles, and generation acceptance criteria. This is authored design, not runtime registration.

## Using These Engines with Campaign Pressure

An Adventure Path generates several ordinary pressures at once. Their consequences update the path's persistent footprint.

For example, a Bane path might create **The Iron Prefect seizes the grain road**, an Escalation Ladder. If that pressure reaches its consequence, the result is not one point of generic Doom: control of food and trade passes to the regime. The next pressures follow from that changed situation—ration registration, a smuggling opportunity, resistance dependence upon foreign supplies, or a frightened temple considering collaboration.

Likewise, a Witch King path might contain separate pressures for an infected refugee column, an unguarded battlefield, and a threatened healer. The path advances according to which of those situations the party changes or neglects. It does not progress merely because a session ended.
