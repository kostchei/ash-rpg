# Populating Adventure Path Engines

Status: **expanded campaign design; not a statement of runtime support.** This companion to [Adventure Path Engines](07_adventure_path_engines.md) turns all twenty-two engines, including the nine outer-power methods, Marut variant, Slumbering Catastrophe, and Stolen Dawn, into procedures for generating connected campaign content. Every engine has three acts and at least two possible antagonists. The four standalone paths remain their detailed authored sources; the nine outer-power engines additionally use the [player mechanics companion](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md). Use the [shared exploration process](../plans/adventure_path_exploration_process.md) to carry this content through taverns, travel, sites, and consequences.

**Continuing quests:** [Objectives, clues, and replenishing leads](11_objectives_clues_and_lead_spawning.md) defines the minimum two path opportunities plus one unrelated lead, saved d6 replenishment, field and tavern discovery, and per-engine objective recipes. This is a design contract pending runtime integration.

## From an Engine to a Saved Campaign

Choose or secretly select the engine once. Generate its underlying truth before its rumors: the people pursuing it, resources they need, places they can reach, and concrete conditions under which they succeed. Secret selection hides identity and unlearned causes; it must not hide a danger, price, or practical requirement that the characters have already established.

The procedures below are a baseline campaign for each engine. Named examples are replaceable; their relationships and causal rules are not. New requirement tables are authored defaults for engines whose framework previously supplied only a progression outline. They are not retroactive descriptions of implemented mechanics.

### Three Acts, Multiple Antagonists

Every engine has **exactly three campaign acts**, providing material for levels 1–10: roughly levels 1–3, 4–7, and 8–10. Advancement within those acts uses the engine's own deeds, calendar, or conditions. A five-state restoration process is not five acts; repeated manifestations or arrival waves occur within the three-act campaign. Level bands budget content and threat; they never prevent an earned early victory or force an enemy success.

Choose an **antagonist dossier separately from its method**. Each option below changes the source of opposition, its motive, its dependencies, and the means of countering it. Complete those differences before generating situations. Names from other settings are adaptation options, not claims that those settings share ASH's exact mechanics. Original alternatives are campaign seeds, not established setting lore.

The outer-power entries are nine methods, not nine exclusive god assignments. A power can use orchestration in one campaign and conjunction in another. Choose one primary method and at most one supporting method with a stated dependency; do not run two unrelated full progress systems against the party. The primary method determines the three acts and ending. For example, Nyarlathotep can manipulate patrons into building Yog-Sothoth's gate geometry: orchestration explains the missions, while closing the committed circuit remains the finale condition.

An antagonist need not be a final monster. The Eternal Cycle has opposed patrons; Displacement can have predatory refugee or resident leaders; Judgment can end in a verdict. A dossier must say who actively opposes the party and what that opposition wants, without declaring an entire population the enemy.

### Generation Order

1. **Choose the conflict and its limits.** Write the antagonist's achievable world state and at least two ways the party could resolve it. Identify the engine's special constraint: invitation, feed, jurisdiction, build order, season, or another concrete limit.
2. **Reserve the necessary geography.** Place the opening community, intermediate operations, and final access requirements. Check that roads, water, gates, underground passages, and travel times support the operations attributed to them. A later destination may be unknown to the party but must already have a feasible connection.
3. **Create the people and institutions.** Give each a present need, knowledge, resources, loyalties, and a reason to occupy its location. Include an independent faction and someone who benefits from the antagonist without understanding or endorsing its full purpose.
4. **Instantiate requirements.** Each requirement records its actor, target, prerequisites, enabling route or resource, completed deed, and granted capability. Four to eight per engine is the usual range; independent Titans use their own Release Deeds. A list of names without placed operations is not a populated path.
5. **Place the opening situations.** Create two distinct path opportunities and one ordinary regional opportunity, each with a different actionable destination. They may share an underlying operation. Give them independent sources and different kinds of intervention; three rumors about the same room are one opportunity.
6. **Derive the rumors and evidence.** Every claim points to an existing situation and a source with a reason to know it. Distortion changes what the source says, not established world truth. Provide three independent evidence routes for every indispensable conclusion; a lost witness must not make the campaign insoluble.
7. **Save truth, knowledge, and path state separately.** Record when a claim was heard, how a fact was established, and whether an old report has become stale. Revisiting a site develops its saved state rather than generating a replacement.

### Content Budget and Detail

Use the framework's three-act budget: **6–8 / 10–12 / 6–8 sites**, **3 / 5 / 4 set-piece events**, and **2–3 / 4–5 / 3–4 standing problems**. The examples below start that budget; they do not constitute all ten levels of content. Expand each requirement into acquisition, intervention, and consequence opportunities, with at least one problem that cannot be solved by clearing rooms. A revisited site counts toward a later act only when its changed situation supports substantial new play.

Reserve important sites, identities, links, and finale conditions early. Populate individual rooms, local encounters, and incidental treasure when first needed, then persist them. At least half the generated people and places remain ordinary regional content. Do not mark every eccentric NPC as a clue or make every local dispute secretly serve the path.

Choose encounters for the site's inhabitants, task, and established threat. Use the standalone path's encounter limits where supplied. Telegraph exceptional danger and provide opportunities to investigate, bargain, evade, or retreat. A generated party is not guaranteed to defeat every placed force by fighting it.

### The Situation Contract

| Record | Required content |
| :----- | :--------------- |
| Identity | Stable situation ID, engine instance, current state, and related requirement or party objective |
| World links | Named actors, site and location, routes, resources, and dependent situations |
| Present action | What someone is doing now; why; what they need to finish |
| Discovery | Rumor sources, observable evidence, hidden evidence, and how each can be learned |
| Choices | At least two materially different interventions, including a practical alternative to combat |
| Trigger | The actual deed, scheduled event, or engine-specific opportunity that changes the situation |
| Consequences | Changes for success, compromise, enemy completion, and abandonment; named beneficiaries and costs |
| Continuation | A follow-up grounded in the changed world, with a real target and a source who can know about it |

Resolve a completed deed once. A requirement's accomplishment stays in history even if its capability is subsequently disabled; record both facts. Restore a capability only through an authored repair operation. The Toll preserves permanent losses even when survivors rebuild. Do not award progress for merely entering a hex, hearing the same clue again, reconnecting, or ending a session.

### Time, Knowledge, and Endings

The engine's explicit trigger overrides any general neglect procedure. Seasonal engines use their calendar; reactive engines require an actual response; concealed accumulation does not produce invented warnings. When no special rule is supplied, an abandoned, actionable situation gives its actor one opportunity to complete an eligible requirement using the resources and route it actually has. Three such ignored opportunities may produce background transformation only if the engine's world-state condition can now be fulfilled. Three unrelated expeditions cannot conjure missing materials or overthrow an inaccessible city.

Keep hidden causes hidden, but preserve established evidence and announce observable consequences. For a no-GM table, concealed facts and triggers are committed in saved records or sealed material before they matter. Do not decide after a player choice that it secretly served an enemy plan. Give discoverable rules a physical demonstration before requiring players to stake the campaign on them; the Sleeper's explicitly concealed scale is a deliberate exception, not a license to alter it later.

An ending follows fulfilled conditions, not an act number. Early resolution can lead to the listed inheritance or overreach; do not invalidate the win to ensure a predetermined catastrophe. Failure creates the transformed setting described by the engine. Continuing after either is a new situation in that world, not a reset of the previous record.

## 1. Captive Domain — A Prison with Rules

**Antagonist options.** Use any of the 38 Domains of Dread dossiers. A **vampire sovereign** maintains an undying reign through invited feeding and stolen burial rites; a **ghost admiral** holds a looping coast through an uncompleted betrayal and a spectral flagship; a **fey creditor** imprisons settlements through inherited promises. They require different laws, anchors, evidence, and resolution conditions, not interchangeable boss statistics.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Survive | Establish shelter and prove how the prison's laws operate |
| II — Identify | Reconstruct the captor's Fall, identify its mask, and locate its protections |
| III — Arm and Overthrow | Assemble and use leverage, then resolve the captivity under the dossier's conditions |

**Generate.** Use [Domains of Dread](../adventure_paths/01_domains_of_dread.md) for the boss dossier, Fall, protections, and resolution matrix. Place Threshold, Dominion, and Heart within a finite map whose outer routes loop. Name three initial havens, three demonstrable laws, the captor's feeding route, and every anchor. Give each law a safe way to observe it and a costly way to exploit it. The six existing Terms remain the captor's requirements.

**Opening pair.** At Bellwater, a widow hires the party to escort a coffin that returns to her doorstep each dawn. The coffin contains evidence of a broken burial oath. Separately, a miller needs a missing apprentice recovered from the lord's annual feast; the apprentice is to reenact the original Victim's role. Escorting the coffin tests a law; intervening at the feast interrupts feeding or reenactment. Ordinary timber work leads to an independent forester.

**Develop the acts.** Survive by proving laws and securing shelter. Identify by comparing the coffin inscription, a former servant's account, and the feast's guest register. Arm and Overthrow by restoring the burial oath, obtaining the Victim's consent or testimony where the dossier permits it, and reaching the Heart under the resulting condition. A sworn haven is an ongoing obligation to protect people, not a checkpoint.

**Resolve.** Record each Term only when its deed occurs. Relocating an anchor requires a carrier and route and leaves evidence; it does not rewrite the party's earlier discovery. Assembling the Killing Condition enables the confrontation; using it or fulfilling an authored alternative ends the captivity. Destroyed havens and accepted Marks remain in the Toll. If the captor falls early, preserving the unmoored domain becomes the inheritance. Transformation or succession must follow the dossier's stated terms; it is not an automatic escape loophole.

## 2. Aboleth Savant — A Network of Held Minds

**Antagonist options.** An **aboleth savant** acquires minds through aquatic transport and psychic installations. An **illithid elder brain** uses implanted agents, feeding houses, and a neural colony: replace aquatic access with protection from psychic detection and a route through the colony's living defenses. A **possessing ancestral council** controls descendants through funerary masks; liberation requires separating inherited memory from compulsory obedience. Each uses the same investigation of held minds but different exposure, relay, protection, and liberation rules. The underwater guarantees below apply to the aboleth dossier.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Altered Loyalties | Establish that apparently voluntary actions share an external controller |
| II — The Control Network | Trace transport and relays; acquire protection and a feasible approach |
| III — Break the Holding | Disable support capabilities and liberate, deceive, isolate, or confront the controlling intelligence |

**Generate.** Use [The Night Below](../adventure_paths/02_the_mind_below.md) for the 27 environment combinations, four tracks, aquatic access, and installations. Select the Savant's need before selecting its victims. Place recruiter, transport lieutenant, warden, dissident, three recurring controlled people, and the Chorus, Memory Well, and Pressure Heart. Link each surface victim to a destination and a use.

| Requirement | Completion and capability |
| :---------- | :------------------------ |
| Secure a recruiter | A trusted intermediary willingly serves or is controlled; it can select victims from a named institution |
| Open the transport | A usable route and operator move a first captive; repeat transfers become possible |
| Seat the Chorus | Its operators and relay are installed; connected thralls can coordinate |
| Feed the Memory Well | A needed captive memory reaches it; the Savant gains the corresponding knowledge and recovery support |
| Prime the Pressure Heart | Its power source is connected; it can control currents at the final site |
| Capture the intake | A named public institution exposes its population to the prepared control method; mass submission can be attempted |

These deeds feed the authored tracks; do not replace the tracks with a second numerical score. A value of six means an operation is ready or imminent, not that its final deed happened automatically.

**Opening pair.** Rescue a surveyor held at the waterworks, or investigate a healer returning patients with identical memories. The surveyor knows a transport route; the healer's supply labels identify a different relay. Neither requires killing the controlled victims.

**Develop and resolve.** Follow transport into the chosen caves, obtain protection and at least three independently sourced access options before the drowned act, then disable or repurpose installation capabilities. A rescue updates the named captive and supplies an actual descent lead. Verification of an NPC goes stale only after a plausible exposure, with its last verification retained. Network liberation, deception, sealing, negotiated transfer, and voluntary succession use the authored endings; killing the body leaves surviving relays and their claimants in play.

## 3. Titans — Five Prisons, One Divided Response

**Antagonist options.** A **storm Titan's revanchist herald** wants the organs and weapon returned regardless of mortal casualties. A **divine jailer** wants to preserve the stolen world and erase all evidence of its crime. A **primordial fire matriarch** offers a selective settlement but treats present cities as temporary growth over her body. Choose who drives the initial release or suppression operations; neither all Titans nor all gods are automatically allies or enemies.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — The Map of Chains | Follow servant operations and learn where the prison system is embedded in the world |
| II — The Gods' Crime | Discover the theft, compare grievances, and choose which functions to replace or defend |
| III — War of Succession | Rebind, free, confront, or reconcile powers while preserving a viable world |

**Generate.** Create five Titans at different restoration states. Give each a grievance, a servant faction, a negotiable demand, one stolen organ or authority, and a chain sustaining a named benefit of the present world. Start no more than two release operations in immediate reach; the remaining prisons have discoverable records and later routes. Use the existing Release Deeds, assigning each to a real site and a specific power.

**Opening pair.** Giant petitioners offer payment for a name erased from a monastery archive. Downriver, a temple requests protection for the bronze lung that keeps the valley's air breathable. Both concern the same Titan, but restoring its name need not mean returning the lung. The archivist knows the original theft; a giant descendant knows its cost; temple machinery demonstrates the present dependency.

**Requirements and conditions.** A Titan cannot gain a returned organ's power without acquiring it. Naming enables communication; a broken chain or returned organ enables Stirring; the stolen weapon enables Armed; opening the final prison enables Incarnate. The revenge faction wins this baseline when an Incarnate, Armed Titan destroys the succession seat and no replacement for its world-sustaining function exists. A merely Named Titan is not a world-ending failure. The party can maintain selected bindings, secure a witnessed settlement, or transfer stolen functions into independent structures before freeing their owners.

**Develop and resolve.** Preserve the five restoration states, but organize the earlier five narrative movements into three campaign acts: Servants and Map of Chains; Gods' Crime and Returning Powers; War of Succession. Standing problems concern which divine benefit can be replaced first and who pays for it. A restored lung may cure a Titan while suffocating a valley; the substitution project must be discoverable before that decision. Neglect advances only a supplied servant operation. After an early settlement, rival gods contest its enforcement. Lost natural features and displaced communities remain in the Toll.

## 4. Tharizdun — Repairing Distinctions

**Antagonist options.** **Tharizdun's unmaking cults** deliberately sever the world's Bonds to release their prisoner. **The Unnamed Archivist**, an original intelligence erased from history, destroys the distinction between names and things to regain existence. **The Mercy of One Flesh**, an original communal power, abolishes self and other to end loneliness. Each chooses different first Bonds and offers a different tempting reason to abandon a distinction.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Contradictions | Identify a damaged Bond and demonstrate a repair |
| II — The Cascade | Map dependencies and address conflicting repair costs before breaches combine |
| III — Remake the Boundary | Restore or replace the load-bearing distinction that separates the world from uncreation |

**Generate.** Put the eight Bonds on a regional dependency map. For each, name a material anchor, a social practice that maintains it, and a specific operation that can damage it. Select three initial dependencies, such as Names sustaining Promises and Promises sustaining Consequences; do not allow every damaged Bond to damage every other one. Preserve original records beside altered versions.

| Requirement | Deed and permanent opening |
| :---------- | :------------------------- |
| Confound Names | Replace a community's naming anchor with contradictory originals; identities can overlap there |
| Empty Promises | Complete an oath-breaking rite at its covenant site; contracts lose their binding distinction |
| Unseat Death | Keep a death anchor suspended between rites; the dead cannot finish dying |
| Fold Distance | Join incompatible boundary stones; separated locations can intrude upon each other |
| Reverse Consequence | Complete a sacrifice before its initiating act through an opened contradiction; causal order fails locally |

**Opening pair.** Two people possess the same perfectly authenticated inheritance; both want help finding the original parish roll. At another site, a funeral procession cannot leave a bridge because its dead passenger is still attending court upstream. Independent inscription, testimony, and physical remains reveal which Bond is failing.

**Develop and resolve.** Act I identifies and repairs one contradiction. Act II makes repairs compete: restoring a single legal identity could erase another person's claim to home and kin. Act III rebuilds the prison around a chosen surviving distinction. Each broken Bond exposes only its named dependencies to a new operation. Three simultaneously broken Bonds in one region create uncreation there. The party wins by restoring a load-bearing Bond and isolating its dependent breaches, or constructing an equivalent new boundary that prevents the three-Bond condition. Publish the needed sacrifice before accepting it. Neglected breaches spread along recorded dependencies; an early repair leaves a new institution charged with keeping the distinction meaningful.

## 5. Bane — Who Can Enforce the Writ?

**Antagonist options.** A **Banite prefect** seeks lasting obedience through emergency decrees. An **infernal chancellor** makes food and security conditional on enforceable contracts, with claims surviving the signer. A **mortal military protector** sincerely prevents civil war while eliminating independent institutions. Choose whether counterplay primarily contests public legitimacy, contractual reach, or dependence on military supply; killing the officeholder is insufficient in all three.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Disorder | Investigate the crises used to justify exceptional authority |
| II — Emergency | Preserve functioning alternatives while decrees capture institutions |
| III — Normalcy | Break the writ's practical reach and sustain communities that refuse it |

**Generate.** Place three settlements, their grain road, an issuing court, a garrison, a temple, a registry, and independent suppliers. Give each service a provider and a practical dependency. Assign the existing six Decrees to crises the regime could exploit; a decree needs an issuer, a public pretext, and a means of enforcement.

**Opening pair.** Escort grain whose disappearance would justify ration registration, or defend a night-watch witness who can prove that the Prefect hired the attackers. One destination is a blocked depot; the other is a threatened sanctuary. A mill cooperative offers ordinary repair work that can later become a real alternative supplier.

**Make Compliance concrete.** Record each Decree's named constituencies and observed voluntary compliance. A frightened crowd alone does not prove a whole province compliant. The framework's violent-success increase records one additional constituency adopting a practice, with the evidence stated. Four Decrees count toward Bane's victory only when each is enforced and voluntarily observed in its declared constituency. An already functioning alternative can prevent the increase; a promise to build one cannot.

**Develop and resolve.** Disorder tests whether the party can expose a crisis before legislation. Emergency asks them to keep a defecting institution operating: payroll, harvest, protection, records. Normalcy tests a refusal at the edge of the writ. The party succeeds when the regime cannot enforce its demands beyond the issuing walls and communities can survive the refusal. Slaying the Prefect changes the officeholder, not the statute book. A neglected convoy can give the regime food control only if it reaches the depot. If coordination breaks early, the party inherit courts and provisioning obligations; missing grain and murdered witnesses remain losses rather than being restored by political victory.

## 6A. Cthulhu — Closing the Coast

**Antagonist options.** **Cthulhu** returns through dreamers, drowned anchors, and conjunction. **The Drowned Emperor**, an original immortal revenant, returns whenever submerged coronation sites recognize his title; exclusion requires breaking succession rather than injuring his body. **The Star Leviathan**, an original migrating outer power, arrives along beacon alignments; counterplay misdirects or permanently occludes those beacons. Translate the six Preparations into the chosen arrival method while retaining the ratchet.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Signs of Return | Identify preparations and face the first possible arrival or prevent it through earned exclusion |
| II — The Cheaper Return | Use earned banishment intervals; discover exclusion and source binding |
| III — Close the Arrival | Complete coastal exclusion or bind the submerged source |

**Generate.** Map three coastal communities, a dreamer refuge, a submerged research site, two terrestrial anchors, and the city's conjunction. Set the next astronomical opportunity and commit what can trigger an Awakening before play. Place the six existing Preparations as expeditions, gatherings, measurements, or rites with named operators.

**Opening pair.** Recover a fishing boat whose crew dream in unison, or escort a tidal surveyor to a newly exposed stair. The boat identifies the chorus organizer; the stair contains geometry useful both to the cult and to eventual exclusion. Tide records, independent dreams, and ruined earlier anchor works support the same conclusion.

**Make the ratchet executable.** Keep completed Preparations as irreversible knowledge and infrastructure history. Record `six minus completed preparations` as the remaining preparation burden. At a committed conjunction, surviving cult operators can temporarily supply the missing preparations through named resources; list those resources before the event. At zero, the permanent intersection needs no new calling. Exclusion works change whether an arrival route is usable, not whether an old Preparation happened. Thus banishment never reverses the ratchet, while anchor inversion can still make future arrival impossible.

**Three acts:** discover contact and first-arrival conditions → exploit a banishment interval and test protection → execute either permanent remedy. Source binding remains possible before a manifestation; arrival is never mandatory.

**Player procedure.** Apply the [approved player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6a-cthulhu--dream-contact-and-safe-recovery), including the benefit/price record, monster objectives, independent clue routes, and actual remedy completion events. Seed at least one recurring choice in the opening expedition and make it consequential by the third. Provide both remedies for every antagonist variant; adapt its dependency and rite rather than simply changing the name.

**Success A — Coastal exclusion:** complete permanent exclusion works at every committed arrival site and discharge the active intersection. **Success B — Source binding:** reach the submerged source and complete its binding through one declared conjunction interval, preventing projection through otherwise intact arrival sites. Neither requires the other. Both end this incursion; killing the manifestation alone does not.

**Failure/neglect:** feasible preparations and conjunctions proceed. Enemy victory requires an actually sustained permanent intersection; a completed source binding or exclusion prevents it even if every preparation is in history. A banished body cannot reappear before the saved re-entry condition. Early success leaves calling knowledge, saved dreamers, and coastal recovery. Lost communities remain in the Toll.


## 6B. Nyarlathotep — Whose Authority?

**Antagonist options.** **Nyarlathotep** uses fallible masks to acquire real authority. A **conspiracy of oracle-priests** uses mandates to enthrone its chosen ruler. A **Tzeentch-inspired schemer** transfers magical authority into a prepared vessel. For each, specify the three actual mandates, voluntary acceptance, and a concrete mask-binding substitute suited to its nature.

**Generate.** Create three masks, two genuinely opposed institutions, one incarnation site, and three indispensable local mandates: custody of a relic, authority over the site, and an invitation from its recognised keeper. Name the issuer, scope, beneficiaries, evidence, expiry or revocation procedure, and means of impersonation for each. The incarnation requires all three active mandates and a voluntary acceptance at its site. The masks must actually acquire them.

**Three acts:** useful commissions and discrepancies → trace who authorised what → revoke authority or trap the embodied mask. At least two witnesses/documents independently reveal each essential mandate; a maker's record and former host independently reveal the trapping condition.

**Opening pair.** A charitable patron requests a public accusation against a reformer in return for rescuing a companion. The reformer independently asks the party to protect a witness at a tollhouse. Both address real harm; neither exhausts the party's available choices. Receipts and independent testimony reveal who benefits.

**Player procedure.** Apply the [approved player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6b-nyarlathotep--endorsements-and-divided-interests), including the benefit/price record, monster objectives, independent clue routes, and actual remedy completion events. Seed at least one recurring choice in the opening expedition and make it consequential by the third. Provide both remedies for every antagonist variant; adapt its dependency and rite rather than simply changing the name.

**Success A — Revoke the incarnation:** expose the common beneficiary to the actual issuers, revoke the three mandates, and complete a public severance at the site; its old endorsements can no longer sustain this incarnation. **Success B — Bind the mask:** use the discovered true designation and prepared vessel to trap and banish the local incarnation during its acceptance, even if the institutions still believe its public identities. Collecting the designation alone is insufficient.

**Failure/neglect:** masks can employ other willing agents with access; they cannot treat every refusal as consent. Enemy victory occurs when the three mandates and voluntary acceptance realise the committed seizure of authority before either remedy. Early exposure or banishment leaves betrayed clients, genuine accusations, and offices requiring legitimate successors.


## 6C. Shub-Niggurath — Feeding the Network

**Antagonist options.** **Shub-Niggurath's brood cult** expands supernatural nurseries connected to a fecundity source. A **demon-fed goblin brood sovereign** sends growing war-bands because the warrens cannot support births imposed by its patron. A **spawning hive queen** links feeding caverns through tunnels and produces specialized castes. Captives, voluntary followers, and offspring need separate treatment; ancestry is not proof of service to the breeding power.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — Raids and Relief | Protect communities and find that victories do not stop the next brood |
| II — Read the Census | Learn birth, food, and connection dependencies and test a sustainable intervention |
| III — End the Compulsion | Stop the fecundity source or dismantle the network while resolving the surviving population's needs |

**Generate.** Place three broods, their feeding grounds, two connecting routes or gates, a fecundity source, and neighboring communities. Set mouths, carrying capacity, and a fixed seasonal birth amount for each brood. Count shared capacity once per connected component; do not give every member the whole pool independently.

| Requirement | Deed and capability |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Root a nursery | Secure a viable site and first brood; seasonal births begin there |
| Bind a feeding ground | Gain an actual food source; add its declared capacity |
| Join the broods | Open and maintain a connecting gate; capacity and reinforcements can be shared |
| Mature a carrier | Keep a seeded carrier alive until its established maturation; it can found another nursery |
| Seat the Mother Gate | Connect the source to two nurseries; replacement broods can reach either |

**Opening pair.** Defend a farm from a hungry war-band, or recover a missing livestock counter from a feeding cavern. The raid is an immediate danger worth stopping; the counter holds evidence explaining why killing raiders does not stop reproduction. Give the census through tally-sticks, observable food movements, and a captured quartermaster.

**Three acts:** save communities and discover useful gifts → provision, cure, or isolate dependencies → sever or stabilise reproduction.

**Player procedure.** Apply the [approved player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6c-shub-niggurath--gifts-with-appetites), including the benefit/price record, monster objectives, independent clue routes, and actual remedy completion events. Seed at least one recurring choice in the opening expedition and make it consequential by the third. Provide both remedies for every antagonist variant; adapt its dependency and rite rather than simply changing the name.

**Success A — Sever propagation:** disable the Mother Gate and every independent forced-propagation source so no compulsory expansion remains possible. **Success B — Bound the ecology:** replace each divine reproductive dependency with the demonstrated regulator, supply its bounded population, and complete one full birth/feeding cycle without tribute or compelled propagation. The sources can remain alive under B. Neither requires killing transformed civilians or curing every voluntary gift.

**Failure/neglect:** advance births and consumption on their declared schedule. The enemy prevails when every food refuge is in its sustained feeding territory and forced replacement remains operative; a stabilised ecology cannot satisfy that predicate. Preserve rescued people, accepted transformations, famine losses, and damaged land in the aftermath.


## 6D. Hastur — The Audience Must Choose

**Antagonist options.** **Hastur's theatrical patron** makes the region a stage for Carcosa. A **Slaanesh-inspired muse** offers perfection and recognition in exchange for increasingly consuming performances. **The Saint of the Last Festival**, an original death-cult patron, offers reunion with the dead if the living accept permanent roles in a memorial rite. Replace the work, benefit, roles, and final transformation while keeping participation necessary.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — The Fashion | Discover why people want the work and who profits from presenting it |
| II — The Cast | Trace the costs of accepted roles and recover ways to withdraw or restore identity |
| III — The Last Performance | Break an indispensable participation condition or reclaim the shared rite |

**Generate.** Create a work, a patron, three performance sites, six indispensable roles, a promised benefit for accepting each role, and the finale's required audience. Name which audience groups must attend; use an actual capacity suitable to the chosen venue. Attending accidentally is not consenting to an indispensable role.

| Requirement | Deed and capability |
| :---------- | :------------------ |
| Win a patron | A respected sponsor commissions the work; public venues become available |
| Publish the invitation | An audience knowingly seeks another performance; recruitment becomes self-sustaining |
| Cast the roles | Named people accept the six parts and their stated practices; rehearsals can change identity |
| Desecrate the stage | A community replaces a site's old rite with the work; it becomes connected to Carcosa |
| Complete the performance | Required cast, consenting audience, and prepared stage remain through the final act; the region becomes the stage |

**Opening pair.** A family asks why an acclaimed singer no longer answers to her name; a shrine keeper needs help after a festival replaces the annual remembrance. The singer's letters, discarded drafts, and witnesses to rehearsals reveal the bargain. A separate troupe can offer satisfying art and belonging without the work's price.

**Three acts:** discover desirable privileges → recover personal identities and role terms → dissolve participation or perform restoration.

**Player procedure.** Apply the [approved player mechanics](09_outer_power_player_mechanics.md#6d-hastur--roles-recognition-and-audiences), including the benefit/price record, monster objectives, independent clue routes, and actual remedy completion events. Seed at least one recurring choice in the opening expedition and make it consequential by the third. Provide both remedies for every antagonist variant; adapt its dependency and rite rather than simply changing the name.

**Success A — Dissolve the cast:** enable an indispensable participant to withdraw and complete that role's dissolution rite at its originating venue, so a replacement audition cannot restore it. **Success B — Counter-performance:** recover the original names and rite and complete a willing counter-performance that restores all linked venues and cancels the work's claims, even with its cast still assembled. Neither is merely burning a book or murdering a performer.

**Failure/neglect:** real invitations and bookings spread the work. The enemy wins only when the intact required cast, consenting audience, and prepared venue complete the finale before dissolution/restoration. Lost identities and damaged relationships remain in the Toll; early victory leaves a cultural and civic inheritance.


## 6E. Yog-Sothoth — A Published Geometry

Excavation, broken binding stones, earthquakes, and concentrated magical discharges awaken a creature whose eventual rampage follows food, vibration, and obstacles rather than political revenge. An **original iron colossus** can use the same engine with recovered power cores and reactivated command beacons; its weaknesses and target selection are mechanical. Generate the dossier's actual behavior rather than giving a tarrasque a dragon's court or a dragon mindless feeding priorities.

| Act | Work and transition |
| :-- | :------------------ |
| I — The Mountain Breathes | Discover the sleeper, demonstrate an awakening trigger, and identify why ordinary attacks will fail; ends with an actionable preparation plan |
| II — The Weapons of the Last Hunt | Gain experience, retrieve or create countermeasures, recruit and provision support, and contest waking operations; ends when the party chooses to strike or full awakening overtakes its preparation |
| III — The Killing Expedition | Approach the lair and use earned advantages while the creature is vulnerable, or intercept its committed rampage if it has already risen |

Full awakening changes the conditions of Act III; it does not erase the weapons already gathered or end the campaign automatically. A party who can reach and kill the sleeper earlier earns that victory. An unprepared party can attempt the approach too, provided the fiction makes it accessible; the system must show the known danger rather than block entry behind an act or level label.

### Generate the Sleeper and the Race

Place the lair, three initial threatened settlements, two routes into the lair, a refuge outside the first projected rampage corridor, and the sites of the previous failed hunt. Determine why the creature sleeps, who bound or wounded it, what maintains that condition, and which interests are disturbing it. The mine that must close may feed a town; the cult bringing tribute may believe an awakened dragon will protect them from something worse.

Create four to eight **awakening operations**, each with a named actor or environmental cause, a reachable location, a completion event, and one capability restored. Select at least one operation whose prevention costs the region a useful service. Commit any calendar events, such as an eclipse or a seasonal magma surge, at generation; the referee or server cannot choose an imminent earthquake merely because the party are preparing successfully.

| Awakening operation | Completion | Restored capability or changed opportunity |
| :------------------ | :--------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| Breach the outer seal | Excavators open the final protected seam | The sleeper senses activity beyond the lair; covert entry needs a new approach |
| Feed the buried furnace | Cultists deliver and ignite its actual fuel | A dragon's breath or a colossus's furnace becomes usable; the tarrasque dossier substitutes a specific metabolic trigger |
| Return the stolen strength | A hoard relic, severed plate, or power core reaches the body | One named defense or regenerative process resumes |
| Break the tether | The last functioning restraint at a placed anchor is severed | A limb, wing, or movement mode becomes usable |
| Sound the calling | A committed ritual or environmental event reaches the exposed lair | The creature becomes alert and can respond deliberately or instinctively |
| Open the way out | The exit collapses, is excavated, or is forced with recovered strength | A fully active creature can leave along a mapped route |

The dossier specifies which events are essential to full awakening and which only strengthen it. The baseline requires **alertness, effective locomotion, and an open exit** for a regional rampage; defenses and weapons can recover in any feasible order. Do not require a dragon to recover a tarrasque's abilities or let generic noise substitute for a missing essential event. Successful prevention leaves an operation incomplete. Recreating a broken seal is a new, feasible repair project; it does not erase its first breach from history.

### Keep Two Different Records

**The waking record** lists each committed operation, its completion, and the resulting observable tell: heated wells, a pulse under the mine, claw movement recorded by survey stakes, a voice in the tribute chamber. Use three behavioral descriptions: **Dormant**, **Stirring**, and **Awake**. These describe the creature, not extra campaign acts. Specific recovered capabilities matter more than the label. A dormant creature may already be dangerous to approach; a stirring one may have its breath back but still lack movement.

**The preparation record** lists acquired advantages and exactly what they do. A rumor of a weapon is not possession; possession is not proficiency, ammunition, or the ability to move it into position. Award ordinary experience and treasure through the system's existing adventure rewards. Never make character level itself an awakening trigger or a proof that the creature is now defeatable.

| Preparation | Ancient red dragon example | Tarrasque example |
| :---------- | :------------------------- | :---------------- |
| Expose a vulnerability | Recover the earlier hunters' account of a scar beneath the wing | Locate a damaged plate and the buried hunter still able to identify it |
| Obtain a killing instrument | Reforge the dragon-piercing lance; provide ammunition and a place to brace it | Restore a siege penetrator able to exploit that particular plate |
| Survive the approach | Secure protection from the dossier's heat and breath hazards | Secure cover and equipment against collapse, trampling, and the declared defensive effects |
| Deny the escape or advantage | Anchor wing chains or block a usable launch shaft | Prepare a terrain trap that restrains movement without depending on negotiation |
| Stop recovery | Recover the rite or substance that prevents the dragon's specified restoration | Discover and test the dossier's means of preventing regeneration or finishing the kill |
| Deliver the expedition | Move allies, medicine, weapon crews, and supplies to a defensible staging site | Build an evacuation corridor and get siege equipment into the intended interception ground |

These are **ASH encounter-design requirements**, not assumptions that an ordinary weapon kills every published version of these creatures. Select the creature's rules and define the instrument's mechanical effect, use conditions, limits, and lethal finish before the relevant preparation is offered. If the selected rules provide no permanent killing method, author and seed one for this path before play. Slaying the creature must be a real, discoverable outcome of the campaign the user selected.

Offer at least two acquisition routes for each indispensable capability: recover an old instrument or forge one with rare material; earn protection through an ally or obtain a consumable; learn the finish from a survivor or reconstruct it from a previous battlefield. Neither route may require a particular class. Give crucial countermeasures a test against a shed scale, lesser related creature, old wound, or functioning fragment so the party can distinguish evidence from a seller's claim.

### Opening Situations and Continuing Play

**First lead: the breathing mine.** A foreman hires the party to recover trapped miners from a gallery that rises and falls. Rescue reveals the outer seal and an imminent, stoppable drilling operation. Closing the mine saves the seal but removes a settlement's income; arranging replacement work becomes a standing problem.

**Second lead: the hunters' burial.** A retired quartermaster offers a map to the old expedition's ossuary, where a lance head or siege sight was buried with its crew. That site contains a partial countermeasure and evidence of the failed approach, not the creature itself. A survivor's account, wound diagrams at a shrine, and physical fragments provide independent evidence of the vulnerability. An ordinary caravan contract supplies the third opening choice and remains worthwhile without the hunt.

Expand Act II through competing preparation expeditions: rescue the only available weapon smith; recover ammunition from an occupied fortress; obtain a rare reagent without destroying a protected habitat; convince a ruler to release the siege train; secure winter food for the miners whose work was stopped. Completion changes the preparation record and provides experience. Each expedition consumes actual travel and work time, during which only already enabled waking operations can finish. Do not advance the sleeper automatically after every recovered weapon.

The core strategic decision is **when to strike**. Show the known capabilities already restored, the advantages ready to deploy, the unfinished preparations, and any established operation nearing completion. Hidden operations remain discoverable; they are not invented to punish cautious preparation. No readiness score replaces the players' assessment.

### The Hunt and Its Endings

Build the final encounter from the dossier's fixed base threat plus the capabilities actually restored, then apply acquired countermeasures. The creature does not silently gain armor because the party leveled up. Preparations must change concrete actions: a lance can penetrate the declared defense, a binding can deny flight for its stated duration, and a proven killing method can finish an otherwise regenerating creature. Supplies, crews, and terrain are part of delivering those actions.

Before full awakening, the approach can reach a vulnerable body while servants, environmental hazards, and the creature's recovered reflexes interfere. After awakening, the dragon chooses from its committed interests and known targets; the tarrasque follows its established attraction and movement rules. Resolve movement along the saved map, giving settlements time according to actual distance and transport. Evacuations save named people but cost supplies and leave property exposed.

**Party victory:** the creature is slain using a valid finishing method, or the party deliberately chooses an alternative such as permanent rebinding, banishment, or a dragon's enforceable settlement. Rebinding has stated maintenance and failure conditions; it is not called a kill. Negotiating with a dragon may be possible; bargaining with a mindless tarrasque is not a default route. No alternative removes the promised possibility of the hunt.

**Catastrophe victory:** the awakened creature destroys or takes the dossier's committed regional objectives and the remaining communities can no longer sustain resistance. The baseline names the three initial settlements and the supply system joining them. Reaching Awake makes this possible, not instantaneous. Play can continue as evacuation, reconstruction, or a second hunt in the ruined region.

**If ignored:** enabled operations and committed natural events proceed using their resources and dates. If no operation can finish, the creature remains asleep; another adventure does not wake it by fiat. **If slain early:** heirs, hoard claimants, displaced servants, dangerous remains, and the people who financed the weapon create the inheritance. Keep the achievement intact. **The Toll:** settlements lost, crews killed, habitat sacrificed for materials, binding oaths accepted, and those left outside an evacuation remain named facts after victory.

## 13. The Stolen Dawn — Three Remedies for a Sustained Catastrophe

Use the [full path dossier](../adventure_paths/04_the_stolen_dawn.md). Choose the Pale Regent and its unique renewal mount or the Mourning Queen and her irreplaceable casting instrument. Commit personal vulnerabilities, renewal schedule, and permanent-disable conditions before placing clues.

**Generate.** Create Hearthhaven and nearby communities, the adjoining outer wilderness, and buried Veyr. Use exactly two Cursed Scroll profiles: Isles of Andrik for Acts I and II, Morzomotha & Karst Deeps for Act III. Town and wilderness are distinct territories on one persistent surface region. Generate supplies, physical routes, two underground approaches, the renewal site, Dawn Engine machinery, and independent evidence for all three remedies.

**Three acts.** Keep the Hearths (town and nearby, levels 1–3); Cross the White Country (wilderness, 4–7); Bring Back the Morning (underground, 8–10). Budget sites and rewards for each normal branch, while allowing earned victory before the final act.

**Opening pair.** Rescue fuel cutters at the eastern shelter or recover the sky-watch keeper's observation plates. An ordinary fishing inheritance dispute supplies the third lead. Reveal credible evidence for at least two remedies in Act I and all three before commitment to a final expedition.

**Develop and resolve.** The party wins by defeating the sovereign, permanently disabling its indispensable renewal means, **or** commissioning the Dawn Engine through one complete night with its self-sustaining regulator intact. These are separately sufficient outcomes. Temporary interruption does not equal permanent cessation. Neither nonlethal route secretly requires the sovereign's death. Concrete retaliation can be diverted or defeated without changing the success rule.

**Neglect and inheritance.** Renewals follow the committed schedule; provisions are consumed on elapsed days; operations require resources and routes. Failure is the loss or submission of all authored communities with no supplied independent refuge, while renewal remains possible and no player success has occurred. Early success ends winter immediately; subsequent expeditions concern reconstruction, salvage, or stewardship. Keep the Toll and prevent duplicate victory rewards. Runtime eligibility remains unimplemented.

## Review a Populated Engine Before Play

For each generated campaign, verify the following with its own record:

- The two opening path leads reach distinct, feasible situations; an ordinary lead remains useful on its own.
- The engine has three acts and its selected antagonist is one of at least two developed options; the dossier changes motives, dependencies, and counterplay as well as appearance.
- Every indispensable conclusion has independent evidence routes, and every finale has a practical access route suitable for the party.
- Every active enemy operation names an actor, resources, route, completion trigger, and changed capability.
- At least one ordinary tactical victory can leave the strategic problem intact, but the evidence also supports a move that really resolves it.
- A failed or ignored situation produces the recorded consequence and a plausible next opportunity; it does not force a predetermined act transition.
- Repeated discoveries and deeds do not duplicate rewards or consequences. A return visit preserves people, ownership, damage, and knowledge.
- The chosen ending can be evaluated from saved facts. Secret selection changes disclosure, not those facts or the rules after a choice.
- At least two player success conditions are independently sufficient and executable; completing either can win while the other remains false. Multiple prerequisites to one compulsory ending do not qualify. Audit older dossiers against this requirement without claiming they already comply.

These are documentation and future generation acceptance criteria. Runtime support must be verified independently; having an entry here does not make an engine eligible for the application's path selector.
