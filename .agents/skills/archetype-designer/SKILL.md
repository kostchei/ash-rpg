# Archetype Designer: The 3-Origin Question Engine

This skill codifies the design principles behind evocative, player-driven character archetypes (as seen in *Stonetop*, *Dungeon World*, and *ASH*). Instead of treating a character class merely as a bundle of combat mechanics, an **Archetype** is a mythic and social pillar embedded directly into a community and its surrounding wilderness.

---

## 1. Anatomy of an Archetype

Every archetype consists of three core components:

```
[ Archetype Identity ]
  ├── Core Mythic & Social Role in the Community
  ├── Primary Tension (Blessing vs. Burden, Duty vs. Desire)
  └── 3 Distinct Origins (The Paths to the Playbook)
        ├── Origin A (e.g., Traditional / Sanctioned)
        ├── Origin B (e.g., Outcast / Grimy / Renegade)
        └── Origin C (e.g., Mystic / Touched / Accidental)
```

Each Origin provides **3 to 6 targeted, leading questions** that the player answers during character creation.

---

## 2. The 5 Narrative Levers of Archetype Questions

Every question set must pull at least 3 of these 5 levers to ensure the character instantly generates adventure hooks, interpersonal drama, and world truths:

| Lever | Purpose | Example Stonetop / ASH Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Community Anchor** | Roots the character in the village, hearth, or crew. Prevents the "lone wolf who cares about nothing" syndrome. | *"What ties you to the village? Do you live in town, on the outskirts, or in the Wood?"* |
| **2. Source of Power / Authority** | Explains *how* and *why* they possess their unique prowess or station without lengthy prologue. | *"What is the symbol of your authority (hammer, shield, or helm)? Where did the Chronicle say it came from?"* |
| **3. Social Friction & Shadows** | Seeds immediate enemies, whispered rumors, past blood, or debts. | *"What lies get whispered behind your back? Who in town resents what you represent?"* |
| **4. Lore Injection (Player Worldbuilding)** | Forces the player to author a piece of the cosmos, ancient history, or regional geography. | *"What do other scholars claim the Stone really is? What do you think instead?"* |
| **5. Asymmetric Relationships** | Establishes contrasting NPC dynamics across the settlement. | *"Who in town does your companion adore? Who in town does your companion terrify?"* |

---

## 3. The Rule of Three Origins

Never offer just one flavor of a class. The three origins should contrast sharply in tone, status, and origins:

1. **The Sanctioned / Traditional Path:**
   * High status, legitimate training, accepted by the elders or institutions.
   * *Examples:* The Blessed: *Initiate*; The Heavy: *Sheriff*; The Judge: *Legacy*; The Marshal: *Scion*.
2. **The Scarred / Outlaw / Veteran Path:**
   * High friction, hard-bitten, blood on their hands, questionable legitimacy, running from or redeeming their past.
   * *Examples:* The Fox: *A Life of Crime*; The Heavy: *Blood-soaked Past*; The Marshal: *Penitent*; The Seeker: *Witch Hunter*.
3. **The Touched / Primal / Unorthodox Path:**
   * Mysterious calling, direct communion with nature or gods, prodigal wanderer, fateful anomaly.
   * *Examples:* The Blessed: *Vessel*; The Heavy: *Storm-marked*; The Lightbearer: *Soul on Fire*; The Judge: *Prophet*.

---

## 4. Question Crafting Rules

When writing questions for an origin:

1. **Ask "Loaded" Questions:** Never ask *"Do people like you?"* Instead ask: *"Who in town resents you, or at least what you represent?"* Assume dramatic tension already exists.
2. **Offer Bracketed Tropes as Inspiration:** Use examples in parentheses to prompt the player's imagination without restricting them: *"What was the symbol of your authority (hammer, shield, or helm)?"*
3. **Use the "Rule of Contrast":** Pair positive ties with dangerous ones: *"Which neighbor is always glad to see you? Which one wants you dead?"*
4. **Anchor to Specific Setting Entities:** Intertwine questions with named gods, factions, landmarks, or lost peoples (e.g., Danu, Helior, Aratis, The Makers, Forest Folk, Things Below).

---

## 5. Workflow: Adapting Characters or Media into Archetypes

1. **Identify the Core Social Archetype:** What niche does the character fill in their warband, village, or world? (e.g., The Scheming Skald, The Cursed Berserker, The Horizon Chaser).
2. **Formulate the Triad of Origins:** What are the three distinct backstories that lead to this exact same archetype?
3. **Draft 4–6 Questions per Origin:** Ensure all 5 Narrative Levers are hit across the questions.
4. **Stress-Test:** Ask: *If a player answers these questions in 5 minutes, can the GM immediately frame a provocative opening scene?* If yes, the archetype is complete.
